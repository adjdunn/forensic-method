# 3. Footnote changes

Use the same conversation as prompts 1 and 2; the two annual reports are already attached. (Running it alone: attach two consecutive annual reports.) Then paste this prompt.

---

Compare the two attached annual reports and find what the company changed in its disclosures.

**Read these sections in both years, in this order:**
1. Risk factors
2. Legal proceedings and contingencies
3. The auditor's report
4. Critical accounting estimates
5. Revenue recognition
6. The receivables, inventory and reserve notes
7. Depreciation, useful lives and capitalized costs
8. Related parties
9. Definitions of adjusted (non-GAAP) measures

**Ignore** changes that only update dates or numbering, or that follow from an acquisition or new accounting standard the filing names.

**For each change that matters, record:**
- the sentence before and after, quoted exactly, with pages;
- why it might matter;
- the innocent reading.

A dropped sentence can matter as much as a new one.

**Also build one side-by-side table**, whether or not anything changed. For both years, list every statement of useful lives, holding periods, residual or salvage values, and changes in accounting estimates, wherever it appears (including the business description and MD&A). A statement that stays the same while the numbers around it move is worth seeing.

**Rules:**
- Use only the attached documents. No outside knowledge of this company.
- Quote exactly, with the document and page. Don't paraphrase a sentence you are using as evidence.
- For every change, give the innocent explanation first.
- These are things to check, not findings. Don't use words like fraud, manipulation or red flag.

**Reply in one to two pages:**
1. A table of the changes that matter, at most eight: Section | Before | After | Pages | Why it might matter | Innocent reading.
2. The side-by-side table of lives, holding periods, residual values and estimate changes.
