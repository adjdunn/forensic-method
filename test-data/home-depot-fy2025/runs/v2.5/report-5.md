**The Home Depot, Inc.: fiscal years ended 2 February 2025 (53 weeks) and 1 February 2026 (52 weeks).**
**Documents:** both annual reports (pages are the [p. N] markers), the statements workbook and the four earlier replies. Sizes are shares of the latest year's pre-tax income, $18,602M.

**Top five items to investigate**

**1. Store costs and the legal benefit.** Size: Primary operating income fell from $21,313M to $20,574M, 4.0%, an effect on reported income; the legal benefit is not quantified.
- **Shown:** Primary SG&A rose 3.8% while Primary sales fell 0.7% (FY2026 p. 55). Last year's legal benefit is now called "non-recurring" (p. 34), though last year's report said "lower legal-related benefits" (FY2025 p. 34), implying one the year before too.
- **Reading:** Most likely the business weakening: operating margin has fallen every year since FY Jan-23 (15.3% to 12.7%, whole company). A large legal benefit would mean fiscal 2024 was flattered instead.
- **Next:** ask for the legal benefit amount; read the Primary segment table in the next quarterly reports, where both years have 13 weeks.

**2. Goodwill and intangibles from SRS and GMS.** Size: $13,780M of goodwill, 74.1%, a possible future loss and not an income effect; $607M of amortization left out of adjusted EPS, 3.3%, a gap between adjusted and reported figures.
- **Shown:** in their first numerical test the SRS units' fair value "exceeded" carrying amount, against "substantially exceeded" for the stores (FY2026 p. 60). Their margin fell from 3.3% to 2.5%, on operating income of $316M (p. 55).
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary: units bought within two years sit near fair value, and the goodwill rise matches the acquisitions. A further fall in margin before amortization would point to the business weakening.
- **Next:** the cushion for each SRS unit at the next annual test; the final GMS allocation.

**3. Borrowings and commercial paper.** Size: long-term debt plus commercial paper rose $2,389M, 12.8%, a balance-sheet movement; no effect on income is quantified.
- **Shown:** buybacks stay paused "as we seek to reduce our outstanding debt" (FY2026 p. 35), yet commercial paper ended at $4,464M against $316M. The average-borrowings table was dropped (FY2025 p. 61), and ROIC leaves commercial paper out (24.7% with it, 25.7% reported).
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents: the aim holds for long-term debt only (down $1,759M). Fiscal 2026 note maturities repaid without new commercial paper would point to ordinary.
- **Next:** commercial paper at each quarter end against the $5.8B peak (FY2026 p. 61).

**4. Inventory and its estimates.** Size: $1,498M of inventory built, 8.1%, a balance-sheet movement; the company's stated shrink sensitivity is $79M, 0.4%, for a 10% change in the rate.
- **Shown:** inventory rose 10.1% against cost of sales up 3.4%, explained only as "higher average inventory levels" (FY2026 p. 32). The auditor's shrink-estimate matter gave way to one on audit evidence over inventory systems (pp. 41-42) as the shrink sensitivity fell from $95M (p. 39 of each report).
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents; possibly ordinary, as days inventory (85.6) is below the Jan-23 level (86.6). Markdowns, shrink rising again or a control deficiency over inventory systems would change it.
- **Next:** gross margin and inventory in the next quarterly reports.

**5. Receivables and trade credit.** Size: 1% of customer receivables is $26M, 0.1%, a possible future loss; the allowance is not quantified.
- **Shown:** receivables rose 47.3% and then 14.2%, far ahead of sales; receivables acquired with SRS and GMS exceed both increases, so that explanation holds. The allowance is only "not material" (FY2026 p. 48) while customers "increasingly use trade credit" (p. 24).
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. An allowance that stops being "not material" would point to the business weakening.
- **Next:** the 2026 quarterly reports (not attached), where receivables again outgrew sales.

**Also noted:** payables down 3.7% with supplier-finance invoices down 14.5%; self-insurance liability up while its tax asset fell; lease renewals outside the lease liability; card-programme profit sharing inside net sales, amount not given; a tax valuation allowance cut from 67 to 4 in FY Feb-25; comparable-sales definition reworded.

**Checked and explained:** operating cash flow down 17.6% (tax timing and working capital account for it); the goodwill rise (matches the acquisitions); sales per retail square foot dropped (about flat on 52 weeks, rough); the Revisions sheet (re-labelling only); stated asset lives and supplier terms (still fit).

**Not examined:** related parties, Schedule II, store square footage, the allowance and legal-benefit amounts, average commercial paper, the 2026 quarterly reports.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
