**Operating margin down again, 13.5% to 12.7% (whole company)**
- **The company says:** SG&A rose on "higher payroll and related costs" and a "non-recurring legal-related benefit" the year before (FY Feb-26 report, p. 34). Last year: different in part; payroll, plus "lower legal-related benefits" (FY Feb-25 report, p. 34).
- **Test:** Primary segment SG&A (stores and online, before SRS and GMS) rose 3.8% to 28,885 while its sales fell 0.7% (52 weeks against 53); neither report sizes payroll or the legal benefit, so the explanation partly holds.
- **Reading:** most likely the business weakening: Primary SG&A is up 8.6% in two years on flat sales, taking its operating margin from 14.2% to 13.5%. A large legal benefit in FY Feb-25 would mean that year was flattered instead; both reports cite one.
- **Read next:** Note 2, Primary segment table (FY Feb-26 report, p. 55); Note 12 (p. 73) gives no legal amounts.

**Inventory up 10.1%, payables down 3.7% in FY Feb-26 (whole company)**
- **The company says:** turnover fell on "higher average inventory levels" (FY Feb-26 report, p. 32) and cash flow on "the timing of vendor payments" (p. 37), with no cause for either. Last year: "lower average inventory levels" (FY Feb-25 report, p. 32); the same vendor-payments phrase (p. 38).
- **Test:** inventories rose 2,366, of which GMS brought 568 (FY Feb-26 report, p. 74) and 1,498 was built; payables fell 447 although GMS brought 380. The turnover figures reproduce (4.36 and 4.67 times on quarter-end averages), so the explanation partly holds: it describes the movement without explaining it.
- **Reading:** can't tell from these documents; possibly ordinary, as days inventory (85.6) is below the Jan-23 level (86.6) and tariffs raised costs (p. 32). Markdowns, or a fall in Primary gross margin (it rose from 33.9% to 34.4%), would point to the business weakening.
- **Read next:** Note 1, supplier finance table (p. 51), where invoices confirmed fell 14.5%; the critical audit matter on merchandise inventories (pp. 41-42).

**Receivables up 47.3%, then 14.2% (whole company)**
- **The company says:** nothing in the FY Feb-26 report on why customer receivables rose from 1,896 to 2,588 (p. 48). Last year: "primarily as a result of the SRS acquisition" (FY Feb-25 report, p. 48).
- **Test:** receivables rose 1,575 in FY Feb-25 against 1,831 acquired with SRS, and 694 in FY Feb-26 against 899 acquired with GMS (p. 74 of each report), so the acquisition explanation holds for both years. Q2 Aug-26 can't be tested from these documents.
- **Reading:** most likely ordinary: the acquired distributors brought more receivables than the whole increase. An allowance that stops being "not material" (FY Feb-26 report, p. 48), with customers said to "increasingly use trade credit" (p. 24), would point to the business weakening.
- **Read next:** Note 1, receivables table (p. 48), and the 2026 quarterly reports, which are not attached.

**Other flags**
- **Cash flow fell more than earnings (FY Feb-26):** "timing of vendor payments and increased inventories", plus a tax payment deferred from the year before (FY Feb-26 report, p. 37). Held: those three cash flow lines swung by 3,838 against a 3,485 fall in operating cash flow, and income taxes paid rose from 3,653 to 4,848 (p. 65).
- **Goodwill and intangibles:** "preliminary determination of goodwill related to the GMS acquisition", after SRS the year before (FY Feb-26 report, p. 60). Held: goodwill rose 13,889 in two years against 13,891 of acquisitions (SRS 11,003 as finalised, 11,006 as first reported); the acquired businesses earned 316 of operating income on 12,717 of sales (p. 55).

**Adjusted earnings:** diluted EPS was 14.91 reported and 15.24 adjusted in FY Feb-25 (gap 0.33, 2.2%), then 14.23 and 14.69 in FY Feb-26 (gap 0.46, 3.2%); the only adjustment in both years is acquired intangible amortization (425, then 607, before tax), which recurs every year.
Neither release uses "non-recurring", "one-time" or "unusual". Left in both measures: the 53rd week (about $0.30 of FY Feb-25 EPS; without it adjusted EPS fell 1.7%, not 3.6%) and the legal benefit that the annual report, not the release, calls "non-recurring".

**Reserve releases:** neither report describes a reserve released through a change in estimate, and no claims or payments are disclosed to test one. Two balances fell: the deferred tax valuation allowance, from 67 to 4 in FY Feb-25, called "not material" (FY Feb-25 report, p. 66) though equal to 0.3 points of that year's tax rate if it went through income; and the shrink reserve, whose 10% sensitivity fell from $95 million to $79 million (p. 39 of each report).
