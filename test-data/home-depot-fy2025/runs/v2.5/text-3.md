**1. Acquired distribution businesses: thin test wording, thin earnings** (FY2026 report, Notes 2, 4 and 13; pp. 55, 60, 75)
- **The note says:** the SRS units' fair value "exceeded its respective carrying amount, including goodwill"; the store units' "substantially exceeded" (p. 60).
- **Test:** Other (SRS and GMS) earned $316M of operating income on $12,717M of sales (2.5%, against 3.3%), which is 1.4% of the $23,109M paid, and it holds $13,780M of goodwill, 62% of the total. The cushion is not given, so that part cannot be tested.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary: units bought within two years sit near fair value, and $398M of intangible amortization comes before that $316M. A further fall in margin before amortization (5.6%, against 6.7% in the part-year) would point to the business weakening.
- **Read next:** the cushion for each SRS unit at the next annual test, and the final GMS allocation (still preliminary, and outside the control assessment; pp. 75, 77).

**2. Growth is acquired; the store business shrank** (FY2026 report, Note 2, p. 55; Item 7, p. 33)
- **The note says:** net sales "also increased due to sales from new stores and the impact of a positive comparable sales environment" (p. 33).
- **Test:** Whole-company net sales rose 3.2%, but Other added $6,311M, so Primary segment sales fell 0.7% and Primary operating income fell 3.5%. The claim holds only without the extra week in fiscal 2024 (about $2.5B): Primary sales then rose about 0.9%.
- **Reading:** Most likely the business weakening slightly: Primary SG&A rose 3.8% on lower sales, and comparable transactions fell for a third year. Primary operating income rising on a clean 52-week comparison would point to ordinary.
- **Read next:** the Primary segment table in the next quarterly reports, where both years have 13 weeks.

**3. ROIC leaves out commercial paper** (FY2026 report, Item 7, p. 35)
- **The note says:** capital is "long-term debt (including current installments) and equity" (p. 35).
- **Test:** 25.7% and 31.3% reproduce as defined. Counting commercial paper, which rose $4,148M while long-term debt fell $1,759M, gives 24.7% and 31.2%: a fall of 6.5 points, not 5.6.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary: the definition matches last year's (FY2025 report, p. 35) and the stated causes of the fall hold. More notes refinanced with commercial paper would point to the measure being stretched.
- **Read next:** commercial paper at each quarter end against the $5.8B peak (p. 61).
