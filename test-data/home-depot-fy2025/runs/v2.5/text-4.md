**1. Auditor's report: two critical audit matters replaced by one**
- **Before:** "Estimation of store shrink" and "Fair values of customer relationships intangible assets" (FY2025 report, pp. 42 and 41).
- **After:** both dropped; the one matter is "Sufficiency of audit evidence over certain merchandise inventories", because of "the highly automated nature of the process" (FY2026 report, pp. 41 and 42).
- **Why it matters:** the auditor's hardest judgment moved from a shrink estimate (10% sensitivity down from $95M to $79M) to whether the systems recording $25,817M of inventory, up 10.1% for the whole company, can be evidenced at all.
- **Reading:** Can't tell: most likely the emphasis followed the finance-system migration both reports describe, and the risk factors now put shrink in the past ("we saw heightened shrink", FY2026 p. 19; "we have seen heightened shrink", FY2025 p. 19). A control deficiency over inventory systems, or shrink rising again, would point to the reporting being stretched.

**2. Management discussion: sales per retail square foot dropped**
- **Before:** "Sales per retail square foot (2) (4) | $599.92 | $604.55 | $627.17" (FY2025 report, p. 33).
- **After:** dropped from the table and the summary (FY2026 report, pp. 32 and 33).
- **Why it matters:** the measure had fallen 3.6% and then 0.8%; rebuilt from transactions, ticket and store count it is about $591 for fiscal 2025 (rough; the method gives $599 against the $599.92 reported), a third fall of about 1.3%, though about flat on 52 weeks.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary: on 52 weeks the measure was about flat, so dropping it hid no fall, and transactions and ticket are still given. A further fall in store transactions, alongside the risk factors' new "historically low levels of housing turnover" (FY2026 p. 24), would point to the business weakening and a measure removed because it showed it.
