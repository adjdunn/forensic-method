The Home Depot, Inc.: what changed between the two annual
reports
Form 10-K for fiscal 2025 (year ended February 1, 2026) against fiscal 2024 (year ended February 2, 2025), with the two
fourth-quarter results releases.
1. Full write-ups
Critical accounting estimates and auditor's report: the shrink estimate fell, and the audit matter on
it was dropped

Before: a 10% higher shrink rate “would have increased cost of sales by approximately $95 million” (FY2024 10-K, p.
39); the auditor listed “Estimation of store shrink” as a critical audit matter (p. 42).

After: “approximately $79 million” (FY2025 10-K, p. 39); the shrink audit matter is dropped (pp. 41–42).

Why it matters: the sensitivity fell 17% while Primary segment cost of sales fell 1.5%. This implies a shrink estimate
roughly $160m lower, and the gross margin discussion credits “lower shrink” (p. 34). Size: about 0.9% of pre-tax
income. This is rough, because the sensitivity is the only figure given.

Reading: most likely ordinary, since the risk factor moved from “have seen heightened shrink” (p. 19) to “saw” (p.
19) and the estimate follows “recent shrink results”. Later counts showing higher losses, or the sensitivity rising
again, would point to an estimate set too low.
Debt note and adjusted measures: average commercial paper no longer disclosed

Before: a table gave “Average daily short-term borrowings” of $1,343m and a maximum of $15,317m (FY2024 10-K,
p. 61).

After: table dropped; only “the maximum amount outstanding during that period was $5.8 billion” (FY2025 10-K, p.
61).

Why it matters: this is the figure needed to adjust the return on invested capital written up in the earlier
assessment, in the year commercial paper rose from $316m to $4,464m. With last year's daily average, fiscal 2024
ROIC would be 30.5% against the 31.3% reported. For fiscal 2025 only the begin-and-end average can be used
(24.7% against 25.7%). Size: no income effect; $4,464m is a balance.

Reading: most likely ordinary pruning, as the table mainly described the SRS bridge financing. It does remove a
figure just as it became relevant, and the ROIC explanation now leads with “higher average equity due to our
ongoing pause in share repurchases” (p. 32) instead of debt (p. 32 last year). Quarterly filings that give the average
would settle it.
Auditor's report: a new critical audit matter on inventory evidence

Before: two matters, “Fair values of customer relationships intangible assets” and “Estimation of store shrink”
(FY2024 10-K, pp. 41–42).

After: one matter, “Sufficiency of audit evidence over certain merchandise inventories”, citing the “highly
automated nature of the process” across “multiple information technology (IT) systems” (FY2025 10-K, pp. 41–42).

Why it matters: it covers the retail-method majority of $25.8bn of inventory, and the controls section in both years
says finance systems are being migrated (p. 76; p. 77). No matter was raised on the $1.5bn of GMS customer
relationships. Size: not quantified; inventory is a balance equal to 139% of pre-tax income.

Reading: most likely ordinary, an auditor flagging reliance on systems during a migration, with clean opinions on the
statements and on controls. A control deficiency disclosed in a later filing would point the other way.
2. Other changes

Sales per retail square foot (“$599.92”, FY2024 pp. 32–33) is dropped from the FY2025 summary and table (pp.
32–33). A rough rebuild gives about $594 for fiscal 2025, down 1.0%, though last year had a 53rd week.

Inventory cost method: “primarily the first-in, first-out method” (FY2024 p. 49) became “primarily the moving
average cost and first-in, first-out methods” (FY2025 p. 49). It is not listed as an accounting change and no effect is
given.

Goodwill: the SRS units were tested “using a qualitative approach” (FY2024 p. 60) and are now tested “using a
quantitative approach”, with fair value that “exceeded” carrying amount (FY2025 p. 60). The Primary units still read
“substantially exceeded”.
