**Hertz Global Holdings, Inc.: FY2013 against FY2012, with the fourth quarters.**
Documents: the FY2013 and FY2012 annual reports (key sections), the statements spreadsheet, and the two numbers-track replies (which also used the fourth-quarter releases).

**Top five items to investigate** (each size is a share of FY2013 pre-tax income of $663.1M, from the income statement in the statements spreadsheet)

**1. Adjusted pre-tax income** (size: exclusions of $490.1M, 73.9%)
- **Shown:** The measure leaves out "certain one-time charges and non-operational items" (FY2013 p. 131), yet restructuring was excluded every year ($645.4M since 2007, p. 140) and repeating add-backs are 75% of the gap. "non-cash debt charges" (FY2012 p. 50) also became "debt-related charges" (FY2013 p. 50).
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting stretched: recurring costs are treated as outside operations. An end to restructuring after the Dollar Thrifty integration would change this.
- **Next:** Whether further relocation spend "in the range of $40 million to $45 million" (FY2013 p. 65) is excluded too.

**2. Fleet depreciation and residual values** (size: a charge about $152m lower, 22.9%, of which stated rate cuts are $44.2M, 6.7%)
- **Shown:** Note 8 calls its rate cuts "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 p. 126), yet U.S. disposals swung from a $100.6M gain to a $48.2M loss and the same report cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (FY2013 p. 54). The longest period for cars went from "4 to 28 months" (FY2012 p. 81) to "4 to 36 months" (FY2013 p. 91), while the stated U.S. average stayed at eighteen months (p. 11 of both reports).
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting stretched: 2013 depreciation looks low for what cars fetched. Losses confined to the acquired Dollar Thrifty cars would point to ordinary.
- **Next:** Quarterly rate changes and disposal results in the 2013 10-Qs; U.S. fleet age.

**3. Receivables and the allowance** (size: 105.8 of the fall not explained, 16.0%)
- **Shown:** Receivables fell 19.5% while revenue rose 19.4%; fleet-sale timing explains 71% of the fall. Write-offs grew 37.5% against an allowance up 5.1%, and Schedule II charges $41.9M (p. 158) where the cash flow statement shows $45.9M (p. 80).
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary timing, possibly a thin allowance. A second year of write-offs outrunning the allowance would point to the reporting stretched.
- **Next:** Receivables split into fleet, trade and other; the two bad-debt figures reconciled.

**4. Tax valuation allowance** (size: $53.0M, 8.0%)
- **Shown:** The tax note credits "a decrease in the valuation allowance" (FY2013 p. 128) for holding the tax charge down, but Schedule II shows the allowance up $53.0M to $279.4M (p. 158).
- **Reading:** Most likely loose wording: the rate table on the same page shows the allowance still adding to the tax rate, by less than in 2012. A real fall in the allowance for particular countries would make the sentence right.
- **Next:** The allowance by country; which losses stopped being reserved.

**5. Error corrections and controls** (size: $46.3M, 7.0%)
- **Shown:** "out of period errors totaling $46.3 million" (FY2013 p. 83) were spread back over 2010 to 2013, yet controls are called "effective as of December 31, 2013" (FY2013 p. 159). A new general ledger went in one quarter before the errors surfaced.
- **Reading:** Possibly controls weaker than reported. The small size against annual income would point to ordinary clean-up.
- **Next:** The next 10-Q's controls section; how the errors were found.

**Also noted:** a $40.0M impairment on cars subleased to the buyer of Advantage, with a new risk factor on further losses (6.0%; pp. 147, 24); a concession-fee judgment that could exceed $40.0 million (6.0%; p. 137). Not quantified: the pricing measure redefined to count fuel and fee revenue, $46.33 a day against $39.07 for the U.S. in 2012 (p. 49); debt due within a year cut from 40.2% to 12.6% of the total by relabelling revolving borrowings (p. 67); a financing requirement unknowingly missed at year end (p. 108); three different ranges for intangible lives (pp. 91, 93, 99).

**Checked and explained:** the fourth-quarter gross margin fall (22.1% to 18.5%) fits weaker pricing and extra fleet; 66% of the rise in prepaid and other assets is the China Auto Rental notes; the public liability reserve is 3.2% of revenue against 3.3% of 2012 pro forma revenue.

**Not examined:** U.S. fleet cost and age, a split of receivables, claims paid against the public liability reserve, and the quarterly reports: none is in the documents.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
