**Flag 1: Receivables fell 19.8% while revenue rose 19.4% (FY2013)**
- **The company says:** total assets rose, "partly offset by a decrease in fleet receivables within our U.S. car rental segment, primarily related to the timing of purchases and sales of revenue earning equipment" (FY2013 annual report, p. 133). Same explanation last year: no; receivables rose in FY2012 without comment.
- **The note adds:** Receivables "represent credit extended to manufacturers and customers that satisfy defined credit criteria" (FY2013 annual report, p. 90); there is no breakdown. Schedule II: allowance 29.3 to 30.8, write-offs 40.3 against 29.3. The cash flow statement's provision (45.9) is 4.0 above the schedule's charge (41.9).
- **Test:** On the revised basis receivables fell 367.1. Unpaid fleet sales fell 261.3 (618.6 to 357.3), 71% of it. The remaining 1,155.3 still fell 8.4% while revenue rose 19.4%. Partly holds.
- **Ordinary reading:** Fewer cars were sold just before year-end, so manufacturers and auction buyers owed less, and customers paid faster.
- **If the business is weakening:** Fourth-quarter fleet disposal proceeds fell 30.2% (1,594.9 against 2,286.2, FY2013 release Table 7) while the company was "carrying extra fleet" (FY2013 release, p. 2). Low receivables would then mean cars not sold on schedule.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Write-offs up 37.5% and the Brazil allowance correction suggest earlier balances were carried too high; part of the fall could be clean-up that belongs to earlier periods.
- **Read next:** a receivables breakdown (vehicle, trade, other) and the liquidity discussion; neither is in the attached pages.

**Flag 2: Prior years changed in the FY2013 annual report**
- **The company says:** "we identified certain out of period errors totaling $46.3 million" (FY2013 annual report, p. 83), 34.7 of it from 2012 and earlier. Same explanation last year: no; the FY2012 report corrected only the parent-company schedule.
- **The note adds:** Vendor incentives "which had been accounted for as a reduction of marketing expenses instead of reducing the cost of revenue earning equipment" (FY2013 annual report, p. 83), plus Brazil assets and allowances and other items. Basis: the spreadsheet and step 1 are as first reported; the FY2013 report and release show 2011 and 2012 as revised.
- **Test:** The stated pieces sum to 6.8 (2010 and earlier), 18.7 (2011) and 9.2 (2012), which is 34.7; adding 11.6 for the first nine months of 2013 gives 46.3. Holds. The release, a day earlier, says "These out of period errors totaling $47.9 million" (FY2013 release, Table 1, p. unnumbered): 1.6 more, unexplained.
- **Ordinary reading:** Small bookkeeping errors, 5.8% of 2011 and 2.0% of 2012 pre-tax income, found and corrected in the year a new ledger system went in.
- **If the business is weakening:** Not a business signal in itself; growth is measured from slightly lower bases, and Brazil had collection problems.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Nearly every error had lowered costs. The total equals 74% of fourth-quarter pre-tax income (62.3), yet "management concluded that our internal control over financial reporting was effective as of December 31, 2013" (FY2013 annual report, p. 159). Errors in three unrelated areas make further ones more likely.
- **Read next:** Note 2, the correction tables by quarter, and Item 9A.

**Flag 3: Fourth-quarter FY2013 gross margin fell**
- **The company says:** "In a difficult fourth quarter, we achieved an adjusted earnings per share of $0.26 despite an estimated $0.12 impact of lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (FY2013 release, p. 2). Same explanation last year: no; then it was "increased volume, strong residual values and strong cost management performance" (FY2012 release, p. 3).
- **The note adds:** My step 1 quarter mixed bases. On the revised basis (FY2013 release Table 1) gross margin was 18.5% against 22.1%, so 3.5 points of the fall stand. Note 8 puts vehicle-sale losses down to "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles" (FY2013 annual report, p. 126).
- **Test:** U.S. car rental in the quarter: average fleet +22.4%, rental days +16.1%, revenue per day −1.4%, depreciation +35.5% (333.4 against 246.0). The 3.5 points are worth 90.2 pre-tax; $0.12 a share is 85.7 pre-tax at the release's 35% tax rate. Holds.
- **Ordinary reading:** One soft quarter while a large acquisition is absorbed: too many cars for the demand, soon corrected.
- **If the business is weakening:** Prices, utilisation and used-car values fell together, and the release expects excess fleet to cost another "$0.07 to $0.08 adjusted earnings per share" (FY2013 release, p. 6) next quarter.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The quarter's tax charge (62.9) exceeded its pre-tax income (62.3) with no quarterly explanation, and the quarter absorbed the corrections; costs held back earlier in the year would surface here.
- **Read next:** the fourth-quarter segment tables and Note 9; a quarterly tax reconciliation is not in the documents.

**Flag 4: Fleet depreciation rate falling as the fleet grows**
- **The company says:** "The favorable adjustments reflect changes from the impact of car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion and the optimization of fleet holding periods" (FY2013 annual report, p. 126). Same explanation last year: no; then the cuts were "indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012 annual report, p. 113).
- **The note adds:** Rate changes cut U.S. car depreciation by 44.2 in 2013, 139.4 in 2012 and 26.7 in 2011. The stated holding period for cars moved from "4 to 28 months" (FY2012 annual report, p. 81) to "4 to 36 months" (FY2013 annual report, p. 91). U.S. vehicle disposals swung from gains of 100.6 to losses of 48.2.
- **Test:** Depreciation before disposal adjustments was 16.1%, 15.2% and 14.3% of average fleet cost in 2011, 2012 and 2013. The 2012 cut equals 31.6% of that year's pre-tax income, the 2013 cut 6.7%. Rates were cut again in a year of sale losses. Partly holds.
- **Ordinary reading:** Cars are kept longer and sold through better channels, so a lower monthly charge is right; Donlen and Dollar Thrifty also changed the fleet mix.
- **If the business is weakening:** Used-car values are falling and the fleet is too large. The FY2012 release forecast "lower monthly depreciation per vehicle in the U.S. of no less than 4% to 5% in 2013" (FY2012 release, p. 5); on segment figures, depreciation per company-operated U.S. car rose about 2.9%.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Residual values were set too high, lifting 2012 and 2013 income; the shortfall now arrives as losses on sale and a longer assumed holding period.
- **Read next:** Note 8 and the depreciation policy in Note 2; the critical accounting estimates section is not in the attached pages.

**Flag 5: Other assets, restricted cash and payables against activity (FY2013)**
- **The company says:** Other assets: total assets rose partly on "our strategic investment in China Auto Rental" (FY2013 annual report, p. 133). Restricted cash (up 308.3) and payables: nothing in the attached pages explains either. Same explanation last year: not applicable.
- **The note adds:** "the fair value of debt securities was $151.0 million and $0.0 million" (FY2013 annual report, p. 145), held in other assets and valued with unobservable inputs. Fleet-related restricted cash rose 277.7 (FY2013 release Table 8). After a missed requirement, "our ability to borrow under these notes was temporarily restricted at December 31, 2013" (FY2013 annual report, p. 108).
- **Test:** Revised basis: other assets rose 227.9, of which the China notes are 151.0 (66%), including a 21.0 unrealised gain. Partly holds. Payables fell 3.5%; without fleet and property purchases owed, 623.2 against 721.2 (−13.6%) while direct operating costs rose 19.7%. Nothing to test that against.
- **Ordinary reading:** An investment was made, more cash sat in fleet-financing accounts after the November refinancing, and suppliers were paid sooner under a new payables system.
- **If the business is weakening:** Cash held in restricted accounts and a borrowing restriction at year-end point to tighter fleet financing; suppliers may be shortening terms.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Lower payables against higher costs can mean expenses not yet booked. The report already corrects "$3.1 million adjustments related to under accruals" (FY2013 annual report, p. 87) for 2013.
- **Read next:** Note 5 (the amortization event), Note 19, and a breakdown of accounts payable and accrued liabilities, which the documents lack.

**Adjusted earnings.** Adjusted pre-tax income exceeded reported by 490.1 in 2013 (74% of reported), 450.9 in 2012 (102%, revised basis) and 356.2 in 2011 (110%). The excluded items are called "certain one-time charges and non-operational items" (FY2013 release, Exhibit 1, p. unnumbered), yet restructuring is excluded every year (77.0, 38.0, 56.4), as are purchase accounting and debt charges, and the line "Other unusual/non-recurring" (FY2013 release, Table 6, p. unnumbered) is 50.7 in 2013 and 44.0 in 2012, each with a litigation accrual. Adjusted net income assumes 35% tax against an actual 47.8%; at the actual rate 2013 would be 602.0, not 749.6. The cash measure also changed: corporate cash flow of −2,183.4 for 2012 (FY2012 release) is now free cash flow of 155.1.

**Reserve releases.** No reserve is described as released through a change in estimate. Nearest items: (1) the depreciation-rate changes are estimate changes that cut 2013 expense by 44.2 while U.S. vehicle sales turned to a 48.2 loss (Flag 4). (2) Public liability and property damage rose 4.7% against revenue up 19.4%; the company says it "was primarily related to Dollar Thrifty" (FY2013 annual report, p. 138), which it already owned at December 2012. Claims paid are not disclosed, so this cannot be tested. (3) U.S. car rental shows a 9.6 unusual credit in the fourth quarter (FY2013 release Table 6) that the release does not describe; the Advantage support agreement, a 15.6 liability at December 2012, was terminated that month. The documents do not connect the two.
