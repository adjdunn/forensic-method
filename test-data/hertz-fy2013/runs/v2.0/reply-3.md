**1. Items**

**A sold business kept using Hertz's cars, then failed** (Note 15, p. 146)
- **The note says:** "Based on our impairment analysis, we recorded an impairment charge of $40.0 million to write down the carrying value of the vehicles subleased to Simply Wheelz to their fair value during the quarter ended September 30, 2013."
- **Why it matters:** Advantage was sold in December 2012 for about 16.0, but Hertz kept leasing it cars and promised 17.0 of support. Within a year the buyer asked Hertz to "forbear from seeking collection of all amounts owed to it by Simply Wheelz" (Note 4, p. 101). The amount owed is not given.
- **Test:** 40.0 on a fair value of 279 is a 12.5% write-down. The adjusted-income table excludes 44.0 for the same event; the extra 4.0 is not explained. Charges on Advantage since the sale total 75.4 against 19.6 received. Partly holds.
- **Ordinary reading:** A divestiture required to win approval for Dollar Thrifty went badly; the loss was taken and the agreements ended in December 2013.
- **If the business is weakening:** The returned cars add to an excess fleet and must be sold into falling used-car prices; the valuation table (p. 147) puts the probability of payment at 0%.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Sublease revenue of 65.0 (FY2013 release Table 8) stayed in adjusted income while the loss was excluded as unusual. Check whether that revenue was collected, and why the charge is dated September when the forbearance request came in October.

**A financing requirement was missed without the company knowing** (Note 5, p. 108)
- **The note says:** "As of December 31, 2013, a requirement under the HVF II Series 2013-B Notes was unknowingly not met, resulting in the occurrence of an amortization event under the HVF II Series 2013-B Notes that also triggered amortization events under certain other series of our outstanding U.S. rental car variable funding notes."
- **Why it matters:** These notes fund the U.S. rental fleet. The breach was found in January 2014 and waived. It sits beside the error corrections and a new ledger system in the same half-year, with controls judged effective.
- **Test:** The note gives no figure for the requirement: nothing to test. For scale, U.S. variable funding notes are 3,025.0 (18.5% of debt); fleet facilities had 23.9 available under borrowing-base limits out of 1,041.1 of unused capacity; 7,337.8 (45% of debt) matures in 2014 and 2015.
- **Ordinary reading:** A technical slip in a facility set up five weeks before year-end, cured by waivers from every noteholder whose consent was needed.
- **If the business is weakening:** Fleet lenders advance against vehicle values. With residual values falling and almost no unused borrowing base, a real shortfall would pull cash into collateral.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** If a compliance test can fail unnoticed, other figures built from the same fleet records (depreciation, borrowing base, restricted cash) deserve checking.

**Share awards depend on an adjusted measure the company shapes** (Note 7, p. 125)
- **The note says:** "Of the total PSUs awarded 1.1 million PSUs have a performance condition under which the number of units that will ultimately be awarded will vary from 0% to 150% of the original grant, based on 2013 and combined 2013-2014 Corporate EBITDA results."
- **Why it matters:** "Corporate EBITDA, as presented herein, represents EBITDA as adjusted for car rental fleet interest, car rental fleet depreciation and certain other items" (FY2013 release, Exhibit 1, p. unnumbered). Depreciation rates and the choice of what is unusual both move the pay measure; loan covenants use it too.
- **Test:** Corporate EBITDA was 2,043.7, which is 7.5% below the low end of the 2,210 to 2,270 guided in February 2013 (FY2012 release, p. 4). It includes 283.4 of unusual items added back (13.9%) and the 44.2 depreciation-rate cut. Whether the award condition was met is not disclosed.
- **Ordinary reading:** A common award design. Because the measure deducts fleet depreciation, managers bear the cost of the fleet.
- **If the business is weakening:** The year ended below guidance, and fourth-quarter stock compensation fell to 3.5 from 8.0, consistent with awards now expected to pay out less.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Those who set residual values and label charges as unusual are paid on the result. The 2012 measure carried a 139.4 rate cut, 8.6% of it.

**Guaranteed airport fees jumped while revenue-based fees fell** (Note 10, p. 129)
- **The note says:** "We have various concession agreements, which provide for payment of rents and a percentage of revenue with a guaranteed minimum, and real estate leases under which the following amounts were expensed"
- **Why it matters:** Minimum fees are owed whatever the revenue, and the commitments are not on the balance sheet. A larger fixed share makes profit fall faster when prices or volumes slip, as in the fourth quarter.
- **Test:** Minimum fixed fees rose 53.5% (263.7 to 404.8); revenue-based fees fell 6.8% (316.2 to 294.6) on revenue up 19.4%. The fixed share of concession fees went from 45.5% to 57.9%. Future minimum rents and concessions total 2,649.9, with 549.2 due in 2014.
- **Ordinary reading:** Dollar Thrifty's airport contracts carry guaranteed minimums, and 2013 was the first full year with them.
- **If the business is weakening:** Fees that should rise with revenue fell, which suggests more locations earning less than their guaranteed minimum.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The 2012 column changed between reports (minimum fixed fees 249.6 last year, 263.7 now) without comment; check the basis of each year before trusting the trend.

**A judgment that could exceed $40 million is called immaterial** (Note 13, p. 137)
- **The note says:** "A judgment - which could potentially exceed $40.0 million - has still not been issued by the court."
- **Why it matters:** The court has already ruled for the plaintiffs on restitution, with interest, yet "We continue to believe the outcome of this case will not be material to our financial condition, results of operations or cash flows." A shareholder suit filed in November 2013 separately alleges "material misrepresentations and/or omissions of material fact in its public disclosures during the period from February 25, 2013 through November 4, 2013" (Note 13, p. 138).
- **Test:** 40.0 is 6.0% of 2013 pre-tax income and 64% of the fourth quarter's. The same report treats 46.3 of errors as material to a quarter. No reserve amount is given. Partly holds: small for the year, large for a quarter.
- **Ordinary reading:** The company expects to win on appeal or settle for less, and says it has reserved where a loss is probable.
- **If the business is weakening:** A cash cost, and refunds on a fee the business depends on at airports.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The reserve may lag the court's rulings, and any charge is likely to be shown as unusual: litigation accruals of 5.7 and 14.0 were left out of adjusted income in 2013 and 2012.

**The sponsors sold out; the company bought part of the block with borrowed money** (Note 1, p. 82)
- **The note says:** "In connection with the offering, Hertz Holdings repurchased from the underwriters 23,200,000 of the 60,050,777 shares of common stock sold by the Sponsors."
- **Why it matters:** The sponsor funds held about 26% at the end of 2012 (FY2012 annual report, p. 79) and none after May 2013. Hertz then issued notes "to replenish a portion of its liquidity, after having dividended $467.2 million in available liquidity to us" (Note 5, p. 106).
- **Test:** 23.2 million shares at $20.14 is 467.2: holds. Share purchases for the year were 554.7, 160% of net income; the 250.0 of new notes replaced 54% of the March outlay.
- **Ordinary reading:** Private-equity owners exit eventually, and buying part of the block limits pressure on the share price.
- **If the business is weakening:** Cash went to shareholders in a year when it was needed for fleet, and with 45% of debt due within two years.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The best-informed holders left months before a year-end that brought error corrections, excess fleet and a missed outlook; the shareholder suit covers the same months.
