**1. Changes that matter**

**Depreciation (Note 8): the reason for lower rates was rewritten and the word strong dropped**
- **Before:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2012 and 2011." (FY2012 annual report, p. 113)
- **After:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013, 2012 and 2011." (FY2013 annual report, p. 126)
- **Why it matters:** The same note now blames sale losses on "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles" (FY2013 annual report, p. 126), yet rates were cut again. U.S. disposals went from a gain of 2.4% of proceeds to a loss of 1.1%.
- **Ordinary reading:** Residual values came back to normal after two unusually good years, and the wording was updated to match.
- **If the business is weakening:** Used-car prices are falling, so cars sell below book value and each sale adds a loss.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The sentence keeps the case for low rates alive after the evidence turned; rates may still be too low.

**Fleet policy (Note 2): the longest holding period for cars rose from 28 to 36 months**
- **Before:** "Useful lives are as follows:" (FY2012 annual report, p. 81), then "Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012 annual report, p. 81)
- **After:** "Holding periods are as follows:" (FY2013 annual report, p. 91), then "Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013 annual report, p. 91)
- **Why it matters:** A longer holding period spreads depreciation over more months. The change is not described as a change in estimate and no effect is given. Average car fleet at cost divided by annual purchases rose from 15.1 to 16.8 months.
- **Ordinary reading:** Dollar Thrifty keeps cars longer, and the heading was corrected to say what the range means.
- **If the business is weakening:** Cars are kept longer because they cannot be sold at book value; an older fleet costs more to run and rents for less.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Stretching lives lowers the monthly charge without a disclosed rate change.

**Reserve notes: earlier years' figures changed with no explanation**
- **Before:** "Balance at December 31 | $17.2 | $21.6 | $27.2" (FY2012 annual report, p. 116)
- **After:** "Balance at December 31 | $11.0 | $18.7 | $41.4" (FY2013 annual report, p. 129)
- **Why it matters:** Unrecognised tax benefits at December 2011 are now 41.4, not 21.6, and the 2012 release of prior-period positions is 26.0, not 6.8. The error-correction note does not mention it. Also moved without comment: 2012 rent and concession expense (714.9 to 722.2) and 2012 restructuring cash payments (26.8 to 36.8).
- **Ordinary reading:** A change from net to gross presentation in tables that do not affect earnings.
- **If the business is weakening:** Little to read into it, beyond tax exposures larger than previously shown.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** A reserve that appears and is released inside a comparative year makes the reserve trend hard to rely on; ask what the 19.8 was.

**Controls (Item 9A): a new ledger system appears; the conclusion does not change**
- **Before:** "No changes in our internal control over financial reporting occurred during the fiscal quarter ended December 31, 2012 that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our internal control over financial reporting." (FY2012 annual report, p. 141)
- **After:** "In the third quarter of 2013, the Company implemented Oracle general ledger, accounts payable and a portion of fixed assets, purchasing and procurement modules" (FY2013 annual report, p. 159)
- **Why it matters:** What stayed the same: management and the auditor call controls effective in both years, although FY2013 corrects 46.3 of errors reaching back to 2010 and a financing requirement was "unknowingly not met" (FY2013 annual report, p. 108). FY2012's exclusion of Dollar Thrifty (21% of assets) is gone.
- **Ordinary reading:** A planned system upgrade surfaced old errors too small to count as a material weakness.
- **If the business is weakening:** Absorbing an acquisition and moving headquarters while replacing the ledger strains the finance function.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Same conclusion, different facts: three kinds of error over four years and no control deficiency named.

**Debt (Note 5): the sentence that put revolving fleet debt in the first year was dropped**
- **Before:** "These amounts are reflected as short-term borrowings, regardless of the facility maturity date, as these facilities are revolving in nature and/or the outstanding borrowings have maturities of three months or less." (FY2012 annual report, p. 93)
- **After:** "dropped".
- **Why it matters:** Debt shown as due within a year fell from 6,218.8 (40% of the total) to 2,053.3 (13%) because revolving facilities now sit at their expected maturity. The change of basis is not stated.
- **Ordinary reading:** The new table is more informative: each facility is shown when it is expected to mature.
- **If the business is weakening:** The old basis was the cautious one. Fleet funding depends on lenders renewing, as the year-end amortization event showed.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** Near-term maturities look two-thirds smaller through a presentation change that is nowhere announced.

**Segments (Notes 2 and 11): one car rental segment became three**
- **Before:** "We have identified two reportable segments: rental and leasing of cars, crossovers and light trucks, or “car rental,” and rental of industrial, construction and material handling equipment, or “equipment rental.”" (FY2012 annual report, p. 117)
- **After:** "In the third quarter of 2013 we changed the composition of our reportable segments upon further consideration of the guidance" (FY2013 annual report, p. 83)
- **Why it matters:** No new standard is named. The old segment's 13.4% adjusted margin in 2012 blended 17.8% in the U.S., 4.1% internationally and Donlen (17.3% and 5.9% in 2013). Note 8 now shows international disposal losses every year (16.0, 17.3, 15.2), once netted against U.S. gains.
- **Ordinary reading:** More detail after Donlen and Dollar Thrifty changed the group's shape.
- **If the business is weakening:** International car rental earned 41.8 before tax on revenue of 2,382.5 (1.8%), so profit rests on the U.S. business where pricing slipped.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The wording suggests the earlier aggregation did not meet the guidance; weak units were out of sight in earlier years.

**Adjusted measure (Note 11): the debt label lost the word non-cash, and more is excluded**
- **Before:** "Adjusted pre-tax income (loss) is calculated as income (loss) before income taxes plus other reconciling items, non-cash purchase accounting charges, non-cash debt charges and certain one-time charges and non-operational items." (FY2012 annual report, p. 118)
- **After:** "Adjusted pre-tax income is calculated as income before income taxes plus non-cash purchase accounting charges, debt-related charges relating to the amortization and write-off of debt financing costs and debt discounts and certain one-time charges and non-operational items." (FY2013 annual report, p. 131)
- **Why it matters:** Lines with amounts excluded rose from 7 for 2012 to 11 for 2013. Three new labels (integration, relocation, impairment) total 91.8, or 18.7% of the 490.1 excluded. FY2012's claim that the measure best reflects ongoing operations is gone.
- **Ordinary reading:** 2013 had unusual events: an integration, a headquarters move, a counterparty failure and a refinancing.
- **If the business is weakening:** If the costs of running the enlarged group keep arriving under new labels, adjusted profit overstates what it earns.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The definition widens as needed: the debt label no longer says non-cash, and cash costs such as integration and relocation are excluded as one-time.

**Legal (Note 13): the fee case gained a number**
- **Before:** "The court has since entered a stay order and the parties will again be engaging in mediation." (FY2012 annual report, p. 122)
- **After:** "A judgment - which could potentially exceed $40.0 million - has still not been issued by the court." (FY2013 annual report, p. 137)
- **Why it matters:** The case moved against the company and a size appears for the first time (6.0% of pre-tax income). Neither year says an amount is accrued for it. A shareholder suit is new in FY2013.
- **Ordinary reading:** Litigation progresses; the company expects a smaller outcome and says so.
- **If the business is weakening:** A cash cost on a fee charged across the airport business.
- **If the reporting is stretched:** The accrual may lag the rulings (step 3).
