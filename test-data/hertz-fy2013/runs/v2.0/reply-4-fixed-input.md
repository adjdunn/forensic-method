### 1. Changes that matter

> **Depreciation of rental cars (Note 8): rates cut again, the reason changed, the word *strong* dropped**
> - **Before:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2012 and 2011." (FY2012 report, p. 113)
> - **After:** "The favorable adjustments reflect changes from the impact of car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion and the optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty." (FY2013 report, p. 126)
> - **Why it matters:** U.S. rate cuts lowered 2013 depreciation by $44.2 million while U.S. car disposals swung from a $100.6 million gain to a $48.2 million loss. The same note now cites "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles" (FY2013 report, p. 126).
> - **Ordinary reading:** Dollar Thrifty cars are kept longer and sold through better channels, so less depreciation is needed each month; the disposal losses came from the timing of sales.
> - **If the business is weakening:** Used-car prices are falling, cars are being held to avoid selling into that market, and more disposal losses follow.
> - **If the reporting is stretched:** Depreciation is set too low against falling resale values, and the shortfall arrives later as disposal losses or a catch-up charge.

> **Accounting policies (Note 2): car useful lives relabelled holding periods; top of range raised from 28 to 36 months**
> - **Before:** "Revenue earning equipment is stated at cost, net of related discounts. Useful lives are as follows:" (FY2012 report, p. 81), then "Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012 report, p. 81)
> - **After:** "Revenue earning equipment is stated at cost, net of related discounts. Holding periods are as follows:" (FY2013 report, p. 91), then "Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013 report, p. 91)
> - **Why it matters:** Depreciation is straight-line over the holding period, so a longer period lowers each month's charge. The top of the range rose 8 months (28.6%), yet the business description still gives eighteen months as the U.S. average.
> - **Ordinary reading:** Over 90% of the U.S. fleet is now non-program cars, which are kept longer, and the label was corrected to match how depreciation is calculated.
> - **If the business is weakening:** Cars are ageing in the fleet because selling them brings less; fleet costs rose on "longer holding periods and the impact of recalls" (FY2013 report, p. 53).
> - **If the reporting is stretched:** A longer assumed holding period spreads the same cost over more months and lifts profit now, with no change in what the cars will fetch.

> **Accounting policies and controls: new Correction of Errors section; controls conclusion unchanged**
> - **Before:** "Certain prior period amounts have been reclassified to conform with current year presentation." (FY2012 report, p. 80) was the only statement about prior-period changes.
> - **After:** "During the fourth quarter of 2013, we identified certain out of period errors totaling $46.3 million, of which $34.7 million ($21.0 million, net of tax) related to our previously issued consolidated financial statements for the years ended December 31, 2012, 2011 and prior." (FY2013 report, p. 83)
> - **Why it matters:** $46.3 million is 74.3% of revised fourth-quarter pre-tax income; prior-year pre-tax income fell 2.0% (2012) and 5.8% (2011). Controls are still called effective, though a new ledger went live in the third quarter and a debt requirement was "unknowingly not met" (FY2013 report, p. 67).
> - **Ordinary reading:** Small bookkeeping errors (vendor incentives in the wrong line, Brazil receivables) were found at year-end and fixed by revising prior years; none was material to any one year.
> - **If the business is weakening:** The Dollar Thrifty integration, a ledger change and a headquarters move are straining the finance function, so more clean-up items may follow.
> - **If the reporting is stretched:** The errors lowered income in every year affected, and calling them immaterial avoided a restatement and a reported control weakness.

> **Adjusted measures: pricing metric redefined; add-back wording loosened**
> - **Before:** "Rental rate revenue per transaction day is calculated as total rental rate revenue, divided by the total number of transaction days, with all periods adjusted to eliminate the effect of fluctuations in foreign currency." (FY2012 report, p. 49)
> - **After:** "Total RPD is calculated as total revenue less revenue from fleet subleases, divided by the total number of transaction days, with all periods adjusted to eliminate the effect of fluctuations in foreign currency." (FY2013 report, p. 49)
> - **Why it matters:** The old metric left out 22.0% of car rental revenue as "driven by factors beyond the control of management" (FY2012 report, p. 49); the new one leaves out 1.0% in the U.S. Adjusted pre-tax income now adds back "debt-related charges" (FY2013 report, p. 50), not "non-cash debt charges" (FY2012 report, p. 50), plus three new lines totalling $91.8 million.
> - **Ordinary reading:** Dollar Thrifty earns more from add-ons, so total revenue per day describes the combined business better; the new add-backs are one-off integration, relocation and impairment costs.
> - **If the business is weakening:** Base rental rates are flat or falling and growth is coming from fees. The old metric fell 3.2% in 2012; the new one shows +1.4% for the U.S. in 2013.
> - **If the reporting is stretched:** The old metric was withdrawn, so its 2013 reading cannot be seen, and looser add-back wording lets more costs sit outside the headline profit figure.

> **Risk factors: fleet-value risk reworded, a forecast dropped, a new loss added**
> - **Before:** "Declines in the value of the non-program cars in our fleet and declines in the overall number of program cars in our fleet could materially adversely impact our financial condition, results of operations, liquidity and cash flows." (FY2012 report, p. 22)
> - **After:** "Increased fleet costs, either generally or due to declines in the value of the non-program cars in our fleet, could materially adversely impact our financial condition, results of operations, liquidity and cash flows." (FY2013 report, p. 23) Dropped: "We expect this percentage to continue to decrease in the future" (FY2012 report, p. 22).
> - **Why it matters:** The forecast of fewer program (manufacturer buy-back) cars is gone; the non-program share of the U.S. fleet fell from 95% to 91%. A new risk factor covers about 20,000 cars subleased to Advantage's buyer, written down $40.0 million (12.5%).
> - **Ordinary reading:** The wording was updated once the fleet mix settled, and the Advantage buyer's bankruptcy is a one-off tied to a divestiture regulators required.
> - **If the business is weakening:** Resale risk on owned cars is biting, so the mix is moving back toward buy-back cars; about 9,000 subleased cars were still out in February 2014.
> - **If the reporting is stretched:** The one group of cars tested for impairment needed a 12.5% write-down. That test also reflects the sublease terms, so it raises a question about the rest of the fleet, not an answer.

> **Legal proceedings: Item 3 cut to a cross-reference; one case now sized; a shareholder suit added**
> - **Before:** "The court has since entered a stay order and the parties will again be engaging in mediation." (FY2012 report, p. 32)
> - **After:** "A judgment - which could potentially exceed $40.0 million - has still not been issued by the court." (FY2013 report, p. 137)
> - **Why it matters:** The Nevada airport-fee case moved from stayed to class certified, with restitution granted in part. $40.0 million is 6.0% of 2013 pre-tax income, and no accrual is stated. A suit filed November 20, 2013 "alleging violations of the federal securities laws" (FY2013 report, p. 138) is new.
> - **Ordinary reading:** Two older class actions settled in 2013, the company is appealing this one and expects an outcome that is not material, and the shareholder suit is at an early stage.
> - **If the business is weakening:** A cash judgment with interest and notice costs could land in 2014, on top of relocation and integration spending.
> - **If the reporting is stretched:** A likely loss may be under-accrued: the report gives a possible size but, unlike the settled cases, does not say an estimate has been booked.

> **Related parties: the Sponsors sold out, partly to the company**
> - **Before:** "the Sponsors reduced their holdings to approximately 26% of the outstanding shares of common stock of Hertz Holdings." (FY2012 report, p. 79)
> - **After:** "In connection with the offering, Hertz Holdings repurchased from the underwriters 23,200,000 of the 60,050,777 shares of common stock sold by the Sponsors." (FY2013 report, p. 82)
> - **Why it matters:** The 2005 buyout funds sold 109.9 million shares in March and May 2013; the company bought 38.6% of the March block. Treasury purchases were $554.7 million, 160% of net income. A new note reports $3.1 million of relocation spending on executive officers, including home purchases.
> - **Ordinary reading:** A planned exit by financial owners years after the buyout; the buyback offset dilution; Sponsor disclosures were removed because the relationship ended.
> - **If the business is weakening:** Owners with board seats sold in the first half; the write-down and the error corrections came in the second half.
> - **If the reporting is stretched:** Lower depreciation rates and results later revised were in place while the largest holders sold. Check the quarterly reports for the order of events.

### 3. Sections not in the documents

Inventory has no note in either report (only the balance sheet line), receivables have no note beyond the policy paragraph and the allowance schedule, and adjusted measures were compared as defined in the annual reports only; revenue recognition was present and unchanged apart from licensees becoming franchisees.
