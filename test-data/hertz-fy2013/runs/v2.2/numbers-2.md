**Rental equipment depreciation: a lower charge on an older fleet**
- **The company says:** rate cuts on U.S. cars were "indicative of the residual values experienced" (FY2013 annual report, p. 42). Last year: the same sentence, but with "strong residual values" (FY2012 annual report, p. 113).
- **Test:** before disposal gains and losses, the charge fell from 15.2% of average fleet cost in 2012 to 14.3% in 2013 (16.1% in 2011; 2011 and 2012 as revised), about $152m on the 2013 fleet. U.S. car disposals swung from a $100.6m gain to a $48.2m loss, and the same report cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p. 54), so the explanation partly holds: it fits 2012, not 2013.
- **Reading:** most likely the reporting stretched: rate changes lowered U.S. depreciation by a further $44.2m in a year when cars sold below book value and accumulated depreciation reached 18.5% of car cost, from 14.7%. Losses confined to the acquired Dollar Thrifty cars would point to an ordinary reading.
- **Read next:** Note 8 (FY2013 annual report, p. 126) and the revenue earning equipment estimate (p. 45).

**Receivables fell 19.5% while revenue rose 19.4% (whole company; FY2012 as revised, 1,879.7)**
- **The company says:** "a decrease in fleet receivables within our U.S. car rental segment", put down to "timing of purchases and sales" (FY2013 annual report, p. 133). Last year: nothing.
- **Test:** receivables from fleet sales fell 261.3 (618.6 to 357.3), 71% of the 367.1 fall. The rest still fell 8.4% (1,261.1 to 1,155.3) against revenue up 19.4%, or 5.6% against 2012 pro forma for Dollar Thrifty, so the explanation partly holds.
- **Reading:** most likely ordinary: year-end timing of car sales explains most of it. Possibly not, if the other 105.8 was written off or deconsolidated instead of collected; the cash flow statement shows receivables as a 34.7 use of cash.
- **Read next:** the cash flow statement and its non-cash disclosures (pp. 80-81); the sections provided have no note splitting receivables into fleet, trade and other.

**Allowance for doubtful accounts: 2.00% of gross receivables, from 1.53% as revised (1.31% first reported)**
- **The company says:** nothing about the 2013 level. Its only comment is that corrected errors included "allowances for doubtful accounts in Brazil" (FY2013 annual report, p. 83), which lifted the 2012 allowance from 25.1 to 29.3. Last year: nothing.
- **Test:** the ratio rose because gross receivables fell 19.2%; the allowance grew 5.1% (29.3 to 30.8) while write-offs grew 37.5% (29.3 to 40.3). The provision grew 19.8% on the revised 2012 figure (38.3), not the 34.6% in my last reply, which used the first-reported 34.1.
- **Reading:** most likely ordinary, possibly thin: the allowance covers 0.76 of a year's write-offs, against 1.00 in 2012. A second year of write-offs outrunning the allowance would point to the reporting stretched.
- **Read next:** Schedule II (p. 158) against the cash flow statement (p. 80): the schedule charges 41.9 to expense, the cash flow statement shows a 45.9 provision.

**The other flags**
- **Fourth-quarter gross margin (22.1% to 18.5%, both quarters as revised in the FY2013 release):** "lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (FY2013 release, p. 2). Held: U.S. Total RPD fell 1.4% and the U.S. fleet grew 22.4% against rental days up 16.1%, though U.S. fleet depreciation per car also rose 10.7%; the SG&A fall is acquisition costs dropping out (adjusted SG&A 204.7 to 201.1).
- **Prepaid expenses and other assets (up 227.9 from the revised 489.3):** "strategic investment in China Auto Rental" (FY2013 annual report, p. 133). Mostly held: its convertible notes sit in this line at 151.0 (p. 145), 66% of the rise.

**Adjusted earnings**
- Adjusted pre-tax income exceeded reported by 490.1 in FY2013 (1,153.2 against 663.1) and by 450.9 in FY2012 (901.5 against 450.6 first reported; 892.3 against 441.4 revised): 74% and about 100% of reported.
- Restructuring charges are added back under "Extraordinary, unusual or non-recurring gains and losses" in both releases (FY2013 release, Table 7, p. 2; FY2012 release, p. 15): 77.0 in 2013, 38.0 in 2012 and 56.4 in 2011, from initiatives dated "During 2007 through 2013" (FY2013 annual report, p. 44). With purchase accounting, debt charges, acquisition costs and other items, repeating add-backs are 75% of the 2013 gap.

**Reserve releases:** none is described as a change in estimate. The one reduction found is a $5.8m "adjustment to Advantage divestiture support payments" (FY2013 annual report, p. 56), made in the year that agreement ended; the public liability reserve rose 4.7% (332.2 to 347.7), "primarily related to Dollar Thrifty" (p. 138), and the reports give no claims paid to test it against.
