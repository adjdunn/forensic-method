**1. Full write-ups**

**Depreciation (Note 8): strong residual values no longer claimed, new reasons for cutting rates**
- **Before:** "indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012 report, p. 113).
- **After:** "also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 report, p. 126), behind a new sentence crediting "car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion and the optimization of fleet holding periods" (FY2013 report, p. 126).
- **Why it matters:** The reason for a $44.2M cut in 2013 U.S. depreciation moved from market prices to sales channels and holding periods, yet retail was about 12% of cars sold against 13% in 2012 and auctions rose from 33% to about 38% (car rental worldwide; 2013 is a rough weighting).
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting stretched: the old reason could be checked against disposal results, which turned from a gain to a loss in the U.S.; the new ones cannot. A rising retail share and a return to disposal gains would point to ordinary.

**Depreciation (Note 2): longest period for cars raised from 28 to 36 months**
- **Before:** "Useful lives are as follows" (FY2012 report, p. 81), with cars at "4 to 28 months" (FY2012 report, p. 81).
- **After:** "Holding periods are as follows" (FY2013 report, p. 91), with cars at "4 to 36 months" (FY2013 report, p. 91).
- **Why it matters:** The top of the range rose 8 months (28.6%) with no separate effect stated, while car depreciation fell from 1.39% to 1.29% of cost a month and accumulated depreciation rose from 14.7% to 18.5% of the cost of cars (whole company, rough).
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting stretched: holding cars longer lowers the monthly charge now and leaves an older fleet exposed to the weaker resale prices the same report describes. It would be ordinary if the wider range only brings in Dollar Thrifty's existing practice.

**Non-GAAP: pricing measure redefined, its justification kept**
- **Before:** rental rate revenue per day, "excluding revenue derived from fueling and concession and other expense pass-throughs" (FY2012 report, p. 49), because those are "driven by factors beyond the control of management" (FY2012 report, p. 49).
- **After:** "Total RPD is calculated as total revenue less revenue from fleet subleases" (FY2013 report, p. 49), still said to cover what "management has the ability to control" (FY2013 report, p. 49).
- **Why it matters:** For the U.S. in 2012 the new measure is $46.33 a day against $39.07 on the old one, so $7.26 a day (18.6% more) of fuel, fee and other revenue now counts as pricing; the old measure showed 2012 U.S. pricing down 3.1% and is not given for 2013.
- **Reading:** Can't tell: the 1.4% rise in U.S. Total RPD for 2013 may owe as much to fees and fuel as to rental rates. The 2013 figure on the old basis would settle it.

**2. Other changes**
- **Non-GAAP:** "non-cash debt charges" (FY2012 report, p. 50) became "debt-related charges" (FY2013 report, p. 50); three new exclusion lines total $91.8M, 13.8% of 2013 pre-tax income.
- **Controls:** the carve-out "management has excluded Dollar Thrifty from its assessment" (FY2012 report, p. 71) is gone, and the company "implemented Oracle general ledger" (FY2013 report, p. 159) one quarter before the errors surfaced.
- **Risk factors and legal:** a new factor opens "We have recognized losses" (FY2013 report, p. 24) on Simply Wheelz. The concession-fee case carried no figure last year (FY2012 report, p. 32) and now "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" (FY2013 report, p. 137).
- **Reserves:** the 2012 allowance for doubtful accounts was restated from $25.1M (FY2012 report, p. 72) to $29.3M (FY2013 report, p. 75). The public liability reserve's rise is now "primarily related to Dollar Thrifty" (FY2013 report, p. 138); no reason was given for 2012's larger $50.7M rise (FY2012 report, p. 123).
- **Debt maturities:** revolving borrowings were short-term "regardless of the facility maturity date" (FY2012 report, p. 63); FY2013 dropped this (p. 67), and debt shown as due within a year fell from 40.2% to 12.6% of the total.
