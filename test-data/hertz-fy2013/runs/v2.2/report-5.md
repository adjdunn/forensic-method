**Hertz Global Holdings, Inc.: FY2013 against FY2012, with the fourth quarters.**
Documents: the FY2013 and FY2012 annual reports (key sections), the statements spreadsheet, and the two numbers-track replies (which also used the fourth-quarter releases).

**Top five items to investigate**

**1. Fleet depreciation and residual values**
- **Shown:** Note 8 calls its rate cuts "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 p. 126), though U.S. disposals swung from a $100.6M gain to a $48.2M loss and the cuts still lowered U.S. depreciation by $44.2M. The longest period for cars went from "4 to 28 months" (FY2012 p. 81) to "4 to 36 months" (FY2013 p. 91) and accumulated depreciation on cars rose from 14.7% to 18.5% of cost, while the stated U.S. average stayed at eighteen months (p. 11 of both reports).
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting stretched: 2013 depreciation looks low for what cars fetched. Losses confined to the acquired Dollar Thrifty cars would point to ordinary.
- **Next:** Quarterly rate changes and disposal results in the 2013 10-Qs; U.S. fleet age.

**2. Adjusted measures**
- **Shown:** Adjusted pre-tax income leaves out "certain one-time charges and non-operational items" (FY2013 p. 131), yet restructuring was excluded every year ($645.4M since 2007, p. 140) and all exclusions were 73.9% of 2013 pre-tax income. The pricing measure was redefined (p. 49): $46.33 a day for the U.S. in 2012 against $39.07 before, with no old-basis figure for 2013.
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting stretched: recurring costs are treated as outside operations. An end to restructuring after the Dollar Thrifty integration would change this.
- **Next:** The 2013 pricing figure on the old basis.

**3. Error corrections and controls**
- **Shown:** "out of period errors totaling $46.3 million" (FY2013 p. 83), 74.3% of fourth-quarter pre-tax income, were spread back over 2010 to 2013, yet controls are called "effective as of December 31, 2013" (FY2013 p. 159). A financing requirement was "unknowingly not met" (FY2013 p. 108), and the tax note's "a decrease in the valuation allowance" (FY2013 p. 128) sits beside a $53.0M rise in Schedule II (p. 158).
- **Reading:** Possibly controls weaker than reported. The errors' small size against annual income (2012 pre-tax income down 2.0%) would point to ordinary clean-up.
- **Next:** The next 10-Q's controls section; how the errors were found.

**4. Receivables, allowance and bad debt**
- **Shown:** Receivables fell 19.5% while revenue rose 19.4%; fleet-sale timing explains 71% of the fall and the rest still fell 8.4%. Write-offs grew 37.5% against an allowance up 5.1%, and Schedule II charges $41.9M (p. 158) where the cash flow statement shows $45.9M (p. 80).
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary timing, possibly a thin allowance. A second year of write-offs outrunning the allowance would point to the reporting stretched.
- **Next:** Receivables split into fleet, trade and other; a reconciliation of the two bad-debt figures.

**5. Advantage buyer and legal exposures**
- **Shown:** A $40.0M impairment on cars subleased to the buyer of Advantage assumed a "Probability of Payment" (FY2013 p. 147) of 0%, and a new risk factor opens "We have recognized losses" (FY2013 p. 24). A concession-fee judgment "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" (FY2013 p. 137) yet is called not material.
- **Reading:** Most likely the business weakening at the edges: a divestiture returning as losses. An orderly return of the cars with no further charge would change this.
- **Next:** The Simply Wheelz settlement terms; the mediation outcome.

**Checked and explained:** the fourth-quarter gross margin fall (22.1% to 18.5%) fits weaker pricing and extra fleet; the rise in prepaid and other assets is mostly the China Auto Rental notes; the public liability reserve is 3.2% of revenue against 3.3% of 2012 pro forma revenue.

**Not examined:** U.S. fleet cost and age, a split of receivables, claims paid against the public liability reserve, and the quarterly reports: none is in the documents.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
