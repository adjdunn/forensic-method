2. Full write-ups
Capitalized costs and useful lives: U.S. car depreciation rates cut while cars sold at a loss
 The report says: "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in
the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013, 2012 and 2011" (Note 8, p. 126).
 Test: The claim is that U.S. car resale values in 2013 supported lower depreciation rates. The matching number is the
2013 result on U.S. car disposals in the same note: a net loss of $48.2M, against gains of $100.6M and $114.9M in 2012
and 2011. The claim holds for 2012 and 2011 but not for 2013. The note itself cites "declining residual values", and the
management discussion blames "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p. 54) after saying per-vehicle
depreciation fell on "improved procurement and remarketing efforts" (p. 42). That per-vehicle claim can't be tested as
stated, because the measure is not defined. Size: the rate cut is 6.7% of pre-tax and the disposal loss 7.3%.
 Reading: Most likely the reporting is stretched on top of a weakening used-car market, because rates were lowered by
$44.2M in the year disposals swung $148.8M from gain to loss. A different reading would follow if the cut came from longer
holding periods after the Dollar Thrifty integration and the losses from one-off sales timing, which the note also cites.
 Read next: The quarterly split of rate changes and disposal results in the 2013 10-Qs, and first-quarter 2014 U.S.
depreciation per vehicle.
Adjusted measures: restructuring treated as one-time
 The report says: Adjusted pre-tax income adds back "certain one-time charges and nonoperational items" (management
discussion, p. 50).
 Test: The claim is that the excluded charges, company-wide, do not recur. Restructuring charges were $77.0M, $38.0M
and $56.4M in 2013, 2012 and 2011, and $645.4M over 2007–2013 (p. 140), about $92M a year. The report also says "we
expect to incur further expenses" (p. 24). The claim does not hold for restructuring. Size: restructuring plus
restructuring-related charges are $98.8M, 14.9% of pre-tax. All exclusions are $490.1M, 73.9% of pre-tax.
 Reading: Most likely the measure is stretched rather than the business weakening, because a cost charged seven years
running is an operating cost. The reading would shift toward ordinary if 2013's charge proved mostly the headquarters
move ($21.9M) and Dollar Thrifty integration, and the charges then stopped.
 Read next: Adjusted pre-tax income with restructuring left in ($1,054.4M against $1,153.2M), tracked across years in the
next step.
Controls: effective, alongside errors in several years
 The report says: The errors "did not, individually or in the aggregate, result in a material misstatement" but "would have
been material to the fourth quarter" (Note 2, p. 83); controls were "effective" (Item 9A, p. 159).
 Test: The stated corrections cut 2012 pre-tax income by $9.2M (2.0%) and 2011 by $18.7M (5.8%; diluted earnings per
share from $0.40 to $0.37). Whether that is material is a judgment, so the claim can't be tested as a fact. The listed errors
do sum to $34.7M. Size: $34.7M moved to prior years is 5.2% of pre-tax; the $46.3M total is 7.0%, of which $11.6M (1.7%)
fell in 2013's first nine months.
 Reading: Possibly the control conclusion is generous, because the errors span vendor incentives, Brazil and
under-accruals, and a debt requirement was "unknowingly not being met" (p. 150). An ordinary reading would follow if the
errors came from one isolated process that had already been fixed.
 Read next: 2014 quarterly filings for further revisions or a changed control conclusion.
3. Also noted
 The provision for doubtful accounts is $45.9M in the cash flow statement (p. 80) but $41.9M in Schedule II (p. 158), a
$4.0M tie-out gap.
 The Simply Wheelz "impairment charges and other" is $44.0M in the adjusted-income table (p. 50) but $40.0M in the cash
flow statement (p. 80). About 9,000 vehicles were still with that lessee at February 1, 2014 (p. 24).
 The out-of-period errors total $46.3M in the annual report (p. 83) but $47.9M in the Q4 results release (unnumbered page).
 The text says selling, general and administrative expense rose $52.6M (p. 55); the table shows $54.1M (p. 52).
4. Not in the documents
Critical audit matters, a self-insurance movement table, goodwill headroom, amounts earned but not yet billed, a
loyalty-programme policy and any receivables sold or factored are not disclosed.
