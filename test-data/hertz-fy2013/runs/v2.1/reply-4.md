**Management discussion and risk factors: the shift to cars carried at the company's own resale risk is no longer the stated direction**
- **Before:** "we expect this percentage to continue to decrease in the future" (FY2012 annual report, p. 41), with "Non-program cars typically have lower acquisition costs and lower depreciation rates" (FY2012 annual report, p. 41).
- **After:** "this strategy remains flexible" (FY2013 annual report, p. 42); the second sentence is dropped, and the risk heading now opens "Increased fleet costs, either generally" (FY2013 annual report, p. 23).
- **Why it matters:** non-program cars were 95% of the U.S. fleet at the end of 2012 and 91% a year later (83% in 2011), in the year U.S. car disposals went from a 100.6 gain to a 48.2 loss.
- **Reading:** the business weakening: the claim that these cars are cheaper to own was removed as resale values fell. If the change only reflects the mix of the acquired fleet, it is ordinary.

**Adjusted measures: the pricing measure was replaced**
- **Before:** rental rate revenue per day, which left out items "driven by factors beyond the control of management" (FY2012 annual report, p. 49).
- **After:** "Total RPD is calculated as total revenue less revenue from fleet subleases" (FY2013 annual report, p. 49), still described as what management "has the ability to control" (FY2013 annual report, p. 49).
- **Why it matters:** the items now included were 1,676.6 in 2012, or 22.0% of car rental revenue, and the 2012 U.S. figure is shown as $46.33 where last year's report showed $39.07.
- **Reading:** can't tell: the new measure rose 1.4% in the U.S. in 2013, but the old one, which fell 3.1% in 2012, is not given for 2013. The old measure for 2013 would settle it.

**Contingencies: a reason added for the public liability reserve**
- **Before:** not there; the reserve rose 50.7 in 2012, the year of the acquisition, with no reason given (FY2012 annual report, p. 123).
- **After:** "The increase in public liability and property damage reserves was primarily related to Dollar Thrifty" (FY2013 annual report, p. 138).
- **Why it matters:** Dollar Thrifty was already in the 332.2 at the end of 2012; in 2013 the reserve rose 15.5, or 4.7%, against rental days up 19.8%, so the reserve per rental day fell from $2.23 to $1.95.
- **Reading:** can't tell: the reason does not fit the timing, and a reserve growing a quarter as fast as volume may mean lower claims or a thinner reserve. A roll-forward of provisions and claims paid would settle it; none is given.

**Other changes**
- Depreciation: "strong residual values" (FY2012 annual report, p. 113) became "indicative of the residual values" (FY2013 annual report, p. 126), and cars held "4 to 28 months" (FY2012 annual report, p. 81) became "4 to 36 months" (FY2013 annual report, p. 91); see my second reply.
- Prior-year figure: 2012 advertising cost was "$158.0 million" (FY2012 annual report, p. 83) and is now "$183.9 million" (FY2013 annual report, p. 93); the 25.9 difference is the vendor incentives moved in the error correction (my third reply), 16.4% of the figure first reported.
- Lives table: other intangibles went from "3 to 20 years" (FY2012 annual report, p. 81) to "3 to 10 years" (FY2013 annual report, p. 91), yet amortisation outside the acquired concessions was flat (78.9, then 77.0) and two acquired assets still run 16 and 20 years (p. 99).
- Tax: "the likelihood of making material cash tax payments in the near future is low" (FY2012 annual report, p. 69) was dropped (FY2013 annual report, p. 73), though federal loss carry-forwards rose 12.8% to 4,170.2.
- Controls: Dollar Thrifty, 21% of assets, was "excluded from our evaluation" (FY2012 annual report, p. 141) last year; this year it is inside, and the company "implemented Oracle general ledger" (FY2013 annual report, p. 159) in the third quarter.
