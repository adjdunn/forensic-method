**Rental equipment depreciation: the rate fell while car sales turned to losses**
- **The company says:** rates were cut for "optimization of fleet holding periods" (FY2013 annual report, p. 42) and were "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 annual report, p. 42). Last year: different, "strong residual values" (FY2012 annual report, p. 113).
- **Test:** the cuts lowered depreciation by 39.6 in 2013 and 130.1 in 2012 (6% and 29% of pre-tax income), and depreciation before disposal results fell from 16.1% of average gross cost in 2011 to 14.3%. Yet disposals swung from a 96.8 gain to a 37.2 loss on "declining residual values" (FY2013 annual report, p. 126), and U.S. cost per company-operated car rose 3.1%, so the explanation partly holds.
- **Reading:** the reporting stretched: estimates were eased, and the car holding range widened from "4 to 28 months" (FY2012 annual report, p. 81) to "4 to 36 months" (FY2013 annual report, p. 91), in a year when cars sold below book value. Disposal gains returning in 2014 would point to ordinary.
- **Read next:** Note 8 and the revenue earning equipment policy in Note 2 (FY2013 annual report, pp. 126 and 91).

**Receivables: down 19.5% with revenue up 19.4%**
- **The company says:** "a decrease in fleet receivables within our U.S. car rental segment" (FY2013 annual report, p. 133), from the timing of fleet purchases and sales. Last year: nothing.
- **Test:** receivables from fleet sales fell 261.3, which is 71% of the 367.1 fall, and Q4 disposal proceeds fell 30.2%, which fits. Other receivables fell 8.4% unexplained and the cash flow statement shows receivables rising 34.7, leaving 110.3 untraced, so the explanation partly holds.
- **Reading:** ordinary for the most part: fewer cars were sold late in the year, so buyers owed less at year end. The untraced 110.3 turning up in another asset line, or in write-offs (up 37.5% to 40.3), would point elsewhere.
- **Read next:** Schedule II and the non-cash lines under the cash flow statement (FY2013 annual report, pp. 158 and 81); a breakdown of receivables by type is not in the documents.

**Q4 2013 fleet purchases: halved**
- **The company says:** the quarter bore "higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (FY2013 release, p. 2); the annual report offers only "timing of purchases and payments" (FY2013 annual report, p. 66). Last year: different, "the shift from the purchase of program cars to more non-program cars" (FY2012 annual report, p. 61).
- **Test:** on the report's own basis Q4 purchases were 978.6 against 1,928.3 (down 49.3%), and U.S. cars averaged 22.4% more against rental days up 16.1%, which supports extra fleet. But international purchases fell 94.4% with that fleet up 3.6%, and year-end purchase commitments fell 14.9%, so the explanation partly holds.
- **Reading:** the business weakening: the fleet outran demand (U.S. revenue per day fell 1.4% in Q4) and buying was cut across the group. Purchases rebounding in early 2014 with firm pricing would make it timing.
- **Read next:** the capital expenditures table (FY2013 annual report, pp. 65 to 66) and Table 7 of the FY2013 release.

**The other flags**
- Q4 2013 direct operating cost: "lower than expected pricing" (FY2013 release, p. 2) and the extra fleet; held. On the revised basis the ratio was 56.3% against 54.2%, not the mixed-basis 57.6% against 53.9%.
- Prepaid expenses and other assets: "our strategic investment in China Auto Rental" (FY2013 annual report, p. 133); held. Its convertible notes sit in this line at 151.0, which is 66% of the 227.9 rise.

**Adjusted earnings:** adjusted pre-tax income exceeded reported by 490.1 in 2013 (1,153.2 against 663.1) and by 450.9 in 2012 (892.3 against 441.4 as revised; 901.5 against 450.6 as first reported).
What the releases total as "extraordinary, unusual or non-recurring items" (FY2013 release, Table 7, p. 3) came to 283.4, 239.3 and 151.4 in 2013, 2012 and 2011, and restructuring and related charges are in all three years (98.8, 49.1, 66.2).

**Reserve releases:** none is described as a change in estimate; the public liability reserve grew 4.7% (332.2 to 347.7) against U.S. rental days up 26.2%, and the documents give no roll-forward of claims paid to test it.

**Basis:** 2011 and 2012 figures are as revised in the FY2013 annual report unless marked. Q4 2013 figures are the release's, on the revised basis; they replace the derived Q4 figures in my last reply, where rental equipment depreciation becomes 13.5% against 13.8%, not 13.0% against 13.9%.
