**Hertz Global Holdings, Inc.:** fiscal years 2009 to 2013, with Q4 2013 against Q4 2012.
**Documents:** the financial statements spreadsheet, the FY2012 and FY2013 annual reports, and both years' fourth-quarter results releases.

**Top five items to investigate**

**1. Car depreciation was eased as resale results turned**
- **Shown:** rate cuts lowered depreciation by 39.6 in 2013 and 130.1 in 2012 and were called "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 annual report, p. 42), yet the same report puts a swing from a 96.8 disposal gain to a 37.2 loss down to "declining residual values" (FY2013 annual report, p. 126). The car holding range widened from 4 to 28 months to 4 to 36 (p. 91).
- **Reading:** the reporting stretched. Disposal gains returning in 2014 would point to ordinary.
- **Next:** Note 8 and the equipment policy in Note 2 (pp. 126 and 91).

**2. Errors that each lowered income, with controls called effective**
- **Shown:** errors "totaling $46.3 million" (FY2013 annual report, p. 83) cut income in all four periods affected, and at year end a borrowing requirement "was unknowingly not met" (FY2013 annual report, p. 108), yet controls were judged effective (p. 159).
- **Reading:** the reporting stretched. Later corrections running both ways would point to ordinary bookkeeping noise.
- **Next:** Item 9A, then the 2014 quarterly reports that will carry the revised figures.

**3. The public liability reserve grew far slower than rentals**
- **Shown:** the reserve rose 4.7% against rental days up 19.8%, falling from $2.23 to $1.95 per rental day. The report says the rise was "primarily related to Dollar Thrifty" (FY2013 annual report, p. 138), which was already in the opening balance.
- **Reading:** can't tell from these documents: lower claims or a thinner reserve.
- **Next:** ask for a roll-forward of provisions and claims paid; none is given.

**4. Adjusted earnings assume a tax rate below every actual rate**
- **Shown:** adjusted net income uses 35%, the "estimate of our long-term tax rate" (FY2013 release, Exhibit 1, p. unnumbered), against effective rates of 47.8%, 45.9% and 39.9%; at 47.8% it would be 602.0, not 749.6. Items totalled as non-recurring recurred: 283.4, 239.3 and 151.4 in three straight years.
- **Reading:** the reporting stretched, in the adjusted figures only. Cash taxes of 70.9 would support a low rate on a cash view.
- **Next:** the rate reconciliation in Note 9 (p. 128).

**5. The fleet outran demand at the end of 2013**
- **Shown:** Q4 fleet purchases were 978.6 against 1,928.3 (pp. 65 to 66), U.S. cars averaged 22.4% more against rental days up 16.1%, and Q4 direct operating cost was 56.3% of revenue against 54.2%. The car-buying strategy now "remains flexible" (FY2013 annual report, p. 42).
- **Reading:** the business weakening. Purchases rebounding in early 2014 with firm pricing would make it timing.
- **Next:** the capital expenditures table and Table 7 of the FY2013 release.

**Checked and explained:** the 19.5% fall in receivables is mostly fewer car sales late in the year (71% of the fall), the rise in prepaid and other assets is mostly the China Auto Rental notes (66%), and the M-score stayed between -3.25 and -2.72.

**Not examined:** balance-sheet measures before 2010, receivables by type (110.3 of the fall is untraced), claims paid against the reserve, and the old pricing measure for 2013.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
