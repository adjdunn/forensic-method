Assess the accounting in the latest annual report as it stands. Read its notes, management discussion, critical accounting estimates and auditor's report. Don't compare it with last year; that is the next step.

**For each area, find what the report says, then judge where the policy or estimate sits: leans conservative, typical, leans aggressive, or can't tell.**

1. **Revenue recognition:** when revenue is booked (on shipment, on delivery, over time); how much rests on estimates (returns, rebates, discounts, completion); whether sales are reported gross or net; bill-and-hold or consignment terms, rights of return, buy-backs; amounts earned but not yet billed.
2. **Reserves and allowances:** doubtful accounts, returns, warranties, self-insurance, restructuring, litigation. How large each is against the balance or the activity it covers, and its movements during the year (Schedule II, if there is one).
3. **Capitalized costs and useful lives:** what is capitalized that could be expensed (software, interest, commissions, development); useful lives, holding periods and residual values, against the depreciation actually charged.
4. **Impairment and goodwill:** how the tests are done, how much room they show, and any statement that there were "no indicators".
5. **Off the balance sheet:** leases, purchase commitments, guarantees, receivables sold or factored, supplier finance, entities that are not consolidated.
6. **Related parties.**
7. **Contingencies and legal proceedings:** what is accrued, and what is disclosed as possible with or without an amount.
8. **The auditor's report and controls:** the opinion, critical or key audit matters, emphasis paragraphs, material weaknesses.
9. **Adjusted (non-GAAP) measures:** what each leaves out, and whether costs that recur are treated as one-time.

**How to judge.** The yardstick is what the accounting standards require and what the report itself discloses, not the company's own past. Signs that a policy leans aggressive: revenue booked before the customer has the goods or on estimates the report does not support; large estimates with little disclosure; reserves small against the write-offs or claims they cover; long lives or high residual values against the depreciation charged; costs capitalized that most companies expense; material items described without amounts; recurring costs left out of adjusted measures. A policy that is normal for the industry is typical, even if it looks unusual in the abstract. Say what each judgment rests on, and say so when it rests on general practice.

"Leans aggressive" is a judgment about the direction of the choice, and it needs a sign you can quote from the report. Missing or thin disclosure alone is "can't tell: not enough disclosed"; say so, but it is not a direction. Don't let size decide the label: a small item can lean aggressive and a large one can be typical. Size goes in its own column.

**Size** means the effect on the latest year's reported pre-tax income, as a share of that income: the charge not taken, the cost left out, the amount released. A balance (goodwill, inventory, a reserve), a commitment or a possible future loss is not an income effect: give it only if it helps, and label it as a balance or an exposure. Where no income effect can be put on an area, write "not quantified".

**Test what can be tested.** Where a sentence makes a claim a number can test, calculate the number in code and say whether the claim holds. Check each claim against other sentences in the same report too: a report that contradicts itself is the strongest thing you can find. Two figures for the same item that differ slightly (a provision shown as one amount in the cash flow statement and another in a schedule) are a tie-out gap, not a contradicted claim: list them under "Also noted".

**Rules:**
- Use only the attached documents. No outside knowledge of this company or of anything that happened later.
- This step reads one year. Use last year's report only for a figure a calculation needs.
- Do every calculation in code. Don't show the code unless asked.
- Quote exactly, with the document and page. Quote the few words that carry the point, not the whole sentence.
- Before testing a sentence, say exactly what it claims: about what, over what period, by what measure. Test it only with a number that matches all three. If the nearest number covers something wider (a whole segment for one acquisition, a full year for a part year), say the claim can't be tested as stated.
- Never give an amount only as a share of a small net change. Give it as a share of the total as well.
- A reading is a judgment, not a finding. Word it as "most likely" or "possibly", never as a fact.
- Say what each growth figure covers (the whole company, a segment or a region), and keep to one basis.
- These are things to check, not findings. Don't use words like fraud, manipulation or red flag.
- If a section is not in the documents, say so.

**Reply with no introduction. Aim for a page and a half; go longer only when leaving something out would hide something that matters.**

1. **Policy profile:** a table with one row per area: Area | What the report says | Where it sits | Why, in a few words | Size. One line a row. For a row judged typical, give the page only, with no quotation, and keep the reason to a few words. For every other row, quote the few words that carry the point, with the page.

2. **Full write-ups for at most three areas,** in this order: a claim contradicted within the audited statements and notes; then a claim contradicted in the management discussion or an adjusted measure; then areas that lean aggressive. Within each group, largest size first. A write-up is only for a claim that did not hold or an area that leans aggressive, one write-up an area. Fewer than three is fine; if nothing qualifies, say so in one line. The profile judges the policy and its disclosure; the reading below judges what is most likely going on. The two can differ, and when they do, say why. Four lines each, in this layout:

   **[The area: the point, in a few words]**
   - **The report says:** "[quote]" (section or note, page).
   - **Test:** [what the sentence claims, the number that matches it, and whether the claim holds], or "nothing to test". Size: the effect on pre-tax income as a share of it, or the balance or exposure, labelled as such.
   - **Reading:** [the most likely reading: ordinary, the business weakening, the reporting stretched, or can't tell; and why, in one sentence]. [What would point to a different reading, in one sentence.]
   - **Read next:** what would settle it.

3. **Also noted:** at most four more items, one line each, with the page.
4. **Not in the documents:** one line naming any area the documents do not cover.
