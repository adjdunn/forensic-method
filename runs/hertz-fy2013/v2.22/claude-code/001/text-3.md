## 2. Write-ups

**Depreciation: the claim that rate cuts matched residual values does not hold**
- **The report says:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013…" (Note 8, AR p126). The same note says the loss on vehicle sales was "primarily due to… declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles."
- **Test:** The claim is about US car rental, for 2013, measured by realized resale values. If the lower rates matched what cars actually fetched, disposals would show roughly no gain or loss. Instead US disposals lost $48.2m in 2013, after a $100.6m gain in 2012, a swing of $148.8m. The MD&A also blames higher US depreciation partly on "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (AR p54). The claim does not hold for 2013. Size: the rate cuts lowered expense by $44.2m, 6.7% of PTI; the disposal loss was $48.2m, 7.3%.
  - A related MD&A claim does hold: US monthly depreciation per car fell, from $218 to $216 on the reported line (AR p42).
- **Reading:** Possibly stretched reporting. The rates were cut while sales showed book values above market, which suggests depreciation was set too low for the cars sold. A different reading is timing: the note says the cuts came "from previous quarters," so they may have been made in the first half, before used-car prices fell late in the year.
- **Read next:** Rate changes and disposal results by quarter in the 2013 10-Qs, and whether rates were raised again in Q1 2014. The release itself flags "residual value risks" in 2014 guidance (ER p6).

**Adjusted pre-tax income: restructuring treated as one-time but charged every year**
- **The report says:** Adjusted pre-tax income excludes "certain one-time charges and nonoperational items" (MD&A, AR p50). The ER labels part of its reconciliation "Other unusual/non-recurring" (ER p9).
- **Test:** The claim is that the excluded charges are one-time. The report's own figures contradict it:
  - Restructuring was charged in each of 2011–2013: $56.4m, $38.0m and $77.0m (AR p50).
  - It totals $645.4m from 2007 to 2013, about $92.2m a year (Note 14, AR p140).
  - The note adds that "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed" (p140).

  The claim does not hold for restructuring. Size: restructuring plus "restructuring related" costs of $98.8m are 14.9% of PTI. All add-backs are $490.1m, 73.9% of PTI.
  - Separately, the $65.0m of Advantage sublease revenue stays in adjusted income, while the $44.0m Simply Wheelz impairment tied to the same counterparty is removed (AR p50–51).
- **Reading:** Most likely stretched reporting. A cost that has recurred for seven years is ongoing, so the adjusted measure most likely overstates underlying earnings. A different reading would hold if the 2013 charges were mostly the one-off HQ move and the Dollar Thrifty integration ($21.9m of relocation sits inside restructuring).
- **Read next:** 2014 filings, to see whether restructuring continues and is still added back.

## 3. Also noted
- **Tie-out gap:** the out-of-period errors total $46.3m in the AR (p83) but "$47.9 million" in the ER (p9). The part falling in 2013, $11.6m, matches the revised nine-month table (AR p87).
- **Errors and controls:** errors found in Q4 go back to 2010. They include vendor incentives booked as "a reduction of marketing expenses instead of reducing the cost of revenue earning equipment" and Brazil bad-debt allowances (AR p83). Even so, internal control is reported effective with no deficiency discussed (AR p159).
- **Goodwill reporting units described two ways:** Note 2 says testing is at "one level below our operating segments" (AR p93). The MD&A lists five reporting units that are the operating segments themselves (AR p46, p83).
- **Untestable claim on the liability reserve:** the increase in the self-insured liability is said to be "primarily related to Dollar Thrifty" (AR p138). Dollar Thrifty was already in the December 2012 balance, and no breakdown is given.

There are no critical audit matters, no roll-forward of the self-insured liability, no warranty reserve, no amounts for capitalized interest or software, and nothing on factoring or supplier finance.
