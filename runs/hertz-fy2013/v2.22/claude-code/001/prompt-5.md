**First gather the candidates.** Go through all four replies, including rows in tables and one-line items, and collect:
- every claim whose test did not hold, only partly held or could not be tested as stated;
- every area judged to lean aggressive;
- every flag the company did not explain;
- every statement left the same that no longer fits, and every change in wording or estimate written up;
- every item with a reading other than ordinary, including "can't tell".

Merge items about the same account or the same estimate into one, and keep all of its evidence: a statement that stayed the same while its number moved belongs with the estimate it describes, and a flag from the numbers track belongs with the note that explains it in the text track.

**Carry over the size and the reading.** Each earlier reply gives a size on the same base: the effect on the latest year's reported pre-tax income, as a share of that income, with balances and exposures labelled as such. Take each item's size from the reply that gave it; calculate the share in code only where a reply gives the amount without the share. Where two replies size the same account differently, give both and say which is the income effect. Rank on the larger income effect; a balance or exposure never ranks the item. Where no amount was put on an item, write "not quantified". Each earlier reply also chose a reading. Use it; don't choose again. Where the two tracks read the same account differently, give both readings and say so: that disagreement is itself worth the analyst's time.

**Rank the items by strength of evidence first, then by size within each level.**

Strength of evidence, strongest first:
1. A sentence contradicted by the company's own numbers, or by another sentence in the same report.
2. Two or more signals pointing at the same account in the same period, from either track.
3. A single measure, a single change in wording, or a single policy that leans aggressive, out of line with the company's history or with general practice.

Within a level: first the items whose size is an effect on reported income, largest first; then the items whose size is a balance or an exposure, largest first; then the unquantified items. A balance never outranks an income effect at the same level, however large. Never move an item across levels on size alone. Where an item at a lower level is much larger than one above it, say so in its Shown line; the reader does the trading off.

**Structure:**
- **Header:** two lines: the company and periods, and the documents used.
- **Items to investigate, up to five.** Fewer is fine; if nothing qualifies, say so in one line. Three lines each:
  - **Shown:** what the filings show, in one or two sentences, with page references; the size, labelled; and the evidence level.
  - **Reading:** the reading from the earlier reply (ordinary, the business weakening, the reporting stretched, or can't tell from these documents) and what would change it.
  - **Next:** what to read or ask to settle it.
- **Also noted:** one or two lines listing the candidates that did not make the list and were not explained.
- **Checked and explained:** one or two lines listing items that looked unusual but have an ordinary explanation that held when tested.
- **Not examined:** one line on what the documents did not allow.
- **Closing line:** "Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's."

**Rules:**
- Use only what is in the attached documents, the earlier answers in this conversation and the pasted replies. Add no new numbers, except a share of pre-tax income where a reply gave only the amount.
- A reading is a judgment, not a finding. Word it as "most likely" or "possibly", never as a fact.
- State nothing more strongly than the evidence behind it.
- An item can matter without any misreporting: a weakening business is a finding too.
- Don't use words like fraud, manipulation or red flag in your own sentences.
- Keep it to about one page. Merge items only when they concern the same account or the same estimate; anything else that misses the list goes under "Also noted".

**Payables fell behind costs: days payable 52.9 → 42.7 (52.7 as first reported)**
- **The company says:** nothing about the payables balance. The only related words are in the operating cash flow discussion: "as well as due to the timing of our payments" (FY2013 annual report, p.64). Last year: the same sentence (FY2012 annual report, p.60).
- **Test:** the claim covers the whole company's $879.9m rise in operating cash flow, 2013 against 2012. The payables line in the cash flow statement added $23.2m in 2013 against $34.0m in 2012, so it doesn't support the claim. Payables, accrued liabilities and accrued taxes together explain $39.7m, or 4.5% of the rise. On the balance sheet, payables fell 3.5% while cost of sales rose 19.4%. Payables for fleet and equipment purchases rose by $62.7m, so the rest of payables fell further: at most from $721.2m to $623.2m, or 13.6%. Dollar Thrifty brought only $43m of payables, in 2012. The company doesn't explain the movement.
- **Reading:** can't tell from these documents. There is no note on what payables contain, and the cash flow line (+$23.2m) points the opposite way from the balance (−$35.3m). If ordinary trade payables were paid faster, that would mean the business is simply paying sooner. A reclassification would also explain it; the only sign of one is the move to a new Oracle accounts payable system in Q3 2013 (p.159), and the company doesn't link the two.
- **Read next:** a breakdown of accounts payable and accrued liabilities (not in these documents), and the Q3 2013 10-Q.

**Rental equipment depreciation rate fell: 15.2% → 14.3% revised (15.3% → 14.2% first reported)**
- **The company says:** "favorable adjustments reflect changes from the impact of car sales channel diversification … optimization of fleet holding periods" (FY2013 annual report, p.42 and p.126). Last year it gave a different reason: "indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012 annual report, p.113).
- **Test:**
  - *Rate changes:* the U.S. rate changes cut depreciation by $44.2m in 2013. That is 29% of the $152.1m charge not taken at the 2012 rate (revised; $174.5m first reported).
  - *Per-car cost:* the claim that U.S. monthly depreciation per vehicle fell holds. Excluding results on disposal, it fell 14.1% ($241.6 to $207.7 per car per month).
  - *Disposal losses:* U.S. car disposals went from gains of $100.6m to losses of $48.2m, a $148.8m swing. Adding back the disposal adjustment leaves the overall rate flat (14.48% → 14.49%).
  - *Same report:* other pages cite "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54) and "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles" (p.126).
  - *Mix:* the non-program share fell from 95% to 91% (p.42). That mix shift should raise depreciation, not lower it.
  - Verdict: partly holds.
- **Reading:** possibly the reporting is stretched. Residual values were assumed higher in the same year that cars sold at a loss, and a year earlier the company had forecast U.S. depreciation per vehicle down "no less than 4% to 5%" (FY2012 release, p.5). A different reading: the losses came from cars bought under the old rates, and Dollar Thrifty's longer holding periods could justify the lower rates.
- **Read next:** the quarterly rate changes and disposal results by segment for 2013 (the annual report gives annual totals only).

**Q4 gross margin fell: 22.1% → 18.5% (release basis)**
- **The company says:** "estimated $0.12 impact of lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (FY2013 release, p.2). Last year it was different: "strong residual values and strong cost management performance, partially offset by negative RPD" (FY2012 release, p.3).
- **Test:**
  - *As stated:* the claim measures Q4 2013 against the company's own expectations, in earnings per share. It can't be tested as stated against the year-on-year fall. For scale, $0.12 is about $85.7m pre-tax.
  - *Year on year, U.S. car rental:* utilisation fell from 79.8% to 75.7%. Fleet depreciation rose 35.5% against 16.1% growth in rental days, an excess of $47.8m. Revenue per day fell 1.4%, worth about $21.0m.
  - *Coverage:* together that is $68.8m, 76% of the $90.2m fall. Restructuring charges within direct operating costs rose $17.5m.
  - Verdict: partly holds.
- **Reading:** most likely the business weakened: lower U.S. pricing and a fleet larger than demand. If the full-year margin also turns in 2014, that would point to something structural rather than one quarter.
- **Read next:** U.S. car rental results in the next quarterly report, and fleet size against rental days.
