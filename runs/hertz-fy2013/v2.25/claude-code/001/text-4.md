## 1. Write-ups

**Depreciation of rental cars: longer holding periods, lower depreciation rates, and "strong" dropped from the resale-value sentence**
- **Before:** the car holding periods table reads "Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012 10-K, p.81). The resale-value sentence reads "was indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (p.113).
- **After:** "Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013 10-K, p.91). The same sentence now reads "was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S.", without "strong". A new reason is given for the rate cuts: "the optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" (p.126). The critical estimates add "industry residual value guidebooks" and "Depreciation rates are adjusted prospectively" (p.45).
- **Why it matters:** The top of the car holding-period range rose 29%. This change is not disclosed as a change in estimate. The U.S. rate cuts it now helps explain lowered 2013 depreciation by $44.2m. Over the same years, U.S. car disposals went from a net gain of $100.6m to a net loss of $48.2m, a $148.8m swing (22.4% of pre-tax). Depreciation, before disposal adjustments, fell from 15.2% to 14.3% of average gross rental equipment (cars and equipment combined). Size: the stated $44.2m = **6.7% of pre-tax**. The report does not split out how much comes from longer holding periods.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting stretched. The assumed holding periods were lengthened and the rate cuts kept, while realized resale prices fell; dropping "strong" admits the weaker prices without changing the conclusion. If the 36-month top covers only a small group of vehicles (for example Dollar Thrifty cars or leased fleet), the effect on the average rate could be small and the change ordinary.

**Adjusted measures: more exclusions, and "Other" relabelled as non-recurring**
- **Before:** the 2012 release labels the $44.0m line "Other (g)" (FY2012 release, Table 5, p.14). The FY2012 10-K calls the debt line "non-cash debt charges" (p.50).
- **After:** the same 2012 $44.0m now sits on a line called "Other unusual/non-recurring (c)". That line carries $50.7m again in 2013 (FY2013 release, Table 6, unnumbered page). The debt line is now "Debt-related charges" (10-K p.50). New 2013 exclusions: integration expenses $40.0m, relocation costs $7.8m, and "Impairment charges and other" $44.0m. Premiums paid on debt ($28.7m) are excluded again; there were none in 2012.
- **Why it matters:** The items adjusted pre-tax income leaves out in 2013 but not in 2012 total **$171.2m = 25.8% of pre-tax**. This counts the new "Other" content: the loss on converting the convertible notes, against 2012's pension withdrawal and Hurricane Sandy costs. Excluding premiums and "Other", the new categories alone are $91.8m (13.8%). The prior-year adjusted figures were also restated: adjusted pre-tax income from $901.5m to $892.3m, and Corporate EBITDA from $1,635.6m to $1,626.4m, both down $9.2m because of the error corrections.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting stretched. A line that carries an amount in both years is now labelled non-recurring, and Dollar Thrifty costs moved from "acquisition related" to a new "integration" line rather than stopping. If integration and relocation end in 2014 as the projects finish, these exclusions would look ordinary.

**Legal proceedings: an amount and a "continue to believe" appear in the Sobel case; a new securities suit**
- **Before:** the Sobel case is described with no amount and no view on materiality: "The court has since entered a stay order and the parties will again be engaging in mediation" (FY2012 10-K, p.32 and p.122).
- **After:** "A judgment - which could potentially exceed $40.0 million" and "We continue to believe the outcome of this case will not be material" (FY2013 10-K, p.137). A new shareholder class action alleges "material misrepresentations and/or omissions" between February 25 and November 4, 2013 (p.138).
- **Why it matters:** The court granted the plaintiffs partial summary judgment on restitution in March 2013. The report still does not say whether anything is accrued. Size: **exposure of $40.0m = 6.0% of pre-tax**; no income effect is stated. The securities suit is not quantified.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. "Continue to believe" refers to a view the prior report never stated for this case, so the amount is new disclosure, not new comfort. If the March 2014 mediation settles near $40m without a charge, the reading would shift to a reserve set too low.

## 2. Other changes
- **Intangible and software lives:** the table range for other intangibles narrowed from "3 to 20 years" (FY2012 p.81) to "3 to 10 years" (FY2013 p.91), and software from "1 to 15" to "1 to 10". Donlen's 16- and 20-year lives were unchanged (p.89 and p.99). This narrowing created the contradiction in my last reply; the effect is under 0.1% of pre-tax.
- **Prior-year figures restated:**
  - 2012 pre-tax income moved from $450.6m (FY2012 p.50) to $441.4m (FY2013 p.84).
  - 2012 year-end doubtful-account allowance moved from $25.1m to $29.3m, and its charge from $34.1m to $38.3m (Schedule II, FY2012 p.140 and FY2013 p.158). This ties to the Brazil correction.
- **Restructuring roll-forward for 2012 recast:** cash payments rose from $(26.8)m to $(36.8)m, and "Other" fell from $(11.8)m to $(1.8)m. The footnote changed from "$10.3 million for facility closures" to "$0.5 million related to a goodwill write-off" (FY2012 p.126; FY2013 p.141). There is no income effect.
- **Controls:**
  - The Dollar Thrifty exclusion from the controls assessment was dropped (FY2012 p.71 and p.141).
  - A new sentence says Oracle general ledger and accounts payable systems were implemented in Q3 2013 (FY2013 p.159). The out-of-period errors were found the next quarter (p.83), and no material weakness is reported.
- **Risk factors:**
  - The heading changed from "Declines in the value of the non-program cars … and declines in the overall number of program cars" (FY2012 p.22) to "Increased fleet costs, either generally or due to declines in the value of the non-program cars" (FY2013 p.23).
  - "We expect this percentage to continue to decrease" (about program cars) was dropped. This fits the numbers: U.S. non-program cars fell from 95% to 91% of the fleet.
  - A new Simply Wheelz risk notes "approximately 9,000 of our vehicles" still in its possession (p.24).
