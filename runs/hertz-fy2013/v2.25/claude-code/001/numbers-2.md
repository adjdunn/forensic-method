**Payables: above the industry level, then falling against costs** *(flags 1 and 3 together, since both are about the same balance)*
- **The company says:** nothing about the payables balance, either about its level or about its fall. The nearest sentence is about operating cash flow, not payables: "due to the timing of our payments" (FY2013 annual report, p.64). It said the same a year earlier (FY2012 annual report, p.60).
- **Test:** on the revised basis, days payable fell from 52.9 to 42.7. The first-reported figure was 52.7. At last year's days, payables would be $232.8m higher (first reported: $226.3m), which is 35.1% of pre-tax income.
  - Dollar Thrifty brought in only $43m of payables (p.98), 4.3% of the year-end 2012 balance. Leaving it out of 2012, it covers 22% of the shortfall.
  - The other explanation available is fleet and capex purchases sitting in payables ($289.1m for fleet, held with accrued liabilities, and $55.6m for capex, p.81). Removing them all leaves payables at 5.8% of sales in 2013, down from 8.0% in 2012 and still above the industry's 4.5%.
  - The balance fell $35.3m, but the cash flow line shows payables adding $23.2m to operating cash. Nothing in the reports reconciles the $58.5m difference.
  - So the acquisition partly explains the fall, and nothing explains the level.
- **Reading:** can't tell from these documents. The balance mixes fleet, capex and operating payables, and the reports never separate them. Signs of the business weakening would be a breakdown showing operating payables shrinking because suppliers tightened terms. Signs of the reporting being stretched would be a breakdown showing payables shifted into accrued liabilities, which rose from $1,180.5m (revised) to $1,104.7m… no: accrued liabilities were $1,180.5m revised at end-2012 and $1,104.7m at end-2013.
- **Read next:** a split of payables into fleet, capex and trade. It is not in these documents.

**Depreciation rate on rental equipment fell**
- **The company says:** "U.S. monthly per vehicle depreciation costs decreased … due to mix optimization, improved procurement and remarketing efforts" (FY2013 annual report, p.42). It also discloses rate changes that cut U.S. depreciation by $44.2m (p.42). Last year the reason was different: "improved residual values in the U.S." (FY2012 annual report, p.42). Residual values have dropped out of the 2013 reason.
- **Test:** on the revised basis the rate fell from 15.2% to 14.3%, worth $152.1m (first reported: $174.5m), or 22.9% of pre-tax income.
  - The disclosed rate changes, net of international (+$5.0m) and equipment rental (−$0.4m), come to $39.6m, which covers 26% of the shortfall.
  - The per-vehicle claim can't be tested as stated, because no per-vehicle figure is given. The nearest measure is wider (U.S. depreciation and lease charges per average company-operated car), and it rose 2.9%.
  - The same report says U.S. depreciation rose on "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54). U.S. car disposals swung from a $100.6m gain to a $48.2m loss (p.126).
  - Once the disposal adjustments are added back, the rate is flat at 14.5% in both years.
  - So the explanation partly holds.
- **Reading:** possibly the reporting stretched. Rates were cut while cars were selling below book value, and the shortfall came back through disposal losses. If the 2013 losses came from one-off disposal timing rather than residual values, the reading would be ordinary.
- **Read next:** Note 8 (p.126) and the residual-value discussion in the critical accounting estimates (p.45). For a fuller test, 2014 disposal results, which these documents don't include.

**Q4 gross margin fell (22.1% to 18.5%)**
- **The company says:** "lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (Q4 FY2013 release, p.2). The Q4 FY2012 release gave no comparable explanation.
- **Test:** the claim covers the whole company, Q4 2013, and is measured against what the company expected, not against the prior year. The quoted $0.12 per share is after tax. So the claim can't be tested as stated.
  - The pieces that can be measured: the fall in U.S. revenue per day (−1.4%) is worth $20.7m, 23% of the $90.2m fall. Adjusted items in direct operating costs rose $19.4m, another 22%.
  - U.S. fleet grew 22.4% against 16.1% more transaction days, so utilization fell 5.1%. The cost of that extra fleet is not given.
  - So the explanation partly holds, for about 45% of the fall.
- **Reading:** most likely the business weakening, on pricing and fleet utilization. Restructuring charges making up the unexplained part would point to an ordinary reading.
- **Read next:** Q4 segment costs (release, Table 5) and the restructuring note.

**The other flag**
- **Receivables above the industry level:** "decrease in fleet receivables … timing of purchases and sales" (FY2013 annual report, p.133). This holds.
  - Amounts owed on fleet sales fell $261.3m, which is 71% of the $367.1m fall in receivables (revised basis), and those amounts are 24% of 2013 receivables.
  - Without them, receivables are 10.7% of sales, below the industry's 11.5%.
  - Most likely ordinary.

**Adjusted earnings**
- The gap between pre-tax income and adjusted pre-tax income was $356.2m in 2011, $450.9m in 2012 (revised) and $490.1m in 2013. That is 105%, 102% and 74% of reported pre-tax income, or 52%, 51% and 42% of adjusted. At the net income level the gap was $403.4m in 2013 and $350.3m in 2012.
- Items labelled "non-recurring" appear in every year:
  - restructuring plus related charges: $66.2m in 2011, $49.1m in 2012, $98.8m in 2013;
  - "Other unusual/non-recurring": $62.4m in 2011, $44.0m in 2012, $50.7m in 2013 (FY2012 release p.17; Q4 FY2013 release, Table 6).

**Changes in estimate that lifted income**
- 2013: net depreciation rate cuts of $39.6m, which is 6.0% of pre-tax income.
- 2012: net cuts of $130.1m.
- The 2013 change is not supported by the numbers around it. The same report describes deteriorating residual values and a $48.2m loss on U.S. car sales.
