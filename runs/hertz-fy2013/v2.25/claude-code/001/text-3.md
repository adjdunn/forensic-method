## 2. Write-ups

**Depreciation of rental cars: lower rates not matched by resale results**
- **The report says:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013…" (Note 8, p.126; also MD&A p.42).
- **Test:** The claim is that U.S. car rental resale values realized in 2013 supported the lower depreciation rates. The matching figure is in the same note. U.S. disposals in 2013 produced "net losses of $48.2 million," which the note attributes "primarily" to "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles." The MD&A also cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54). Lower rates would need resale prices at or above book value; losses mean they were below it. **The claim does not hold for 2013.** Size: the rate cuts lowered depreciation by $44.2m, or 6.7% of pre-tax. The disposal losses of $48.2m (7.3%) are already in the income statement.
- **Reading:** Most likely the business weakening: used-car prices fell during the year, and rate cuts made in earlier quarters were not reversed fast enough. The policy row still leans aggressive because the report kept the cuts and described them as supported by resale values. If the cuts were all made early in 2013 and the losses came only in the fourth quarter, this would be an ordinary timing lag.
- **Read next:** The quarterly split of the $44.2m and the $48.2m (the 2013 10-Qs), and the rate changes in the first 2014 10-Q.

**Intangible-asset lives: the stated range does not cover the actual lives**
- **The report says:** finite-lived intangibles are amortized over lives "which range from two to fifteen years" (Note 2, p.93); the property table says "Other intangible assets | 3 to 10 years" (p.91).
- **Test:** The claim covers finite-lived intangibles held at the reporting date. Donlen's customer relationships use **16 years** and its trademark **20 years** (Note 4, p.99), so neither stated range holds. Size: amortizing these over 15 years instead would add about $0.4m a year, or 0.06% of pre-tax.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary: policy text not updated, with a negligible effect. If other acquired intangibles also carry lives beyond 15 years, the range would understate more than this one case.
- **Read next:** A schedule of remaining lives by intangible class (the concession rights use nine years, per p.98).

**Adjusted pre-tax income: restructuring treated as one-time**
- **The report says:** the measure adds back "certain one-time charges and nonoperational items" (MD&A p.50).
- **Test:** The claim is that the excluded charges are one-time. Restructuring was excluded in each of 2011, 2012 and 2013 ($56.4m, $38.0m, $77.0m). Note 14 shows $645.4m of restructuring from 2007 to 2013, an average of $92.2m a year, and says "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed" (p.140). The release's "Other unusual/non-recurring" line is also excluded in both years shown ($44.0m in 2012, $50.7m in 2013; release Table 6). **The claim does not hold for restructuring.** Size: $98.8m of restructuring and related charges = 14.9% of pre-tax. All add-backs total $490.1m = 73.9%.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting stretched. A seven-year recurring cost is presented as outside operations, although the reconciliation to GAAP is complete. If the headquarters move and the Dollar Thrifty integration end in 2014 and charges then stop, the one-time label would hold better going forward.
- **Read next:** The 2014 reconciliations, and whether restructuring and "other unusual" charges recur.

## 3. Also noted
- **Out-of-period errors:** $46.3m of errors were found in Q4 2013. Of this, $11.6m (1.7% of pre-tax) fell in 2013, including vendor incentives booked against marketing expense instead of fleet cost. The report still states no material weakness (p.83–84, p.159).
- **Tie-out gaps:**
  - The doubtful-account provision is $45.9m in the cash flow (p.80) but $41.9m "charged to expense" in Schedule II (p.158).
  - "Impairment charges and other" is $40.0m in the cash flow (p.80) but $44.0m in the adjusted reconciliation (p.50).
- **International resale values:** the MD&A cites "slight strengthening of used vehicle residual values" (p.43), yet international rate changes raised depreciation by $5.0m and international disposals lost $15.2m (p.126).
- **Simply Wheelz:** the Advantage buyer filed for bankruptcy in November 2013, leading to a $44.0m charge (p.56, p.101). It still uses Hertz vehicles under a settlement.

## 4. Not in the documents
The report has no critical audit matters, which were not required for reports of this date (general practice). It gives no amounts for capitalized interest or capitalized software. It has no disclosure of receivables sales or supplier finance, and no warranty or returns reserves. Schedule II covers only doubtful accounts and tax valuation allowances. Item 13 on related parties is incorporated from the proxy statement, which is not attached.
