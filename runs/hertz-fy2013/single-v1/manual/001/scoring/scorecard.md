**Reading of the experiment.** On this pack one prompt reaches the pass bar with the same quotations and pages the five reach, in 41% of the words and one round trip, and its arithmetic is as broad and nearly as clean. What it loses is the middle layer: the write-ups where a tested sentence is set beside its number before the merge. Without that layer the merge absorbs rows by number instead of by words (PR54), a quoted reason gets cut at the clause that mattered (PR55), the company's own holding-period attribution never enters the reply, and the second-tier level-1 candidates are reclassified downward to make room for level-3 items. The five-prompt set's extra 3,600 words buy the holding-period sentences in the report's own text, the acquisition on every 2012 base, and a third level-1 item; the single prompt's compression buys the Q4 column and the M-score components. Whether that trade is worth making depends on what the analyst reads: the items alone (where the five-prompt report is stronger) or the whole reply (where the single pass gives the same evidence on one page, less three sentences).
