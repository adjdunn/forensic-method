4. Items to investigate
(1) Depreciation rates and residual values of the car ﬂeet
Shown. In 2013 depreciation rates were cut again (“net decreases of $44.2 million, $139.4 million
and $26.7 million” in 2013, 2012, 2011; 2013 10-K p.126 and p.42) while disposals of U.S. cars
swung from a $100.6M net gain to “net losses of $48.2 million”, and MD&A attributes higher U.S.
depreciation to “a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values” (p.54). The same note
nonetheless keeps the sentence that the rate reductions were “also indicative of the residual values
experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013” (p.126); in 2012 it read “indicative
of the strong residual values” (2012 p.113). The car holding-period range was lengthened from “4
to 28 months” (2012 p.81) to “4 to 36 months” (2013 p.91) with no eﬀect stated, and a further
$40.0M impairment was taken on about 20,000 cars subleased to Advantage (p.146). Size: the
income eﬀect ranks on the company’s stated 2013 rate reduction, $44.2M net of the $5.0M
international increase = $39.6M, 6.0% of 2013 pre-tax income; the computed per-car charge not
taken at the 2012 gross rate is about $139M (U.S.), 21% of pre-tax income; exposure: $48.2M loss
plus $40.0M impairment, 13.3%; cumulative U.S. rate reductions 2011 to 2013 $210.3M.
Persistence: the rate fell three consecutive years (16.2%, 15.3%, 14.3%). Level 1, audited notes
(sentence and numbers in the same Note 8). Absorbed: the Part A rate ﬂag; the explanation test
(the stated reasons “car sales channel diversiﬁcation, acceleration of our retail sales expansion”
should raise residuals, yet the realised result was a loss, so the explanation does not hold); Part C
capitalized costs (leans aggressive); Part D rows 1 to 5 above and the dropped 2012 sentence “With
fewer program cars in our ﬂeet, we have an increased risk that the market value of a car at the
time of its disposition will be less than its estimated residual value” (2012 p.41, absent in 2013).
Reading. Most likely the reporting is stretched on this estimate; possibly the business weakening
(used-car prices fell in late 2013 and the company says it carried “extra ﬂeet”, release p.2). A 2014
disposal result near break-even and a stated eﬀect for the holding-period change would point back
to ordinary.
Next. Ask for depreciation rate changes by quarter in 2013, the gain or loss per car by quarter, the
average age and holding period of Dollar Thrifty versus Hertz cars, and why the 36-month range
was adopted.
(2) Adjusted measures treat recurring and ﬂeet-related charges as one-time
Shown. Adjusted pre-tax income is “income before income taxes plus certain non-cash purchase
accounting charges, debt-related charges … and certain one-time charges and nonoperational
items” (p.50), yet restructuring charges have been recorded every year from 2007 ($568.4M
through 2012, 2012 p.124, plus $77.0M in 2013, p.44); “litigation accrual” is excluded in both years
($14.0M in 2012, $5.7M in 2013, release p.5 note g); the $44.0M “Impairment charges and other”
on rental cars is excluded (p.50); the assumed tax rate is 35% against an actual 47.8% (p.57); and
“free cash ﬂow” adds “net worldwide car rental ﬂeet ﬁnancing” of $133.9M (release Table 7), so
without ﬂeet borrowing it is $314.8M not $448.7M. The 2012 deﬁnition said “non-cash debt
charges”; 2013 drops “non-cash” (2012 p.50 / 2013 p.50). Size: the gap from reported to adjusted
pre-tax income is $490.1M in 2013 (74% of pre-tax income) and $450.9M in 2012 (102%), not an
income eﬀect; the item ranks on restructuring and related charges excluded, $98.8M, 14.9% of pre-
tax income. Level 1, second group (an adjusted measure: “one-time” contradicted by seven
consecutive years of the charge). Absorbed: the Part B reported-versus-adjusted test; Part C
adjusted measures (leans aggressive).
Reading. Most likely ordinary presentation for the sector, but the measures overstate recurring
earnings; this would change if restructuring ceased in 2014.
Next. Rebuild adjusted pre-tax income adding back only purchase accounting and debt-cost
amortisation.
(3) Out-of-period errors of $46.3M found in Q4 2013, with controls declared eﬀective
Shown. “we identiﬁed certain out of period errors totaling $46.3 million, of which $34.7 million
($21.0 million, net of tax) related to” 2012, 2011 and prior (p.83): vendor incentives booked against
marketing instead of car cost, “charges related to certain assets and allowances for doubtful
accounts in Brazil”, “under accruals”, and “certain reclassiﬁcations of $23.1 million” (p.87). The
remaining $11.6M was moved between 2013 quarters (Q3 2013 pre-tax cut $7.0M, p.87).
Management and PwC conclude internal control was “eﬀective as of December 31, 2013” (p.159,
p.74); an Oracle general ledger was implemented in Q3 2013 (p.159); a securities class action
covers disclosures from “February 25, 2013 through November 4, 2013” (p.138). Size: 2013 full-
year pre-tax income is $34.7M (5.2%) higher than if the prior-period errors had been booked in
2013; the vendor-incentive correction lowered the 2013 nine-month depreciation line by $22.1M
and raised SG&A by $21.2M. Level 2 (errors in the allowance and ﬂeet-cost accounts, the same
accounts as items 1 and 4, in the same period). Absorbed: Part C auditor and controls (can’t tell);
the Brazil allowance understatement ($3.6M 2012, $6.2M 2011).
Reading. Can’t tell from these documents; possibly reporting stretched if further errors surface in
2014; ordinary if none do.
Next. Read the 2014 10-Qs for further revisions and any change in the Item 9A conclusion.
(4) Concession-fee litigation (Sobel) and legal reserves
Shown. The court “granted, in part, the plaintiﬀs’ motion for partial summary judgment with
respect to restitution and granted the plaintiﬀs’ motion for class certiﬁcation” and “A judgment -
which could potentially exceed $40.0 million - has still not been issued”, while “We continue to
believe the outcome of this case will not be material” (p.137); whether a reserve exists for this case
is not stated, and the only litigation accrual quantiﬁed is the $5.7M excluded from adjusted results.
In 2012 the case was described as stayed for mediation with no amount (2012 p.122). Size:
exposure $40M plus, 6.0% of pre-tax income; unquantiﬁed accrual. Level 3. Absorbed: Part C
contingencies (leans aggressive on this case).
Reading. Possibly ordinary (mediation in March 2014); would change if a judgment is entered
above the accrual.
Next. Ask what is accrued for Sobel and read the Q1 2014 10-Q.
(5) Accrued liabilities fell while revenue grew 19%, unexplained
Shown. Accrued liabilities 1,163.1 (revised) to 1,104.7 (p.75), 12.9% to 10.3% of revenue, yet the
cash ﬂow statement shows accrued liabilities as a $25.2M source (p.80), a gap of $83.6M between
the two statements that the report does not explain; the company says nothing about the balance
beyond “timing of our payments” (p.64). Size: the balance less what it would be at the prior year’s
share of revenue is $284M, 43% of pre-tax income (a balance exposure, not an income eﬀect).
Persistence: the share fell two consecutive years. Level 3, unexplained.
Reading. Can’t tell; possibly ordinary (ﬂeet-purchase accruals move through investing), possibly
under-accrual given the “under accruals” cited in the error correction (p.87).
Next. Ask for the components of accrued liabilities at both dates.
5. Also noted
DSO fell from 76 to 51 days: $260M of the $367M fall is manufacturer receivables for car sales
(“Sales of revenue earning equipment included in receivables” 617.3 to 357.3, p.81), operating
receivables actually rose $34.7M, and $72M is unexplained; no income eﬀect. Payables at 9.0% of
sales sit at twice the industry 4.5% ($483M above, 73% of pre-tax income) but include $289.1M of
ﬂeet purchases and fell 10 days in 2013. Q4 2013 gross margin fell 3.5 points ($90M, 13.6% of pre-
tax income); the release’s “$0.12” per-share impact of pricing and “extra ﬂeet” (p.2) is about $86M
pre-tax, so it largely holds. Tie-out gaps: provision for doubtful accounts 45.9 (cash ﬂow, p.80) vs
41.9 charged to expense (Schedule II, p.158); 2012 gains on P&E disposals $6.3M (2012 p.81) vs
$8.3M (cash ﬂow and 2013 p.91); three ranges for intangible lives; Q4 2012 operating cash ﬂow
600.5 (release) vs 578.5 implied by the revised annual and nine-month ﬁgures. Reworded:
multiemployer plans “have underfunded liabilities” (2012 p.25) became “could have signiﬁcant
underfunded liabilities” (2013 p.26); dropped: “We are not a party to any long-term car supply
arrangements with manufacturers” (2012 p.22).
6. Checked and explained
The public liability reserve grew 4.7% against car-rental revenue up 21.6%, but its share of car-
rental revenue (3.99%) is back at the 2011 level (4.06%) after Dollar Thrifty’s reserve arrived with
43 days of revenue in 2012; at the 2011 share the shortfall is $5M. Prepaid and other assets
+$228M is mostly the China Auto Rental notes and shares ($151M Level 3, p.146). Q4 2012 SG&A
of $331.1M carried $126.4M of acquisition costs, so the Q4 operating-margin rise is not operating.
The allowance for doubtful accounts rose to 2.0% of gross receivables with the provision roughly
equal to write-oﬀs (41.9 vs 40.3). The spreadsheet’s mixed-basis Q4 2013 pre-tax of 50.7 diﬀers
from the release’s 62.3 by exactly the $11.6M moved into earlier 2013 quarters.
7. Not examined
No receivables ageing, no claims development table for the $347.7M public liability reserve, no
gross ﬂeet by segment, no goodwill or trade-name headroom (ﬁve reporting units, $3,330M trade
name, “did not exceed their estimated fair values”, p.94 and p.96), no quarterly balance sheet in
the release, and the proxy statement with related-party detail is not attached.
Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst’s.
