Hertz annual reports for FY2012 (filed 4 March 2013) and FY2013 (filed 19 March 2014), plus the two Q4 earnings releases. Both reports are complete 10-Ks, so every section asked for exists in both years. FY2013 pre-tax income is **$663.1M**, and every size below is a share of it.

## 1. Write-ups

**Depreciation and lives: the longest car holding period was stretched, with a new reason given for cutting depreciation (an estimate change that lifted income)**
- **Before:** "Useful lives are as follows: Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012 10-K, Note 2, p.81). The 2012 rate cuts were tied only to residual values: "indicative of the strong residual values experienced" (Note 8, p.113).
- **After:** "Holding periods are as follows: Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013 10-K, Note 2, p.91). The cuts now also rest on "the optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" (Note 8, p.126; MD&A, p.42).
- **Why it matters:** The longest holding period rose by 8 months (29%), and in the same year US rate cuts lowered depreciation by $44.2M while US car sales turned to a $48.2M loss. Size: **6.7%** (the company's stated effect of the rate changes). The holding-period part of that effect is not stated separately.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting was stretched. Longer holding periods spread cost forward just as sale prices fell (my earlier write-up showed the Note 8 claim does not hold). If the 36-month top applies only to Dollar Thrifty's existing fleet, the change would be ordinary; the report does not say which cars it covers.

**Depreciation note: "strong" dropped from the residual-value claim, but the claim itself kept**
- **Before:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012 10-K, Note 8, p.113). The MD&A said per-vehicle depreciation fell "due to improved residual values in the U.S." (p.42).
- **After:** "…was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 10-K, Note 8, p.126). The MD&A reason now reads "mix optimization, improved procurement… channel diversification" (p.42); the residual-value reason is dropped.
- **Why it matters:** US sales of cars went from a $100.6M gain to a $48.2M loss, a swing of $148.8M (22.4% of pre-tax income). The company removed the word "strong" but kept the sentence saying the rate cuts were supported. Size: the loss on sale is **7.3%**; the rate cuts are 6.7% (already counted above).
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting was stretched. The wording was softened to match weaker prices, but the conclusion was not withdrawn. Evidence that the cuts were all made before prices fell, such as rate changes and sale results shown quarter by quarter, would point to an ordinary lag instead.

**Accounting policies and controls: new disclosure of errors, with the controls conclusion unchanged**
- **Before:** not there. Controls were "effective", and Dollar Thrifty was excluded from the assessment (21% of assets) (FY2012 10-K, pp.71, 141).
- **After:** "identified certain out of period errors totaling $46.3 million". As a result, 2012 pre-tax income is now $441.4M, against $450.6M as first reported (Note 2, pp.83–84). Controls are still "effective", and a new sentence says Oracle general ledger, payables and fixed-asset modules went live in Q3 2013 (p.159).
- **Why it matters:** The errors cover 2010 to Q3 2013 (vendor incentives and Brazil). They cut 2011 pre-tax income by 5.8% and 2012 by 2.0%. Revised 2012 adjusted pre-tax income is $892.3M, against $901.5M in the FY2012 release. Size: the 2013 portion is $11.6M = **1.7%**; the total of $46.3M = 7.0%.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The amounts per year are small, but two kinds of error ran for several years and were found in the quarter after the ledger system changed. If further revisions appear in the 2014 quarterly reports, that would point away from an ordinary clean-up.

## 2. Other changes
- **Adjusted measures:** the add-back for "non-cash debt charges" (FY2012, p.50) is now "debt-related charges" (FY2013, p.50). 2013 also adds categories that 2012 did not have: integration $40.0M, relocation $7.8M, impairment $44.0M and debt premiums $28.7M, together $120.5M = **18.2%**. The "normalized" tax rate used for adjusted net income rose from 34% to 35% (releases, FY2012 p.296 line note and FY2013 p.818 line note).
- **Pricing statistic redefined:** "rental rate revenue per transaction day", which excluded "fueling and concession and other expense pass-throughs" (FY2012, p.49), has been replaced by "Total RPD" (revenue per transaction day), which is total revenue less sublease revenue (FY2013, pp.42, 49).
- **Legal:** the Sobel case moved from "the parties will again be engaging in mediation" (FY2012, p.32) to "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" and "We continue to believe the outcome… will not be material" (FY2013, p.137). That is an exposure of **6.0%**. A new securities class action is disclosed, with no amount given (p.138).
- **Program cars:** "we expect this percentage to continue to decrease" (FY2012, p.41) was dropped, and the text now says "this strategy remains flexible" (FY2013, p.42). The non-program share of the US fleet fell from 95% to 91%.
- **Critical estimates:** two additions, "industry residual value guidebooks" and "Depreciation rates are adjusted prospectively through the remaining expected life" (FY2013, p.45; not in FY2012, p.44).
