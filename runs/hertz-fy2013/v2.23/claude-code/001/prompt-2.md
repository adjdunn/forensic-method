Take each flag from your last reply and find out what the company says about it. Read the annual reports (the notes, the management discussion, the critical accounting estimates) and the results releases.

**For each flag:**
1. **Find the company's explanation.** If the company says nothing about a large movement, say so. Silence is worth noting.
2. **Check last year.** Was the same explanation given a year earlier?
3. **Read the note behind the account:** reserves and allowances with their movements during the year, changes in estimates or policies, acquisitions, anything sold, factored or financed.
4. **Test the explanation.** Calculate, in code, the number the explanation relies on, and how much of the movement it accounts for (for example, receivables that came with an acquisition against the whole rise). Check it against other sentences in the same report too. Decide whether it holds, partly holds or does not hold.
5. **Choose the most likely reading:** ordinary (nothing unusual), the business weakening, the reporting stretched, or can't tell from these documents.

**Also check, whether or not they were flagged:**
- **Adjusted earnings.** From the results releases: the gap between reported and adjusted earnings in each year, and any item called "non-recurring", "one-time" or "unusual" that appears in both years.
- **Changes in estimate that lifted income.** Any change the reports disclose in a depreciation rate, a useful life, a reserve, an allowance or another estimate that raised income, with the effect the company states. Say whether the numbers around it support the change.

**Rules:**
- Use only the attached documents. No outside knowledge of this company or of anything that happened later.
- Do every calculation in code. Don't show the code unless asked.
- Quote exactly, with the document and page. Quote the few words that carry the point, not the whole sentence.
- Work on the revised basis in this step. Where a figure in your last reply was as first reported and the revised figure differs, give both once.
- Before testing a sentence, say exactly what it claims: about what, over what period, by what measure. Test it only with a number that matches all three. If the nearest number covers something wider (a whole segment for one acquisition, a full year for a part year), say the claim can't be tested as stated.
- Never give an amount only as a share of a small net change. Give it as a share of the total as well.
- A reading is a judgment, not a finding. Word it as "most likely" or "possibly", never as a fact.
- Say what each growth figure covers (the whole company, a segment or a region), and keep to one basis.
- These are things to check, not findings. Don't use words like fraud, manipulation or red flag.
- If something you need is not in the documents, say so. Don't estimate.
