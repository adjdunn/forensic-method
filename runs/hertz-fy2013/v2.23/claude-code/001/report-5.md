# Hertz Global Holdings: items to investigate

**Company and periods:** Hertz Global Holdings, FY2013, compared with FY2012 and with Q4 2013 against Q4 2012. FY2013 pre-tax income was $663.1M.
**Documents used:** the 10-Ks for FY2012 and FY2013, the two Q4 results releases, statements.xlsx (numbers track), and the earlier replies in this conversation.

## Items to investigate

**1. Car depreciation: rates were cut in a year when cars sold below book value**
- **Shown:** The FY2013 10-K says US rate cuts ("net decreases of $44.2 million") were "indicative of the residual values experienced". The same note shows US car sales turning from a $100.6M gain to a $48.2M loss "from falling demand for used vehicles" (Note 8, p.126), and the MD&A cites "deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54).
  - **Wording:** the word "strong" was dropped from the FY2012 version of that sentence (FY2012, p.113).
  - **Holding periods:** the longest car holding period was raised from 28 to 36 months (FY2012 p.81; FY2013 p.91), and "optimization of fleet holding periods" was added as a reason for the cuts. The stated US average holding period stays at 18 months (p.11), while the MD&A cites "longer holding periods" (p.53). A rough calculation of US non-program cars held against cars sold gives a shorter period, 29.9 → 25.9 months.
  - **Numbers track:** the depreciation rate on rental equipment fell from 16.2% to 15.3% to 14.3%. In Q4, US depreciation per car rose 10.7%.
  - **Size:** the company's stated effect of the 2013 rate change is 6.7% of pre-tax income, and the loss on sale is 7.3%; both are income effects. The numbers track's depreciation not charged at the prior year's rate is 23% (revised) to 26% (as first reported); that comparison is distorted by the Dollar Thrifty acquisition date.
  - **Evidence level:** 1.
- **Reading:** The text track says most likely the reporting was stretched; the numbers track says possibly stretched. The two tracks agree on direction. Rate changes and sale results shown quarter by quarter, with the cuts made before prices fell, would point to an ordinary lag instead.
- **Next:** Quarterly rate changes and disposal results; which fleet the 36-month holding period applies to; residual-value assumptions by model year.

**2. Adjusted pre-tax income: recurring costs treated as "one-time"**
- **Shown:** Adjusted pre-tax income adds back "certain one-time charges" (MD&A, p.50; release definitions). Yet restructuring appears every year: $645.4M over 2007–2013 (Note 14, p.140). Acquisition costs appear every year from 2011 to 2013, and a litigation accrual appears in "Other" in both 2012 and 2013.
  - **New in 2013:** integration, relocation, impairment and debt-premium add-backs. "Non-cash debt charges" was renamed "debt-related charges", and the normalized tax rate went from 34% to 35%.
  - **Size of the gap:** adjusted pre-tax income is $490.1M above GAAP (74%).
  - **Size:** restructuring and related costs are 14.9%; the new 2013 categories are 18.2%. Both are costs left out of the adjusted figure.
  - **Evidence level:** 1.
- **Reading:** Most likely the measure is stretched to keep a standing cost program out of headline profit. If restructuring stopped after the Dollar Thrifty integration and the headquarters move, that would point to ordinary.
- **Next:** Restructuring charges for 2014, and a breakdown of "restructuring related" and "Other".

**3. Payables: the company's explanation does not fit the numbers**
- **Shown:** Days payable fell from 52.7 to 42.7 for the year, and from 50.9 to 42.8 in Q4. The company gives no reason except that operating cash flow rose "due to the timing of our payments" (p.64, the same wording as FY2012 p.60). A later payment schedule would raise payables, not lower them. $79.1M of the fall is not explained by any line in the statements.
  - **Context:** Oracle "accounts payable" modules went live in Q3 2013 (p.159).
  - **Size:** a balance of $226M (as first reported) to $233M (revised), 34–35%.
  - **Evidence level:** 1. The size is a balance, not an income effect, which is why it ranks below items 1 and 2.
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents. It is possibly ordinary: Dollar Thrifty brought in $43M of payables in late 2012, and the ledger system changed.
- **Next:** A breakdown of payables and accrued liabilities, including the $289.1M of fleet purchases held in them (p.81).

**4. Q4 2013: margin fell while the full year held**
- **Shown:** Gross margin fell from 22.1% to 18.5% in Q4 (full year 23.2%). The release blames "lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (FY2013 release, p.2). That compares with expectations, so it can't be tested as stated. The US figures fit it: revenue per day down 1.4%, utilisation down from 79.8% to 75.7%, depreciation per car up 10.7%.
  - **Size:** 14% (income effect).
  - **Evidence level:** 2.
- **Reading:** Most likely the business is weakening. Q4 per-car depreciation is shared with item 1. Pricing and utilisation recovering in the first half of 2014 would point to a one-quarter fleet-sizing miss instead.
- **Next:** Utilisation and per-car depreciation for Q1 2014, and fleet size against demand.

**5. Errors spanning several years, with controls still called effective**
- **Shown:** The FY2013 10-K adds "out of period errors totaling $46.3 million" (p.83), covering 2010 to 2013. The vendor-incentive errors were booked against marketing instead of reducing the cost of cars, so they touch the same account as item 1; the others are Brazil assets and doubtful accounts.
  - **Effect on earlier years:** they cut 2011 pre-tax income by 5.8% and 2012 by 2.0%.
  - **Controls:** still "effective" (p.159).
  - **Size:** 7.0% in total; the 2013 portion is 1.7% (income effects).
  - **Evidence level:** 3.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. Further revisions in the 2014 quarterly reports would point away from an ordinary clean-up.
- **Next:** The make-up of the "other immaterial errors", and the 2014 quarterly reports.

## Also noted
- **Receivables:** days sales outstanding fell from 76.5 to 51.3, leaving about $110M unreconciled. The balance is $737–746M (111–113%), larger than items 3–5 but only a balance, and no explanation is given.
- **Legal:** the Sobel case "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" with no accrual stated (exposure, 6.0%), and a new securities class action has no amount.
- **Impairment tests:** no headroom is disclosed on goodwill ($1,347.5M) or trade names ($3,330.0M) (balances).
- **Intangible lives:** the policy table now says "3 to 10 years" for other intangibles, but Donlen intangibles are still 16 and 20 years.
- **Reserves and tie-outs:** the public-liability reserve has no table of movements. The doubtful-accounts allowance covered 1.00 year of write-offs, now 0.76. Bad-debt expense is $41.9M in one place and $45.9M in another; the impairment is $40.0M in one place and $44.0M in another.
- **Other changes:** "Total RPD" redefined; "continue to decrease" dropped from the program-car outlook; a fleet-debt requirement that was "unknowingly not being met" (p.150).

## Checked and explained
- **Operating margin vs industry:** the GAAP pre-tax margin of 6.2% is close to the industry's 5.9%. The gap is most likely fleet interest sitting below the operating line, though the interest split could not be tested.
- **Other checks that held:** US per-vehicle depreciation fell 1.1%, as stated; the 2012 rate cut was supported by gains on sale; international and equipment residual statements fit; lease schedules tie out; the lower prior-year adjusted figure follows from the error revision.

## Not examined
These documents did not allow: interest split between fleet and corporate debt, receivables by type, quarterly rate changes, movements in the public-liability reserve, related-party detail in the proxy statements, and the FY2010 measures.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
