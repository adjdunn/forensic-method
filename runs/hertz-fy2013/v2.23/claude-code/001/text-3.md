## 2. Write-ups

**Capitalized costs and lives: depreciation cut despite losses on sale (contradicted in the audited notes)**
- **The report says:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (Note 8, p.126).
- **Test:** The sentence claims that, for 2013 and in the US, the sale prices actually achieved supported the lower depreciation rates. The matching number is in the same note: 2013 US disposals made "net losses of $48.2 million", "primarily due to … declining residual values". In 2012 the same line was a gain of $100.6M, a swing of $148.8M. Sales below book value point to residual values that were too high, not supported. **The claim does not hold.** The MD&A also attributes US depreciation to "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54). Size: $44.2M of depreciation not charged = 6.7% of pre-tax income; the loss on disposal is 7.3%.
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting was stretched. Residual estimates were raised in earlier quarters and then had to be corrected through losses when the cars were sold. If the rate cuts were all made early in the year and prices fell only late in the year, the note's mention of "timing of sales" would point to an ordinary lag instead.
- **Read next:** The rate changes and disposal results quarter by quarter, the residual-value assumptions by model year, and per-vehicle depreciation in Q1 2014.

**Adjusted measures: restructuring treated as one-time (contradicted in the MD&A and earnings release)**
- **The report says:** adjusted pre-tax income adds back "certain one-time charges and nonoperational items" (MD&A, p.50; earnings release definitions).
- **Test:** The claim is that the excluded restructuring costs are one-time. Restructuring charges were $56.4M in 2011, $38.0M in 2012 and $77.0M in 2013, and $645.4M from 2007 to 2013, or about $92.2M a year (Note 14, p.140). The same note adds that "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed". **The claim does not hold for restructuring.** Size: restructuring plus related charges of $98.8M = 14.9% of pre-tax income. All add-backs together come to $490.1M (73.9%), and the purchase-accounting and debt-cost add-backs also recur every year. Adjusted net income also uses a "normalized" 35% tax rate, against the 47.8% actually charged.
- **Reading:** Most likely the measure is stretched. The business is running a standing cost program, and its cost is being kept out of the headline profit figure. If restructuring stops once the Dollar Thrifty integration and the headquarters move are finished, the "one-time" label would fit better.
- **Read next:** Restructuring charges in 2014 and later, and a breakdown of the $21.8M of "restructuring related charges".

**Auditor and controls: errors over several years with controls called effective (leans aggressive)**
- **The report says:** "did not, individually or in the aggregate, result in a material misstatement", yet correcting them in Q4 "would have been material to the fourth quarter" (Note 2, p.83).
- **Test:** Revising the prior years lowered pre-tax income by 2.0% in 2012 and 5.8% in 2011; net income fell 6.1% in 2011. The $46.3M total equals 74% of Q4 2013 pre-tax income ($62.3M). The errors cover vendor incentives, which were booked against marketing instead of reducing the cost of cars, and Brazil, across 2010–2013. Size: 7.0% in total; the 2013 portion is 1.7%.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The amounts are small for a single year, but two separate kinds of error ran for several years while internal controls were reported "effective" (p.159). If no further errors come to light, that would point to ordinary clean-up.
- **Read next:** Later 10-Qs for any further revisions, and what makes up the "other immaterial errors".

## 3. Also noted
- **Tie-out gap:** bad-debt expense is $41.9M in Schedule II (p.158) but $45.9M in the cash-flow statement (p.80).
- **Tie-out gap:** the impairment is $40.0M in the cash-flow statement and Note 15 (pp.80, 146) but "Impairment charges and other" is $44.0M (p.50). The words "and other" may explain the difference.
- The MD&A says US monthly depreciation per vehicle "decreased" (p.42). It holds on the whole fleet including leased cars ($215.9 against $218.3, down 1.1%). On company-operated cars only, it rose 3.2%.
- Subsequent events: a fleet-debt requirement was "unknowingly not being met" and needed noteholder waivers (p.150).

## 4. Not in the documents
There is no table of movements in the public-liability reserve and no warranty reserve. Nothing is disclosed on factoring or supplier finance. The auditor's report has no critical audit matters, which were not required in 2014. The related-party detail in the proxy statement is incorporated by reference and is not attached.
