1. Write-ups
Depreciation (Notes 2 and 8): car range lengthened, and the reason for the rate cuts changed

Before: "Useful lives are as follows: Cars | 4 to 28 months"; the rate reduction "was indicative of the strong residual
values" (2012 pp. 81, 113).

After: "Holding periods are as follows: Cars | 4 to 36 months"; the cuts "reflect ... the optimization of fleet holding
periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" and were "also indicative of the residual values" (2013 pp. 91, 126).

Why it matters: The top of the range rose by 8 months (29%) and "strong" was dropped in the year U.S. disposals
swung from a 100.6 gain to a 48.2 loss. Neither report states the effect of the longer range separately. Size: the
company's stated effect of the 2013 U.S. rate changes is 44.2 = 6.7%.

Reading: Most likely the reporting stretched, because the justification moved from resale values to holding periods just
as resale results turned negative. It would be ordinary if the longer range only reflects Dollar Thrifty's fleet practice,
though that fleet was already owned when the 2012 range was set.
Business description: average holding period unchanged while its range moved

Before: "approximate average holding period for a rental car was eighteen months in the United States" (2012 p. 11).

After: the same words (2013 p. 11), while 2013 cites "longer holding periods" as a cause of higher maintenance cost
(p. 53).

Why it matters: The average reads the same while the top of its range went from 28 to 36 months, and neither report
says how the average is computed, so it does not fit as stated. Size: not quantified; any income effect sits inside the
44.2 above.

Reading: Can't tell; possibly the average is rounded or computed on cars sold rather than cars held. A stated method,
or an average by fleet (Hertz and Dollar Thrifty), would settle it.
Controls (Item 9A): acquisition exclusion dropped, new ledger added, errors not mentioned

Before: "We have excluded from our evaluation the internal controls ... of Dollar Thrifty", at "21% and 2%" of assets
and revenues (2012 p. 141; auditor's report p. 71).

After: exclusion dropped; new: "implemented Oracle general ledger, accounts payable and a portion of fixed assets" in
the third quarter (2013 p. 159).

Why it matters: 2013 is the first year Dollar Thrifty is in scope and the year of a ledger change, and the fourth quarter
found 46.3 of errors (2013 p. 83) that Item 9A does not mention. Size: no 2013 income effect after revision; 46.3 = 7.0%
as scale.

Reading: Possibly the reporting stretched, as in the earlier single-year write-up. The comparison adds that the control
environment changed in two ways during the year, which makes the unchanged "effective" conclusion harder to read; a
disclosed deficiency assessment would point the other way.

2. Other changes

Legal: the Nevada concession-fee case went from "parties will again be engaging in mediation" (2012 p. 122) to a
judgment that "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" (2013 p. 137; exposure, 6.0%). "none of those reserves are
material" is unchanged (2012 p. 123; 2013 p. 138). A securities class action is new (2013 p. 138).

Fleet wording: dropped "We believe the residual values have remained fairly stronger" (2012 p. 42) and "Non-program
cars typically have ... lower depreciation rates" (2012 p. 41). "we expect this percentage to continue to decrease"
became "this strategy remains flexible" (2013 p. 42).

Adjusted pre-tax income: "non-cash debt charges" became "debt-related charges" (2012 p. 50; 2013 p. 50). New
2013 exclusions are integration 40.0, relocation 7.8, impairment 44.0 and a 39.4 loss on note conversion, together
131.2 = 19.8%. The releases move the assumed tax rate from 34% to 35% (2012 release p. 20; 2013 release,
unnumbered page).

Prior-year figures shown differently: the 2012 allowance for doubtful accounts is 25.1 in 2012 (p. 72) and 29.3 in
2013 (p. 75); 2012 pre-tax income is 450.6 and then 441.4 (2013 p. 84); 2012 adjusted pre-tax income is 901.5 (2012
release p. 14) and then 892.3.

Property lives table: buildings "3 to 50" became "5 to 50" years, software "1 to 15" became "1 to 10", other intangibles
"3 to 20" became "3 to 10" (2012 p. 81; 2013 p. 91). No reason or effect is given.
