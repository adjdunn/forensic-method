**Hertz Global Holdings: FY2013 10-K (year to 31 Dec 2013), compared with FY2012**
Sources: FY2012 and FY2013 annual reports, the Q4 2012 and Q4 2013 results releases, statements.xlsx, the three earlier replies in this conversation and the pasted numbers-track replies. Sizes are shares of 2013 pre-tax income ($663.1m).

### Items to investigate

**1. Adjusted pre-tax income: excluded charges called "one-time" recur every year**
- **Shown:** The adjusted measure adds back "certain one-time charges and nonoperational items" (MD&A p.50; release definitions). But restructuring was charged every year from 2007 to 2013, $645.4m in total (Note 14, p.140). Purchase accounting, debt charges and "Other" also recur (numbers track).
  - New exclusions in 2013: integration, relocation and impairment total $91.8m (13.8%), or $130.4m (19.7%) with the convertible-note loss.
  - The $21.9m of integration costs in reconciling items equals the $21.9m headquarters move booked in restructuring. If they are the same costs, $21.9m (3.3%) was excluded twice. Not tested.
  - Corporate EBITDA labels $283.4m (42.7%) "unusual or non-recurring".
  - Size: all adjustments $490.1m = 73.9%; restructuring and related costs $98.8m = 14.9%. Ranks on 73.9%. **Evidence: level 1.**
- **Reading:** Most likely the presentation is stretched; the GAAP figures carry the full costs. It would read as ordinary if restructuring stops after the Dollar Thrifty integration and the headquarters move.
- **Next:** 2014 restructuring charges. Ask whether the $21.9m integration figure and the $21.9m relocation are the same costs.

**2. Car depreciation: rates cut and the holding-period range lengthened while U.S. used-car prices fell**
- **Shown:** Note 8 says the rate cuts were "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." The same note reports $48.2m of losses on U.S. car sales from "declining residual values" (p.126).
  - Last year's wording was "strong residual values". "Strong" was dropped, and the reasons became internal ones: sales channels, holding periods (FY12 p.113 → FY13 p.126).
  - The car holding-period range went from 4–28 to 4–36 months (p.81 → p.91). The stated U.S. average holding period stayed at 18 months (p.11), which doesn't fit as stated.
  - U.S. depreciation per car-month before sale results fell from $241.6 to $207.7 (rough).
  - The numbers track finds the depreciation rate fell from 15.17% to 14.27%. Including sale gains and losses, it was flat.
  - Size: the company's stated effect is $44.2m (6.7%) for the U.S., or $39.6m (6.0%) net. The numbers track's calculated shortfall against the prior-year rate is $152.1m (23%; first reported $174.5m, 26%), and the stated effect explains only 26% of it. Both are income effects; ranks on 23%. **Evidence: level 1.**
- **Reading:** Both tracks read it as possibly stretched reporting. It would read as ordinary if retail-channel sales bring back gains on disposal.
- **Next:** Residual values assumed against those realized, by group of cars. What the 36-month top of the range covers. The 2014 quarterly gains and losses on sale.

**3. Q4 2013 margin fell on extra fleet**
- **Shown:** Q4 gross margin fell from 22.1% to 18.5%. The CEO cites "lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (release p.2–3), a claim that can't be tested as stated.
  - U.S. average fleet rose 22.4% against 16.1% more transaction days.
  - Q4 pre-tax income was $62.3m (p.149).
  - Size: $90.2m = 14% (11.4% after the company's adjustments). Ranks on 14%. **Evidence: level 2.**
- **Reading:** Most likely the business weakening. It would be a one-quarter dip if fleet was "aligned with transaction day growth" by Q1 2014.
- **Next:** U.S. fleet utilization and depreciation per vehicle by quarter, and Q1 2014 results.

**4. Errors found in Q4 2013 handled as revisions; controls judged effective**
- **Shown:** Out-of-period errors totalled $46.3m: vendor incentives, Brazil receivables allowances and under-accruals (Note 2, p.83–89).
  - They changed 2012 pre-tax income from $450.6m to $441.4m, and the 2012 allowance from $25.1m to $29.3m.
  - The auditor's report has no explanatory paragraph, and controls were judged effective (p.74, p.159).
  - A fleet-note requirement "was unknowingly not met" at year-end (p.67).
  - Size: the 2013 portion is $11.6m = 1.7%. **Evidence: level 2.**
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The text track leans aggressive on how the errors were handled. It would read as ordinary if they were a one-off clean-up tied to the Q3 2013 Oracle ledger rollout.
- **Next:** Any further revisions in the 2014 10-Qs, and the audit committee's disclosures.

**5. Payables fell behind costs, with nothing said**
- **Shown:** Days payable fell from 52.9 to 42.7 while cost of sales rose 19.4%.
  - The cash flow statement shows payables as a $23.2m inflow, yet the balance fell $35.3m. The $58.5m difference is unexplained.
  - Payables to fleet suppliers can't be split from accrued liabilities.
  - Size: $232.8m is a working-capital balance, not an income effect. **Evidence: level 2.**
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents. Faster payment of ordinary suppliers would make it ordinary.
- **Next:** A split of payables into trade and fleet purchases.

### Also noted
Neither the company nor these documents explain the following:
- **Pricing measure:** RPD now includes fuel and concession fees that the 2012 report called "beyond the control of management", yet keeps the "ability to control" sentence (FY12 p.49 / FY13 p.49). Level 3; not quantified.
- **Sobel case:** a possible judgment "could potentially exceed $40.0 million", yet "will not be material" (p.137). Exposure 6.0%.
- **Intangible lives:** the table range was cut to 3–10 years, while Donlen lives of 16 and 20 years remain (p.91/p.99).
- **Multiemployer pensions:** underfunding went from a fact to "could have" (p.73).
- **Disclosure gaps:** no goodwill headroom, and no development table for the $347.7m public liability and property damage reserve.
- **Tie-out gaps:** bad-debt provision $45.9m vs $41.9m; Simply Wheelz impairment $40.0m vs $44.0m.

### Checked and explained
- **Receivables above the industry share of sales:** fleet-sale receivables explain 71% of the 2013 fall. Without them, receivables are 10.7% of sales, below the industry.
- **Free cash flow below net income:** explained by fleet growth of $546.2m.
- **Tie-outs that held:** the segment rate changes and sale results tie to the totals.

### Not examined
The documents give no residual values by group of cars, no goodwill test inputs and no insurance-reserve development. Related-party detail sits in proxies that are not attached. Nothing after March 2014 is covered.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
