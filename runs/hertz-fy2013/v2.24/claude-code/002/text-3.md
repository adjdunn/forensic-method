## 2. Write-ups

**Depreciation: lower U.S. depreciation rates are said to match residual values, but U.S. car sales lost money**
- **The report says:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (Note 8, p. 126).
- **Test:** The sentence claims that, in U.S. car rental in 2013, the prices actually realized on car sales supported the lower depreciation rates. The matching number is in the same note: U.S. car sales produced "net losses of $48.2 million," which the note attributes to "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles." MD&A gives the same cause for higher U.S. depreciation: "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p. 54). For 2013 the claim does not hold: cars sold below book value even as the rates were cut. Size: U.S. $44.2m = 6.7% of pre-tax income ($39.6m net = 6.0%). The segment figures tie to the $39.6m in Item 6.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting is stretched. Residual-value assumptions went up while the residual values realized went down. The executive share awards (PSUs) are tied to Corporate EBITDA, which is struck after fleet depreciation, so the bonus measure moves with this estimate (p. 124). A different reading would hold if the losses came from the "timing of sales" the note also cites, or from different groups of cars than the ones whose rates were cut. In that case both statements could be true at once.
- **Read next:** Quarterly U.S. gains and losses on car sales and the rate changes by group of cars, from the 2013 10-Qs and the Q1 2014 10-Q.

**Adjusted pre-tax income: restructuring is called "one-time" but recurs every year**
- **The report says:** Adjusted pre-tax income excludes "certain one-time charges and nonoperational items" (MD&A, p. 50). The earnings release uses the same words (Exhibit 1).
- **Test:** The sentence claims the excluded charges are one-off. Restructuring was charged in every year shown: $56.4m in 2011, $38.0m in 2012 and $77.0m in 2013. Note 14 gives "$645.4 million" from 2007 to 2013, an average of $92.2m a year (p. 140), and says "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed" (p. 140). The claim does not hold for restructuring. Size: $98.8m = 14.9% of pre-tax income. Purchase accounting from the 2005 buyout ($132.2m, 19.9%) also recurs, though it is correctly labelled non-cash. The adjusted figure also assumes a 35% tax rate against an actual 47.8%.
- **Reading:** Most likely the stretch is in presentation only. The GAAP figures carry the full costs, but the adjusted figure treats a steady stream of costs as one-off. A different reading would hold if restructuring stops once the Dollar Thrifty integration and the headquarters move ($21.9m in 2013) are finished.
- **Read next:** The 2014 quarterly restructuring charges, and the FY2012 reconciliation (next step).

**Controls: errors handled as revisions, and controls still judged effective**
- **The report says:** "did not, individually or in the aggregate, result in a material misstatement" (Note 2, p. 83). Separately, a fleet-note requirement "was unknowingly not met" at year-end (p. 67).
- **Test:** The claim is that the errors were immaterial to prior years, measured against pre-tax income. The revisions were 2.0% of 2012 pre-tax income ($9.2m), 5.8% of 2011 ($18.7m) and 1.9% of nine-month 2013 ($11.6m). The claim holds against common thresholds for 2012 and 2013; 2011 is borderline. Size: the 2013 portion is $11.6m = 1.7%, already in the reported 2013 figures.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The profile leans aggressive because the company chose a revision rather than a restatement and still concluded controls were effective. However, the amounts are small and the auditor agreed, so this is a judgment call, not a clear stretch. The errors span three kinds (vendor incentives, Brazil allowances, under-accruals) over 2010 to 2013, and that would point to weaker controls. If they came from a one-off clean-up around the Q3 2013 Oracle ledger rollout (p. 159), the reading would be ordinary.
- **Read next:** Whether the 2014 10-Qs make further revisions, and the audit committee's disclosures.
