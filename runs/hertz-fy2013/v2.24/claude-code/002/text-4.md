## 1. Write-ups

**Depreciation policy (Note 2): the car holding-period range was lengthened and relabelled**
- **Before:** "Useful lives are as follows: … Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012 10-K, p. 81).
- **After:** "Holding periods are as follows: … Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013 10-K, p. 91). A new reason appears for the lower depreciation rates: "optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" (p. 42, p. 126).
- **Why it matters:** The top of the range rose by 8 months (+28.6%) on a $14,456.6m car fleet. U.S. car-rental depreciation per car per month, before gains and losses on sale, fell from $241.6 to $207.7 (−14.0%, a rough measure). That fall came in a year the company says used-car prices "deteriorat[ed]" (p. 54). The report never presents the longer range as a change in estimate. Size: not stated separately. It sits inside the U.S. depreciation-rate changes, which cut depreciation by $44.2m, or 6.7% of pre-tax income.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting is stretched. A longer range and a new holding-period reason arrive in the year sale prices fell, and both lower the depreciation charge. A different reading would hold if the 36-month top covers only Dollar Thrifty or lease-type cars. The unchanged 18-month U.S. average (p. 11) would fit that reading.

**Depreciation (Note 8 and MD&A): "strong" dropped, and the reason for lower rates moved from the used-car market to the company's own actions**
- **Before:** The rate cuts were "indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012 p. 113). The report also said "We believe the residual values have remained fairly stronger" and gave "improved residual values in the U.S." as the reason (p. 42).
- **After:** The cuts were "also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 p. 126, p. 42). "The favorable adjustments reflect … car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion…" (p. 126). The "fairly stronger" sentence was dropped.
- **Why it matters:** U.S. car sales went from a $100.6m gain in 2012 (as revised) to a $48.2m loss in 2013. That swing of $148.8m equals 22.4% of 2013 pre-tax income, yet depreciation rates were still cut. Size: the company's stated effect is $44.2m, or 6.7% of pre-tax income ($39.6m, or 6.0%, net of all segments).
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting is stretched. Dropping "strong" admits the market turned, but the sentence stayed in place. The cuts now rest on internal factors that the 2013 sale losses do not yet show. A different reading would hold if retail-channel sales in 2014 realize prices above book value.

**Pricing measure (MD&A and earnings release): RPD now includes fees once called outside management's control, but keeps the "ability to control" sentence**
- **Before:** RPD (revenue per transaction day) was calculated "excluding revenue derived from fueling and concession and other expense pass-throughs," because those items "are driven by factors beyond the control of management" (FY2012 p. 49).
- **After:** Total RPD is "total revenues less revenues from fleet subleases" (FY2013 p. 42). The same closing words were kept: "encompasses the elements in car rental pricing that management has the ability to control" (p. 49). The prior year is restated: U.S. RPD for 2012 "declined by 3.1%" (FY2012 p. 42) becomes "decreased 2.8%" (FY2013 p. 58).
- **Why it matters:** The excluded revenue in 2012 was $1,676.6m, or 22.0% of car-rental revenue. The 2013 report credits $49.3m of U.S. refuelling fees for revenue growth. The headline U.S. price gain of +1.4% now includes such fees. Size: not an income effect; not quantified.
- **Reading:** Possibly the presentation is stretched. The pricing measure was widened to include items the prior report called uncontrollable, while its justification was left unchanged. A different reading would hold if the change aligned the measure with peers, but the report gives no reason.

## 2. Other changes
- **Legal, new:** For the Sobel case, a judgment "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" and "We continue to believe the outcome … will not be material" (FY2013 p. 137). The 2012 report gave no amount and no view on materiality (FY2012 p. 32). Exposure: 6.0% of annual pre-tax income and 64% of Q4 pre-tax income. In 2013 Item 3 only points to Note 13, so the comparison used Note 13.
- **Controls and prior-year figures, new:** A new "Correction of Errors" section (FY2013 p. 83–89) changes prior-year figures:
  - 2012 pre-tax income goes from $450.6m to $441.4m.
  - The 2012 doubtful-accounts allowance goes from $25.1m to $29.3m (Sch. II, FY2012 p. 140 vs FY2013 p. 158).
  - Neither auditor's report has an explanatory paragraph (p. 71 and p. 74). Item 9A adds the Q3 2013 Oracle ledger rollout (p. 159).
- **Pension, softened:** "Several of our remaining multiemployer plans have underfunded liabilities" (FY2012 p. 70, p. 25) becomes "Our multiemployer plans could have, significant underfunded liabilities" (FY2013 p. 73, p. 26). A statement of fact became a possibility; neither year gives an amount.
- **Risk factors:** "We expect this percentage [program cars] to continue to decrease" was dropped (FY2012 p. 22). The heading now starts "Increased fleet costs" (FY2013 p. 23). A Simply Wheelz risk is new, with 9,000 vehicles still held at February 2014 (p. 24).
- **Adjusted pre-tax income, new exclusions:**
  - New in 2013: integration costs ($40.0m), relocation ($7.8m) and impairment ($44.0m), totalling $91.8m (13.8% of pre-tax income). Adding the convertible-note conversion loss inside "Other" brings this to $130.4m (19.7%).
  - The label "non-cash debt charges" became "debt-related charges" (FY2012 p. 50 vs FY2013 p. 50).
  - Worth checking: the $21.9m of integration costs in reconciling items equals the $21.9m headquarters move booked in restructuring (Note 14, p. 140). If they are the same costs, they were excluded twice.
