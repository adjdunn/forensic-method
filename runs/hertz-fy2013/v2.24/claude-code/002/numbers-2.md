## Flags with the weakest explanations

I worked on the revised basis this time. Where a figure from the last reply was first-reported and differs, both are given once.

**1. Payables fell behind costs.** Days payable went from 52.9 to 42.7 (52.7 first reported). Payables fell 3.5% while cost of sales rose 19.4%, both whole-company. Size: $232.8m, or 35% of pre-tax income (first reported: $226.3m, 34%).
- **The company says:** nothing about the accounts payable balance, in either annual report or either results release. Last year: also nothing.
  - The nearest sentence is about operating cash flow, not the payables balance: OCF rose partly "due to the timing of our payments" (FY2013 annual report, p.65). So I didn't test it.
- **Test:** there is no explanation to test. Two numbers stand out without resolving it:
  - The cash flow statement shows payables as a $23.2m inflow, yet the balance fell $35.3m. That $58.5m difference is not explained.
  - Unpaid fleet purchases rose $42.1m (from $247.0m to $289.1m), but this figure is shared with accrued liabilities and can't be split out (p.82).
  - Measured against direct operating costs alone, days payable still fell, from 76.4 to 61.4.
- **Reading:** can't tell from these documents; the balance mixes ordinary trade payables with fleet-purchase payables, and neither is shown on its own. A trade-payables figure falling in days while fleet payables rose would point toward the ordinary reading (paying suppliers faster).
- **Read next:** a split of accounts payable into trade and fleet purchases. The reports don't give one.

**2. Rental equipment depreciation rate fell.** It went from 15.17% to 14.27% (15.28% first reported for 2012). Size: $152.1m, or 23% of pre-tax income (first reported: $174.5m, 26%).
- **The company says:** U.S. rate changes produced "net decreases of $44.2 million" in 2013 depreciation, reflecting "car sales channel diversification" and "optimization of fleet holding periods" (FY2013 annual report, p.43 and Note 8, p.127). The net effect across all operations was −$39.6m (p.41). Last year: the same reason ("net decreases of $130.6 million", FY2012 annual report, p.114).
- **Test:** the claim concerns 2013 depreciation expense from rate changes, in dollars, and matches the measure.
  - The net −$39.6m covers 26% of the $152.1m shortfall, or 1.6% of total rental depreciation. So the explanation **partly holds**.
  - The same report sits against the cut:
    - Note 8 shows $48.2m of losses on 2013 U.S. car sales, after a $100.6m gain in 2012, due to "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles" (p.127).
    - The MD&A cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.55).
  - Counting gains and losses on disposal, the rate was flat at 14.48% and 14.49%.
- **Reading:** possibly reporting stretched, because rates were cut by $44.2m in the U.S. in the same year U.S. car sales lost $48.2m. If the stated drivers (retail sales channel, holding periods) bring disposal gains back in later years, the ordinary reading would fit better. Those later years aren't in these documents.
- **Read next:** Note 8 and the U.S. car rental fleet discussion, for residual values assumed against those realised. Neither is given by vehicle type.

**3. Q4 gross margin fell.** It went from 22.1% to 18.5%, whole-company. Size: $90.2m, or 14% of pre-tax income.
- **The company says:** "lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet", estimated at "$0.12" per share (FY2013 results release, p.3). Last year: a different story; the Q4 2012 release credited "strong residual values" (FY2012 results release, p.4).
- **Test:** the claim is measured against management's own expectations, not against Q4 2012, so it **can't be tested as stated**. The year-on-year figures fit its direction:
  - U.S. average fleet rose 22.4% against 16.1% more transaction days, about 5.4% more fleet per transaction day (U.S. car rental only).
  - U.S. revenue per transaction day fell about 1.7%.
  - Rental depreciation rose 16.9% against revenue up 10.2% (whole company).
  - Stripping out the company's own adjustments still leaves 84% of the fall ($75.5m).
- **Reading:** most likely the business weakening: too much fleet for demand in the quarter, plus softer pricing. The outlook says fleet would "be aligned with transaction day growth" by the end of Q1 2014 (release, p.7). If that happened, this was a one-quarter dip, but no later quarter is in the documents.
- **Read next:** U.S. fleet utilisation and depreciation per vehicle by quarter. Neither release gives them.

## Other flags
- **Receivables against the industry (14.0% of sales vs 11.5%; days sales outstanding 76.2 → 51.3):** the company cites "a decrease in fleet receivables within our U.S. car rental segment… timing of purchases and sales" (FY2013 annual report, p.134). The FY2012 report said nothing on receivables.
  - That claim is for one segment, so it can't be tested as stated. Company-wide, receivables from fleet sales fell $261.3m. That is 71% of the $367.1m fall in receivables, and 14% of the 2012 balance.
  - Without fleet-sale receivables, the balance is 10.7% of sales, below the industry. The policy note says receivables include credit "extended to manufacturers" (p.91).
  - The explanation largely holds; most likely ordinary.
  - The allowance rose to 2.0% of gross receivables (1.53% revised in 2012), as write-offs rose to $40.3m from $29.3m.
- **Net income above free cash flow ($346.2m vs $314.5m; 2012 revised $238.6m vs $46.5m, first reported $54.8m):** the company points to fleet growth ("increase in net investments", release, p.3). Net fleet spending rose $546.2m. This holds; most likely ordinary.
  - The company's own definition gives free cash flow of $448.7m, above net income.

## Adjusted earnings
- **2013:** adjusted pre-tax income was $1,153.2m against GAAP $663.1m. The $490.1m gap equals 74% of GAAP and 42% of adjusted.
- **2012:** adjusted was $892.3m against GAAP $441.4m (first reported: $901.5m against $450.6m). The $450.9m gap equals 102% of GAAP.
- **Recurring items:** the definitions describe the adjustments as "certain one-time charges" (release, unnumbered definitions page). Yet several items appear in every year:
  - restructuring: $66.2m, $49.1m and $98.8m in 2011–13;
  - purchase accounting;
  - debt-related charges;
  - acquisition costs;
  - "Other".
- The Corporate EBITDA tables label $185.1m (2012) and $283.4m (2013) as "unusual or non-recurring".

## Changes in estimate that lifted income
- Changes in residual-value depreciation rates cut rental depreciation by $39.6m in 2013 (6.0% of pre-tax income) and $130.1m in 2012 (FY2013 annual report, p.41).
- In 2012 the numbers around the change support it: U.S. car sales made a $100.6m gain.
- In 2013 they don't: U.S. car sales lost $48.2m, and the report describes residual values as falling.
- No other income-raising change in a useful life, reserve or allowance is disclosed.
