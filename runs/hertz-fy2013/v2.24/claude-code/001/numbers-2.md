**1. The rental equipment depreciation rate fell** (from 15.2% to 14.3% in FY2013; it was 16.2% in FY2011).
- **The company says:** U.S. rate changes "resulted in net decreases of $44.2 million" in depreciation, and "the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 10-K, p. 42). Last year it gave the same reason more strongly: "strong residual values experienced in the U.S.", for a $130.6m cut (FY2012 10-K, p. 113).
- **Test:** The claim is that U.S. car residual values in 2013 supported lower rates. Across all segments, the 2013 rate changes net to −$39.6m. That covers 26% of the $152.1m charge not taken at the FY2012 rate (first reported as $174.5m), and equals 1.6% of the $2,407.8m total charge. The same report goes against the stated reason:
  - U.S. car disposals moved from net gains of $100.6m to net losses of $48.2m, a $148.8m swing, put down to "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles" (p. 126).
  - The MD&A cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p. 54).
  
  So the explanation does not hold.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting is stretched. Rates were lowered in a year when the company's own disposal results and text show residual values falling. Two things would point to an ordinary reading instead: rate cuts limited to Dollar Thrifty cars on new holding periods ("optimization of fleet holding periods", p. 42), or a mix shift toward Donlen leases, whose rates "are not adjusted" (p. 45).
- **Read next:** A quarterly split of the U.S. rate changes against the quarterly disposal gains and losses; this is not in these documents.

**2. Payables fell while costs rose** (days payable fell from 52.9 to 42.7 on the revised basis; first reported as 52.7).
- **The company says:** Nothing about the payables balance. The nearest sentence is about operating cash flow, which rose partly "due to the timing of our payments" (FY2013 10-K, p. 64). Last year it said the same about cash: "timing of our payments" (FY2012 10-K, p. 60).
- **Test:** Payables fell 3.5% while the cost line rose 19.4%, both company-wide. The fall holds on direct operating costs alone (76.4 to 61.4 days). The cash flow statement shows payables rising by $23.2m, but the balance fell $35.3m, a $58.5m gap these documents do not reconcile. My earlier acquisition reading covers little: Dollar Thrifty brought $43m of payables (p. 98), 18% of the $232.8m size (first reported as $226.3m). Fleet purchases in payables and accrued liabilities rose, from $247.0m to $289.1m. With nothing from the company, the explanation does not hold.
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents. The fall also coincides with a new "accounts payable" system put in place in Q3 2013 (p. 159). A cash flow statement that bridged the balance, covering currency and acquisitions, would point to an ordinary reading.
- **Read next:** A breakdown of payables between trade, fleet and other, which is not disclosed.

**3. Payables are about twice the industry's share of sales** (9.0% against 4.5%; $483.2m above, 73% of pre-tax income).
- **The company says:** Nothing about the level. The only related disclosure is "Purchases of revenue earning equipment included in accounts payable and accrued liabilities | $289.1" (FY2013 10-K, cash flow supplement). Last year it gave the same disclosure, at $247.0m.
- **Test:** Property purchases in payables ($55.6m) cover 12% of the gap. Adding all $289.1m of fleet purchases would cover 71%, which still leaves payables at 5.8% of sales. Fleet purchases are not split between payables and accrued liabilities, so the explanation partly holds at most.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary: fleet buying on credit inflates payables for a rental company. If most of the $289.1m sits in accrued liabilities, more of the gap would be left unexplained.
- **Read next:** The split of the $289.1m between payables and accrued liabilities, which is not in these documents.

**Other flags:**
- **Receivables above the industry's share of sales:** The company describes receivables as "credit extended to manufacturers and customers" (p. 90), and $357.3m of them are uncollected fleet sales. Without those, receivables are 10.7% of sales, below the industry's 11.5%. The $261.3m drop in fleet-sale receivables also covers 71% of the $367.1m fall in total receivables. This holds; most likely ordinary.
- **Q4 gross margin fell:** The company cites "lower than expected pricing and higher expenses related to carrying extra fleet" (FY2013 release, p. 2). That claim is measured against expectations, not against Q4 2012, so it can't be tested as stated. Against Q4 2012, the numbers fit it:
  - U.S. revenue per day fell 1.4% while the U.S. average fleet grew 22.4% and rental days grew 16.1%.
  - Depreciation running ahead of revenue accounts for 41% of the $90.2m margin fall, and direct operating costs for 59%.
  
  This partly holds; most likely the business weakening.

**Adjusted earnings:** Adjusted pre-tax income exceeded reported pre-tax income by:
- FY2011: $356.2m (110%).
- FY2012: $450.9m (102% revised; 100% as first reported).
- FY2013: $490.1m (74%).

Adjusted pre-tax income is defined to include "certain one-time charges" (FY2013 release). Yet restructuring is added back every year: $66.2m in 2011, $49.1m in 2012 and $98.8m in 2013 (15% of FY2013 pre-tax income). Purchase accounting, debt charges, acquisition costs and litigation accruals also appear in both years.

**Changes in estimate that lifted income:** Lower depreciation rates cut the charge by a net $130.1m in 2012 (29% of revised pre-tax income) and $39.6m in 2013 (6%), from the FY2013 10-K (pp. 42–43). In 2012 the $100.6m of U.S. disposal gains supports the cut; in 2013 the $48.2m of U.S. losses does not. No other income-raising change in estimate is disclosed; the allowance for doubtful accounts rose, from $29.3m to $30.8m.
