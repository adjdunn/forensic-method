## 1. Write-ups

**Revenue earning equipment (note): longer car holding-period range, with holding periods newly cited as a reason for lower depreciation**
- **Before:** "Useful lives are as follows: … Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012 10-K, Note 2, p. 81). The 2012 rate cuts were explained only by "the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (Note 8, p. 113).
- **After:** "Holding periods are as follows: … Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013 10-K, Note 2, p. 91). The rate cuts now "reflect changes from the impact of car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion and the optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" (Note 8, p. 126).
- **Why it matters:** The longest car holding period rose by 8 months (+29%). Keeping cars longer spreads their cost over more months, which lowers depreciation, and the U.S. rate changes cut depreciation by $44.2m. Size: $44.2m = 6.7% of 2013 pre-tax income, as stated by the company. The holding-period part of that amount is not given separately.
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting is stretched. A longer life was added as a new reason in the same year that U.S. car sales turned to losses (covered in my last reply). If the 36-month cars were only Dollar Thrifty fleet that came in already on longer cycles, that would point to an ordinary change; the report doesn't say which cars sit at the top of the range.

**Depreciation (Note 8 and MD&A): "strong" dropped from residual values while U.S. sale results swung from gains to losses**
- **Before:** "indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012, Note 8, p. 113). The MD&A added "We believe the residual values have remained fairly stronger" and "With fewer program cars in our fleet, we have an increased risk that the market value of a car… will be less than its estimated residual value" (FY2012, p. 41–42).
- **After:** "was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013, p. 126). Both MD&A sentences are dropped (FY2013, p. 42). The risk sentence survives only in the risk factors (p. 23).
- **Why it matters:** The sentence was softened rather than withdrawn. Gains of $100.6m on U.S. car sales in 2012 became losses of $48.2m in 2013, a swing of $148.8m (22.4% of 2013 pre-tax income), yet U.S. depreciation rates were cut again. Size: rate cut $44.2m = 6.7%; sale loss $48.2m = 7.3%.
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting is stretched: the claim was kept in a weaker form when the numbers no longer supported it. If the 2013 rate cuts were all made in the first half, before used-car prices fell, that would point to a timing lag instead; the quarterly split is not disclosed.

**Controls and auditor: Dollar Thrifty now inside the controls opinion; past errors corrected; prior-year figures restated lower**
- **Before:** Dollar Thrifty was excluded from the internal-control assessment and audit: "21% and 2%" of total assets and revenues (FY2012, p. 71, p. 141). There was no correction-of-errors note.
- **After:** The exclusion is dropped (FY2013, p. 74, p. 159). A new "Correction of Errors" note covers "$46.3 million" of errors (p. 83). A new controls sentence says "the Company implemented Oracle general ledger… In the third quarter of 2013" (p. 159).
- **Why it matters:** Several prior-year figures are now shown differently:
  - 2012 pre-tax income: $450.6m → $441.4m (–2.0%).
  - 2012 adjusted pre-tax income: $901.5m in the FY2012 release (p. 4) → $892.3m. On the restated base, 2013 adjusted growth for the whole company reads 29.2% instead of 27.9%.
  - The 2012 bad-debt allowance: $25.1m (FY2012 Schedule II, p. 140) → $29.3m (FY2013, p. 158).
  
  Size: 2013 errors $11.6m = 1.7%. Prior-year errors of $34.7m are an exposure, not a 2013 income effect.
- **Reading:** Possibly a weakening in the year-end close. The errors were found in the same year a new general ledger went in, yet controls were judged unchanged in Q4. If the auditor or management reported a remediated control deficiency elsewhere, that would point to an ordinary clean-up; none is mentioned.

## 2. Other changes
- **Adjusted measures:** Five exclusions appear in 2013 that were not taken in 2012, totalling $159.9m (24.1% of pre-tax income):
  - integration $40.0m;
  - impairment and other (Simply Wheelz) $44.0m;
  - loss on converting the convertible notes $39.4m;
  - premiums paid on debt $28.7m;
  - relocation $7.8m.
  
  "non-cash debt charges" became "debt-related charges" (FY2012 10-K, p. 50 → FY2013, p. 50; releases, Exhibit 1). The tax rate assumed for adjusted net income went from 34% to 35%, which lowers adjusted net income by about $11.5m.
- **Legal:** The Nevada fee case (Sobel) gains "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" and "We continue to believe the outcome… will not be material". No such amount or view was given in FY2012 (FY2012, p. 121–122 → FY2013, p. 137). The exposure is 6.0% of pre-tax income. A new securities class action has no amount (p. 138).
- **Risk factors:** The heading "Declines in the value of the non-program cars… and declines in the overall number of program cars" became "Increased fleet costs, either generally or due to declines in the value of the non-program cars" (FY2012, p. 22 → FY2013, p. 23). "We expect this percentage to continue to decrease" became "this strategy remains flexible" (FY2012, p. 41 → FY2013, p. 42). A new risk factor covers losses from Simply Wheelz (p. 24).
- **Useful lives of other assets:** Shortened. Software went from 1–15 to 1–10 years, other intangibles from 3–20 to 3–10, and buildings from 3–50 to 5–50 (FY2012, p. 81 → FY2013, p. 91). Depreciation of property and equipment rose from 6.8% to 7.9% of gross cost, which fits the shorter lives.
- **Critical estimates:** The source for residual values adds "industry residual value guidebooks", and a new sentence says "Depreciation rates are adjusted prospectively through the remaining expected life" (FY2012, p. 44 → FY2013, p. 45).
