**Hertz Global Holdings: fiscal 2013, compared with fiscal 2012**
Sources: the 10-Ks for 2012 and 2013, the Q4 2012 and Q4 2013 earnings releases, the statements spreadsheet, two text-track replies and two numbers-track replies.

### Items to investigate

**1. Adjusted pre-tax income: restructuring called "one-time" but charged every year, plus new add-backs this year**
- **Shown:** The measure adds back "certain one-time charges" (MD&A p. 50). Restructuring was charged every year from 2007 to 2013, $645.4m in total (Note 14 p. 140), and "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed" (p. 140). Five add-backs not taken in 2012 total $159.9m: integration, relocation, the Simply Wheelz write-off, the loss on converting notes and debt premiums. The 2012 phrase "non-cash debt charges" now reads "debt-related charges" (p. 50 in both years).
  - Sizes: restructuring plus related costs $98.8m = 14.9% of pre-tax income; new add-backs 24.1%; all add-backs together 73.9%.
  - Ranks on 24.1%. Evidence level 1: the "one-time" sentence is contradicted by Note 14. Both tracks found it.
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting is stretched (text track): recurring cost-cutting is left out of headline growth of 29.2%. A stated end date for the programs would change this, and none is given.
- **Next:** Restructuring by program, with start and end dates and the savings achieved, and the reason for each new add-back.

**2. Car depreciation: rates cut and holding periods lengthened while U.S. car sales lost money**
- **Shown:** Note 8 says the rate cuts were "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." The same note shows U.S. car sales swinging from gains of $100.6m to losses of $48.2m, put down to "declining residual values" (p. 126). The MD&A cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p. 54). Both years go against the same sentence:
  - Last year's "strong" was dropped from it (2012 p. 113).
  - The longest car holding period rose from 28 to 36 months (2012 p. 81; 2013 p. 91), and "optimization of fleet holding periods" is a new reason for the cuts. Yet the stated U.S. average holding period stayed at eighteen months (p. 11 in both years).
  - Three more statements no longer fit: "longer holding periods" (p. 53) and "decreased the percentage of program cars" (p. 42), while the U.S. program share rose from 5% to 9%. The depreciation rate on rental equipment fell from 16.2% to 15.3% to 14.3% between 2011 and 2013 (numbers track).
  
  Sizes: the company's stated effect is $44.2m for U.S. cars (6.7%), or a net $39.6m across all segments (6%). The numbers track puts the charge not taken at the 2012 rate at $152.1m (22.9%), and the item ranks on that figure. The 2013 sale loss is $48.2m (7.3%). Evidence level 1.
- **Reading:** Both tracks agree: most likely the reporting is stretched (text track), possibly stretched (numbers track). Rate cuts limited to Dollar Thrifty cars on new holding periods, or made before used-car prices fell, would point to an ordinary reading.
- **Next:** The quarterly split of rate changes against disposal results, the cars that sit at 36 months, and early 2014 resale prices.

**3. Q4 margins fell as the fleet outgrew demand**
- **Shown:** Q4 gross margin fell from 22.1% to 18.5% ($90.2m, 14% of pre-tax income). U.S. revenue per rental day fell 1.4% while the average U.S. fleet grew 22.4% and rental days grew 16.1%. Management cites "carrying extra fleet" (release p. 2) and guides to excess fleet in Q1 2014. Evidence level 2.
- **Reading:** Most likely the business weakening (numbers track). Fleet back in line with demand by the end of Q1 2014, as guided, would change it.
- **Next:** Q1 2014 fleet size against rental days, and per-vehicle depreciation by quarter.

**4. Year-end close and controls: past errors found in Q4 while controls were judged effective**
- **Shown:** $46.3m of errors were found in Q4 and fixed by revising prior years (Note 2 p. 83): vendor incentives, Brazil bad-debt allowances and under-accruals. Several figures changed:
  - 2012 pre-tax income: $450.6m → $441.4m.
  - 2012 adjusted pre-tax income: $901.5m → $892.3m. This lifts 2013 adjusted growth for the whole company from 27.9% to 29.2%.
  - The 2012 bad-debt allowance: $25.1m → $29.3m.
  
  A new general ledger went in during Q3 (p. 159), yet controls were reported unchanged in Q4. A debt requirement was "unknowingly not being met" at year-end (p. 150).
  - Sizes: 2013 errors $11.6m = 1.7% of pre-tax income. Prior-year errors of $34.7m are an exposure, equal to 56% of Q4 pre-tax income.
  - Evidence level 2.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting is stretched (text reply 1), or possibly a weakening in the year-end close (text reply 2). A disclosed control deficiency that has since been fixed would point to an ordinary clean-up.
- **Next:** The audit committee or the auditor's view of the control deficiencies, and the 2014 10-Qs for further revisions.

**5. Payables fell while costs rose, and the cash flow statement doesn't bridge the balance**
- **Shown:** Days payable fell from 52.9 to 42.7. The payables balance fell 3.5% while costs rose 19.4%. The cash flow statement shows payables rising by $23.2m while the balance fell $35.3m, a $58.5m gap. Dollar Thrifty explains only 18% of the fall. The fall coincides with the new accounts-payable system (p. 159).
  - Size: a balance of $232.8m, not an income effect.
  - Evidence level 2.
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents (numbers track). A cash flow statement that bridged the balance, covering currency and acquisitions, would point to an ordinary reading.
- **Next:** A split of payables into trade, fleet and other, and the reconciling items between the balance and the cash flow line.

### Also noted
These candidates did not make the list and were not explained:
- **Simply Wheelz (the buyer of the divested Advantage business):** $65.0m of 2013 sublease revenue came from it; it went bankrupt, and the $44.0m write-off (6.6%) is excluded from adjusted income. It still held about 9,000 Hertz vehicles at February 1, 2014.
- **Nevada fee case:** exposure above $40.0m (6.0%), with no accrual disclosed. The securities class action has no amount.
- **Goodwill and trade name:** $4,677.5m (a balance) with no impairment headroom disclosed.
- **Payables at twice the industry's share of sales:** only partly explained by fleet purchases on credit; most likely ordinary.
- **Adjusted net income:** uses an assumed 35% tax rate against an actual 47.8%.
- **Other intangibles:** stated range of 3–10 years, but Donlen intangibles run 16 and 20 years.
- **Tie-out gaps:** bad-debt expense of $41.9m in Schedule II against $45.9m in the cash flow statement; HQ relocation of $21.9m in the text against $19.0m in the table.

### Checked and explained
- **Receivables:** above the industry's share only because of uncollected car-sale proceeds; without them they are 10.7% of sales, below the industry's 11.5%.
- **Accident-claims reserve:** grew slower than revenue, but holds steady at 3.26% → 3.23% of revenue when 2012 includes Dollar Thrifty for the full year.
- **Other items that fit when tested:**
  - International "slight strengthening" of residual values matches smaller sale losses.
  - Shorter lives for software and buildings match a higher depreciation rate on property and equipment.
  - Concession agreements amortize over about 9.2 years, as stated.
  - The error amounts tie to $34.7m.
  - Prior-year rate changes re-split by segment tie ($139.4m − $8.8m = $130.6m).

### Not examined
The documents give no goodwill headroom, no claims-history table for the accident reserve, no quarterly split of depreciation rate changes, no split of payables, and no Q4 free cash flow. Neither year's auditor's report has a critical audit matters section.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
