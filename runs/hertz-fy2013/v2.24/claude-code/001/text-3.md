## 2. Write-ups

**Useful lives and residual values: depreciation rates cut while car sales lost money**
- **The report says:** The rate cuts gave "net decreases of $44.2 million… in depreciation expense" and were "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." for 2013 (Note 8, p. 126). Residual value is what a car fetches when sold.
- **Test:** The sentence claims that 2013 U.S. car resale values supported lower depreciation. The matching number is in the same note: 2013 U.S. car disposals made "net losses of $48.2 million", put down to "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles". Selling below book value means depreciation had been too low, so the claim does not hold for 2013. It does hold for 2012 and 2011, which show gains of $100.6m and $114.9m. The MD&A also says U.S. depreciation rose on "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p. 54). Size: $44.2m = 6.7% of pre-tax income (charge not taken); the disposal loss is another 7.3%.
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting is stretched: rates were cut on assumptions about sales channels and holding periods (how long cars are kept) while actual resale prices were falling. Possibly the cuts reflect longer holding periods after the Dollar Thrifty integration, and the losses come from timing and excess fleet late in the year.
- **Read next:** A quarterly split of the rate changes and disposal losses, and the residual values assumed for each car cohort against actual 2014 sale prices.

**Adjusted measures: restructuring called one-time but charged every year**
- **The report says:** Adjusted pre-tax income adds back "certain one-time charges and nonoperational items" (MD&A, p. 50). Corporate EBITDA treats restructuring as "extraordinary, unusual or non-recurring" (release, Table 7).
- **Test:** The claim is that restructuring, which is excluded, is one-time. Note 14 shows restructuring charges in every year from 2007 to 2013, $645.4m in total, an average of $92.2m a year: $56.4m in 2011, $38.0m in 2012 and $77.0m in 2013 (p. 140). The same note says "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed." The claim does not hold. Size: restructuring plus related costs of $98.8m = 14.9% of pre-tax income. Other adjustments that also recur, purchase accounting ($132.2m) and debt-cost amortization ($68.4m), bring the total to 45.2%.
- **Reading:** Most likely the reporting is stretched: cost-cutting is an ongoing cost of running the business and is left out of the headline profit growth of 29.2% on the adjusted measure. Evidence of a different reading would be a clear end date for these programs in the report, and none is given.
- **Read next:** Restructuring by program, with start and end dates and the savings achieved.

**Controls: past errors found in Q4 and handled as revisions**
- **The report says:** The errors "did not, individually or in the aggregate, result in a material misstatement", although correcting them in Q4 "would have been material to the fourth quarter" (Note 2, p. 83). Management also says "No changes in our internal control… during the fiscal quarter ended December 31, 2013" had a material effect (Item 9A, p. 159).
- **Test:** The claim is that the errors were not material in any prior year, on pre-tax and net income. The 2011 errors were $18.7m, which is 5.8% of 2011 reported pre-tax income ($324.3m) and 6.1% of net income. The 2012 errors were 2.0%. The $34.7m of prior-year errors equals 56% of Q4 2013 pre-tax income ($62.3m). Nothing in the report contradicts the claim. A 5% cut-off is a common rule of thumb, not a requirement, so 2011 sits just past it. Size: 2013 errors $11.6m = 1.7%; prior-year errors $34.7m (exposure).
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting is stretched. The errors cover several areas: vendor incentives booked as a cut to marketing costs instead of vehicle cost, Brazil bad-debt allowances, and under-accruals. That points to a weakness in the year-end close more than to one isolated slip. A different reading would follow if no further errors were found in 2014.
- **Read next:** The auditor's view of the control deficiencies behind these errors, and the 2014 10-Qs.

## 3. Also noted
- Bad-debt expense is $41.9m in Schedule II (p. 158) but $45.9m in the cash flow statement (p. 80), a tie-out gap of $4.0m.
- Headquarters relocation within restructuring is $21.9m in the text (p. 140) but $19.0m in the table and accrual footnote (p. 140–141). A further $7.8m of relocation costs is excluded separately in the adjusted measures (p. 50).
- Adjusted net income of $749.6m assumes a 35% tax rate (release, p. 5). The actual effective rate was 47.8%.
- $65.0m of 2013 revenue was vehicle sublease income from Simply Wheelz (p. 50). It went bankrupt, and the $44.0m write-off that followed is excluded from the adjusted measures (p. 51). Separately, a debt covenant was "unknowingly not being met" at year-end and needed waivers (p. 150).

## 4. Not in the documents
There is no critical audit matters section, no supplier-finance or receivables-factoring disclosure, and no goodwill or trade-name headroom figures.
