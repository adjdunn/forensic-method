## 1. Write-ups

**Depreciation of cars: the holding-period range was extended with no stated effect, alongside disclosed rate cuts**
- **Before:** "Revenue earning equipment… Useful lives are as follows: Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012 10-K, p.81). U.S. car depreciation-rate changes "resulted in net decreases of $130.6 million" (worldwide car rental, p.113).
- **After:** "Holding periods are as follows: Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013 10-K, p.91). U.S. rate changes gave "net decreases of $44.2 million" in 2013 (p.126). The new reasons given for these changes include "optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" (p.126).
- **Why it matters:** The top of the range rose by 8 months (29%). Neither report names this as a change in estimate or gives its effect. Depreciation of cars, excluding sale results and lease rents, fell from 16.7% to 15.4% of average gross car cost. At the 2012 rate, 2013 depreciation would have been about $177m higher. That is rough: it mixes the rate cuts, the fleet mix, and Dollar Thrifty's late-2012 entry into the averages. Size: the stated rate-cut effect is $44.2m = **6.7%** of pre-tax income. The range change is not quantified by the company.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting is stretched. Longer lives and lower rates lifted income in the same year U.S. car sales turned from a $100.6m gain to a $48.2m loss. If the 36-month cars are only Dollar Thrifty's longer-held fleet, the change would be ordinary. A breakdown of the range by brand or of the fleet by age would show that.

**Note 8: "strong" dropped from the residual-value claim as sale results turned to losses**
- **Before:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012, p.113).
- **After:** "…was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013…" (FY2013, p.126). New in the same note: "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles" (p.126). In management discussion, the 2012 explanation for lower per-vehicle depreciation, "due to improved residual values in the U.S." (p.42), is replaced in 2013 by "mix optimization… channel diversification" (p.42).
- **Why it matters:** Last reply showed the 2013 claim does not hold. The comparison adds this: the company softened the adjective and kept the sentence. Meanwhile U.S. disposal results swung by $148.8m, from a $100.6m gain to a $48.2m loss. That swing is 22.4% of pre-tax income. Size: the $48.2m loss = 7.3% of pre-tax income.
- **Reading:** Most likely the wording was adjusted to the numbers rather than the claim being dropped. That suggests the reporting is stretched rather than the business weakening alone. A disclosure that the 2013 rate cuts were confined to vintages or channels that sold above book value would point to ordinary.

**Legal proceedings: an exposure figure appears and a securities suit is added**
- **Before:** The Nevada concession-fee (Sobel) case had no amount: "the parties will again be engaging in mediation" (FY2012, p.32 and p.122). There was no securities suit.
- **After:** "A judgment - which could potentially exceed $40.0 million"; "We continue to believe the outcome of this case will not be material" (FY2013, p.137). A shareholder class action is new: "Pedro Ramirez, Jr. v. Hertz Global Holdings" covers Feb–Nov 2013, with no amount given (p.138).
- **Why it matters:** The court granted plaintiffs partial summary judgment on restitution, yet no accrual is stated. Size: exposure above $40m, equal to **6.0%** of pre-tax income (an exposure, not an income effect).
- **Reading:** Can't tell. "Continue to believe" asserts a view the 2012 report never stated for this case. The amount accrued, or a settlement figure from the March 2014 mediation, would settle it.

## 2. Other changes
- **Adjusted pre-tax income:** 2013 adds back items that 2012 did not have:
  - integration $40.0m
  - relocation $7.8m
  - impairment and other $44.0m

  Together they total $91.8m = 13.8% of pre-tax income. "Non-cash debt charges" becomes "debt-related charges" (10-K p.50 both years; releases p.19 and unnumbered). Restructuring plus related costs doubled, from $49.1m to $98.8m.
- **Prior-year figures shown differently:**
  - 2012 pre-tax income: $450.6m → $441.4m
  - 2012 adjusted pre-tax income: $901.5m → $892.3m; Corporate EBITDA: $1,635.6m → $1,626.4m (FY2012 release p.4; FY2013 release p.5)
  - doubtful-accounts allowance at end-2012: $25.1m → $29.3m (p.140 → p.158)
  - gross cars: $12,591.1m → $12,548.8m

  As a result, adjusted pre-tax growth (whole company) reads 29.2% rather than 27.9%.
- **Risk factors:** "We expect this percentage to continue to decrease" (program cars) was dropped. The heading changed from "declines in the overall number of program cars" to "Increased fleet costs…" (p.22 → p.23). The international program-car share rose from 53% to 57%; the U.S. share went from 19% to 18%.
- **Critical estimates:** "industry residual value guidebooks" was added, as was "Depreciation rates are adjusted prospectively through the remaining expected life". "and assessing depreciation rates" was dropped (p.44 → p.45). Property lives also changed: buildings from "3 to 50" to "5 to 50" years, software from "1 to 15" to "1 to 10", other intangibles from "3 to 20" to "3 to 10" (p.81 → p.91). No effect is stated.
- **Controls:** The Dollar Thrifty exclusion was dropped (p.71/141). New: "implemented Oracle general ledger… [in Q3 2013]" (p.159). In the same year, Q4 found $46.3m of errors. The opinion is still unqualified.
