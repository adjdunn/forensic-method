## 2. Write-ups

**Useful lives / residual values: depreciation rates were cut in a year of losses on car sales**
- **The report says:** "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013…" (Note 8, p.126; repeated in management discussion, p.42).
- **Test:** The claim is that U.S. car sale prices in 2013 supported lower depreciation. If they had, U.S. car sales would have come in at or above book value. The same note gives U.S. disposals as a **net loss of $48.2m** in 2013, against a $100.6m gain in 2012, "primarily due to… declining residual values". Management discussion attributes higher U.S. depreciation to "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54). **The claim does not hold for 2013.** Size: the rate cuts reduced 2013 depreciation by $44.2m, 6.7% of pre-tax income. The disposal losses were $48.2m, 7.3%.
- **Reading:** Possibly the reporting is stretched. Rate cuts that lower expense while sales show losses suggest depreciation is set too low and the difference surfaces later as losses on sale. A different reading: the note credits retail-channel expansion and longer holding periods for the Dollar Thrifty fleet, and blames "timing of sales" for the losses. If so, the cuts could be sound and the losses a timing effect.
- **Read next:** Quarterly split of rate changes and disposal results; residual assumptions by model year; 2014 disposal results against book value.

**Adjusted pre-tax income: recurring restructuring added back as one-time**
- **The report says:** adjusted pre-tax income adds back "certain one-time charges and non-operational items" (management discussion, p.50; Note 11, p.131).
- **Test:** The claim is that the excluded charges are one-time. Restructuring was $56.4m in 2011, $38.0m in 2012 and $77.0m in 2013. The note gives $645.4m over 2007–2013, about $92m a year. It also says "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed" (p.140). **The claim does not hold for restructuring.** Size: restructuring plus restructuring-related costs of $98.8m = 14.9% of pre-tax income. Adding integration ($40.0m) and relocation ($7.8m) gives 22.1%. All add-backs together are 73.9%.
- **Reading:** Most likely the measure is stretched rather than the business weakening. Restructuring has been a cost in every year for seven years. A different reading: 2013's charges are tied to specific events, namely the Dollar Thrifty integration and the $21.9m headquarters move.
- **Read next:** Restructuring charges for 2014 and later; whether lenders' Corporate EBITDA definition, which excludes "non-recurring" items, also excludes these costs.

## 3. Also noted
- Tie-out gap: the provision for doubtful accounts is $45.9m in the cash flow statement (p.80) but $41.9m "charged to expense" in Schedule II (p.158).
- Tie-out gap: the 10-K puts the out-of-period errors at $46.3m (p.83); the earnings release puts them at "$47.9 million". The Dec-2010 accumulated deficit is $1,123.2m / $1,127.7m in the note (p.84) but $1,123.3m / $1,127.8m in the equity statement (p.78).
- Prior-period errors: vendor incentives had been booked as "a reduction of marketing expenses instead of reducing the cost of revenue earning equipment". The prior-period errors add up to $34.7m, matching the note. Booking them in Q4 would have equalled 56% of Q4 pre-tax income of $62.3m. Even so, internal control was judged effective and Item 9A reports no material Q4 changes (p.83, p.159).
- "U.S. monthly per vehicle depreciation costs decreased" (p.42) can't be tested as stated, because the result depends on the fleet basis. Including leased cars, it is down 1.1%; company-operated cars only, it is up 3.1%. The segment figure also includes lease charges.
