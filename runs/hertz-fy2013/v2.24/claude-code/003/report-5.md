**Hertz Global Holdings: FY2013 (to 31 Dec 2013), compared with FY2012. All sizes are a share of FY2013 pre-tax income of $663.1m.**
Sources: the FY2012 and FY2013 10-Ks, the Q4 2012 and Q4 2013 results releases, statements.xlsx, the three earlier text-track replies and the two pasted numbers-track replies.

### Items to investigate

**1. Car depreciation: rates cut and holding periods lengthened while U.S. resale values fell**
- **Shown:**
  - Note 8 says the U.S. rate cuts were "also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." for 2013. The same note reports U.S. car-sale "net losses of $48.2 million" from "declining residual values", against $100.6m of gains in 2012 (FY13 p.126). Management discussion cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54).
  - Compared with FY12, the word "strong" was dropped from the residual-value sentence (FY12 p.113). The top of the car holding-period range moved from 28 to 36 months with no stated effect (p.81 → p.91). The stated U.S. average holding period stayed at "eighteen months" (p.11, both years), even though p.53 now cites "longer holding periods".
  - The numbers track finds the rental-equipment depreciation rate fell from 16.1% to 15.2% to 14.3% (FY11–13). Counting gains and losses on sale, the all-in rate is flat at 14.5%.
  - Sizes: the company's stated rate-cut effect is $44.2m (6.7%), and the U.S. disposal loss is $48.2m (7.3%). Estimates of the 2013 charge not taken at the 2012 rate: about $177m (26.7%, text track, rough), $152.1m on revised figures (23%, numbers track) and $174.5m as first reported (26%).
  - **Ranks on 26.7%** (rough). Evidence level 1.
- **Reading:** Both tracks say possibly reporting stretched: rates were cut and the stated asset lives lengthened in a year the company itself describes as having falling resale values. If the 2013 losses came from Dollar Thrifty integration timing, or the 36-month cars are only Dollar Thrifty's fleet, the reading would be ordinary.
- **Next:** Note 8 by segment and quarter, setting rate changes against disposal results; holding-period and residual-value assumptions by brand and model year; 2014 disposal results against book value.

**2. Adjusted pre-tax income: restructuring that recurs every year is added back as one-time**
- **Shown:**
  - The measure adds back "certain one-time charges" (p.50; Note 11, p.131). Restructuring was charged in every year from 2007 to 2013, $645.4m in total, and "additional… initiatives are being developed" (p.140).
  - Restructuring plus related costs doubled, from $49.1m to $98.8m. New add-backs in 2013 (integration, relocation, impairment) total $91.8m (13.8%).
  - Total add-backs are $490.1m (73.9%). The numbers track lists restructuring, acquisition costs and "Other" as "non-recurring" in both years.
  - **Ranks on 14.9%** (restructuring plus related). Evidence level 1.
- **Reading:** Most likely the measure is stretched, not the business weakening. If 2014 shows no restructuring, the reading would shift toward ordinary.
- **Next:** restructuring charges after 2013, and the lenders' definition of "non-recurring" items in Corporate EBITDA.

**3. Prior-period errors, the doubtful-accounts allowance, and controls**
- **Shown:**
  - In Q4 the company found $46.3m of errors. Of these, $34.7m related to earlier years (5.2%) and $11.6m to 2013 (1.7%). They cover vendor incentives and Brazil allowances (p.83); the release gives the total as $47.9m.
  - The provision for doubtful accounts is $45.9m in the cash flow statement (p.80) but $41.9m in Schedule II (p.158), a gap of $4.0m (0.6%). The end-2012 allowance was revised from $25.1m to $29.3m. Coverage of write-offs fell to 0.76 (rough).
  - Internal control was judged effective, with no material Q4 change (p.159).
  - **Ranks on 1.7%.** Evidence level 2.
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents whether the errors reflect a control gap.
- **Next:** the auditor's and audit committee's view of the errors; a roll-forward of the allowance that ties the $4.0m gap.

**4. Payables fell behind costs**
- **Shown:**
  - Days payable fell from 52.9 to 42.7. Payables excluding unpaid capital purchases fell 13.6% while costs rose 17.3%. The Dollar Thrifty acquisition explains about 22%.
  - The cash flow statement shows payables up $23.2m while the balance sheet shows them down $35.3m. Accrued liabilities rose. Oracle accounts-payable and general-ledger modules were implemented in Q3 2013 (p.159).
  - Size: $232.8m, a **balance** (35% of pre-tax income on the numbers track's basis; $226.3m, 34%, first reported). Not an income effect. Evidence level 2.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. Possibly faster payment of suppliers; a reclassification into accrued liabilities would change the reading.
- **Next:** a split of payables and accrued liabilities, including where the $289.1m of unpaid fleet purchases sits, set against the timing of the Oracle change.

**5. Nevada concession-fee case: an exposure amount appears, with no accrual stated**
- **Shown:**
  - FY13 adds that a judgment "could potentially exceed $40.0 million" yet "will not be material" (p.137). The FY12 report gave no amount (p.32). Plaintiffs won partial summary judgment.
  - A new securities class action names no amount (p.138).
  - Size: **exposure** above $40m (6.0%). Evidence level 3.
- **Reading:** Can't tell.
- **Next:** the amount accrued; the outcome of the March 2014 mediation.

### Also noted
- **Not tested or not consistent:**
  - Goodwill has no headroom disclosed (goodwill plus trade name is a $4,677.5m balance, 169% of equity).
  - The claim that U.S. monthly depreciation per car fell can't be tested: it depends on the fleet basis.
  - The vehicle liability reserve has no roll-forward, and its 2013 increase is attributed to Dollar Thrifty.
  - Intangible life ranges disagree within each report.
- **Changed without explanation:**
  - Property life ranges changed with no effect stated.
  - The risk factor on program cars was reworded and an expectation dropped.
  - "Non-cash debt charges" was renamed "debt-related".
- **Small tie-out gaps:** impairment $40.0m vs $44.0m; accumulated deficit differs by $0.1m.

### Checked and explained
- **Margin vs. industry:** the operating margin's lead over the industry is mostly fleet interest (7.1% vs 5.9% after net interest).
- **Receivables and payables vs. industry:** receivables above the industry are explained by vehicle-sale proceeds. Payables above the industry are 71% explained by unpaid fleet and property purchases.
- **Other figures that held:** the Simply Wheelz impairment, the restructuring accrual roll-forward, equipment and concession lives, and the error totals by year all tied out.

### Not examined
The documents give no rental-only industry peers and no fleet ageing by brand. Related-party detail is in proxy statements, which were not attached.
