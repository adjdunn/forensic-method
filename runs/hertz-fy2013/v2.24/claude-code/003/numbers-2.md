**1. Operating margin above the industry: 13.7% against 5.9% (FY2013)**
- **The company says:** nothing that compares its margin with the industry or with peers, in either annual report or either release. The nearest company figure is a pre-tax margin of "6.2%" (Q4 2013 release, Table 1). Last year: nothing.
- **Test:** There is no company explanation to test, so this checks the measure itself. My 13.7% is measured before $704.4m of net interest. Most of that interest carries the debt that pays for the rental fleet. After net interest the margin is 7.1%, against 5.9% for the industry (gap $836.7m before interest, $132m after). The industry row is Transportation, not rental. The company's explanation: none given.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. The gap comes mainly from comparing a fleet-financed business before its fleet interest with a mixed transport group. A gap that stayed wide against true rental peers would point the other way, but the documents have no such peers.
- **Read next:** the segment note (adjusted pre-tax margin by segment) and a rental-only industry figure. The second is not in these documents.

**2. Payables fell behind costs: days payable 52.9 → 42.7 (FY2012 → FY2013, whole company)**
On the revised basis, FY2012 days payable are 52.9 (first reported 52.7). Size $232.8m (first reported $226.3m), 35% of pre-tax income.
- **The company says:** nothing about the payables balance. The only nearby sentence is "due to the timing of our payments" (FY2013 annual report, p. 64). It is about the rise in operating cash flow, not the payables balance, so I have not tested it. Last year the same words were used, also about cash flow (FY2012 annual report, p. 60).
- **Test:**
  - **Dollar Thrifty base effect:** the acquisition added "Accounts payable | (43)" on 19 Nov 2012 (FY2013 annual report, p. 98). That put a full payables balance into the 2012 year-end with only about six weeks of its costs. Taking it out of the 2012 base covers $51.5m of the $232.8m (22%).
  - **Unpaid capital purchases rose, not fell:** fleet purchases unpaid in payables and accrued liabilities went from $247.0m to $289.1m. Property purchases in payables went from $35.0m to $55.6m.
  - **What's left fell:** payables less those purchases went from $721.2m to $623.2m, down 13.6%. Over the same year, direct operating plus selling and administrative costs rose 17.3%.
  - **Cash flow doesn't match:** the cash flow statement shows payables rising $23.2m, while the balance sheet fell $35.3m. The documents don't split the $289.1m between payables and accrued liabilities, so this can't be reconciled.
  - **Verdict:** no explanation is given; the acquisition covers about a fifth.
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents; possibly faster payment of operating suppliers. A shift of operating payables into "Accrued liabilities" (which rose from $739.6m as first reported to $1,104.7m) would point to a classification change instead.
- **Read next:** a breakdown of accounts payable and accrued liabilities, and how the $289.1m splits between them. The payables-system change "implemented Oracle general ledger, accounts payable" in Q3 2013 (FY2013 annual report, p. 159) is worth checking against the timing.

**3. Rental equipment depreciation rate fell: 16.1% → 15.2% → 14.3% (FY2011–13)**
On the revised basis (first reported 16.2% / 15.3% / 14.3%). Charge not taken in FY2013: $152.1m (first reported $174.5m), 23% of pre-tax income.
- **The company says:**
  - U.S. rate changes cut depreciation by "$44.2 million" in 2013, reflecting "car sales channel diversification" and "optimization of fleet holding periods"; also "indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 annual report, p. 42 and p. 126).
  - Last year the reason was different: "improved residual values in the U.S." (FY2012 annual report, p. 42), with a $139.4m cut.
- **Test:**
  - **The claim:** rate changes on U.S. cars lowered FY2013 depreciation expense by $44.2m. Net of international (+$5.0m) and equipment (−$0.4m), the disclosed changes total $39.6m. That covers 26% of the $152.1m and is 1.6% of total depreciation of $2,407.8m.
  - **Conflicts in the same report:**
    - U.S. car sales made "net losses of $48.2 million" in 2013, after $100.6m of gains in 2012, "due to… declining residual values" (p. 126).
    - U.S. depreciation rose partly on "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p. 54).
  - **All-in rate is flat:** counting gains and losses on sale, the rate is unchanged at 14.5% in 2012 and 2013. The lower base rate was matched by losses when cars were sold.
  - **Verdict:** partly holds. The cut is real, but the residual-value reason runs against the company's own disposal results.
- **Reading:** Possibly reporting stretched: rates were cut in a year the company itself describes as having falling residual values, and losses on sale followed. If the 2013 losses come from Dollar Thrifty integration timing rather than residuals, that would point to ordinary.
- **Read next:** Note 8 by segment and by quarter, the rate changes against disposal results, and holding-period assumptions for the Dollar Thrifty fleet.

That is 71% of the $367.1m fall and 13.9% of the 2012 total, so it holds. 2012 receivables are $1,879.7m revised, $1,886.6m first reported.
  - **Reading:** most likely ordinary.
