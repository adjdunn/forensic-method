## 2. Write-ups

No claim was contradicted, either in the audited statements and notes or in the management discussion and adjusted measures. Both write-ups below are areas that lean aggressive.

**Adjusted measures: a recurring amortization charge is excluded**
- **The report says:** "excludes the impact of amortization expense from acquired intangible assets … We do not adjust for the revenue that is generated in part from the use of our acquired intangible assets" (earnings release, Non-GAAP, p. 8).
- **Test:** The reconciliation recomputes: $14.23 + $0.61 − $0.15 = $14.69 adjusted EPS, and adjusted operating margin works out to 13.05%. The charge is not one-time. The 10-K schedules $683m of amortization for fiscal 2026 (p. 61), which is about $0.52 per share after tax and consistent with the guided ~$0.50. Size: $607m = 3.3% of pre-tax income.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. The exclusion is labelled plainly, applied the same way to both years, and the release says outright that the related revenue stays in. The profile still says "leans aggressive" because the direction of the choice is to drop a cost that recurs. What would change the reading: other items starting to appear in the adjustment, or amortization growing faster than the acquired revenue.
- **Read next:** The adjusted-measure definitions in later releases, and the split of amortization between SRS ($398m) and the Primary segment ($209m).

**Acquired intangibles: long straight-line lives for customer relationships**
- **The report says:** Amortized straight-line, "as this approximates the pattern of expected economic benefit" (Note 1, p. 51). The fair values rest on "expected customer attrition rates" (Note 13, p. 76). Weighted lives are 20 years for SRS and 19 years for GMS (pp. 75–76).
- **Test:** Nothing to test directly. The report gives no attrition rate, so it can't show whether the benefit really is even across the period. For scale only, on my own assumption: a 15-year life would add about $99m a year, roughly 0.5% of pre-tax income. Size: not quantified. The customer-relationship balance is $9.0bn net.
- **Reading:** Possibly stretched, mildly. Valuing customers on attrition implies front-loaded benefit, while straight-line over about 20 years spreads the cost evenly and keeps the near-term charge low. This combination is also common among acquirers of distribution businesses. What would point to an ordinary reading: disclosed attrition rates low enough to support a 20-year even pattern.
- **Read next:** The attrition and discount-rate assumptions behind the SRS and GMS valuations, and the final GMS allocation, which is "preliminary" (p. 75).

## 3. Also noted

- **Depreciation tie-out gap:** the cash flow statement shows $3,514m of depreciation excluding intangibles (p. 47); Note 3 shows $3,452m (p. 58). The gap is $62m.
- **Goodwill headroom:** the SRS fair value test says only "exceeded" (p. 60). "Other" operating income was $316m, or about $714m before amortization. That is roughly 3% of the about $23.5bn paid for SRS and GMS, partly because GMS was owned for only part of the year.
- **Claims tested that held:**
  - Acquisitions added $6.3bn of sales; the "Other" segment rose by $6,311m (p. 33).
  - The fall in gross margin came from SRS and GMS: the Primary segment's margin was 34.4% against 20.6% in "Other".
  - Inventory turnover recomputes to 4.46 times.
  - ROIC recomputes to 25.7%.
  - GMS is "approximately 1%" of sales; it is 1.2%.
  - GMS is "approximately 3%" of assets excluding goodwill and intangibles (p. 77). At the acquisition date it was 3.8% (p. 74), but the dates differ, so this can't be tested as stated.
- **Gross reporting of installation:** when "we provide or arrange for" installation, material and labour both go into services revenue (p. 52). The principal-or-agent judgment is not explained. Services are 3.4% of sales, and the choice affects revenue only, not income.
