## 1. Write-ups

Neither report discloses a change in an estimate or policy that lifted income. The three write-ups below are two statements left the same while the numbers moved, and one change in how goodwill was tested.

**Inventory shrink: same estimating method, a smaller estimate, and the auditor dropped shrink as a critical audit matter**
- **Before:** A 10% rise in the shrink rate "would have increased cost of sales by approximately $95 million for fiscal 2024" (FY2024 10-K, p. 39). The auditor's report had a critical audit matter titled "Estimation of store shrink" (p. 42).
- **After:** The same sensitivity is "approximately $79 million for fiscal 2025" (FY2025 10-K, p. 39). The method sentence is word-for-word the same in both years: the estimate "is primarily based on recent shrink results" (p. 39). The auditor replaced the shrink matter with "Sufficiency of audit evidence over certain merchandise inventories", which is about the IT systems behind the retail method (p. 41).
- **Why it matters:** The sensitivity implies the shrink estimated between physical counts fell from about $950m to about $790m, or $160m lower. The management discussion credits "lower shrink" for gross margin, and the Primary segment's gross margin rose from 33.9% to 34.4%. Size: about 0.9% of pre-tax income. This is rough: it assumes the sensitivity scales in a straight line and covers only the estimated part of shrink, not the losses actually counted.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. Lower recent losses feed a lower estimate under the stated method, which fits both the management discussion and the margin. What would point the other way: next year's physical counts showing losses above this year's estimate, which would mean the estimate ran light in the same year the auditor stopped singling it out.

**Customer receivables: credit risk now "increasingly" important, but the allowance is still "not material"**
- **Before:** Pro customers "primarily use trade credit"; the risk is if they "are unable to repay the trade credit from us" (FY2024 10-K, p. 23). Customer receivables "increased … primarily as a result of the SRS acquisition" (p. 48).
- **After:** They "increasingly use trade credit … as do some of our other Pros" (FY2025 10-K, p. 24). The sentence explaining the increase is dropped (p. 48). The allowance sentence, "was not material", is unchanged in both years (p. 48 both).
- **Why it matters:** Customer receivables rose $692m, or 36.5%, to $2,588m. They are now 46% of total receivables, up from 39%, and the allowance still has no amount. GMS brought $899m of receivables of all types at acquisition, so the rise can't be split between acquired balances and growth in credit. Size: not quantified. Balance: $2,588m.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. Most of the rise plausibly came with GMS, and the "Pro Trade Credit" programme is being expanded as planned (p. 24). What would point to a weaker reading: an allowance amount and roll-forward showing write-offs growing faster than the balance.

**Goodwill (from my last write-up): first quantitative test of the SRS units shows no stated headroom**
- **Before:** The SRS units "were also analyzed for impairment … using a qualitative approach" (FY2024 10-K, p. 60).
- **After:** "using a quantitative approach … fair value of each reporting unit exceeded its respective carrying amount" (FY2025 10-K, p. 60). The Primary segment keeps "substantially exceeded" (p. 60 both years).
- **Why it matters:** This is the first full test of the $13.8bn of goodwill in "Other". The word "substantially" is used for the Primary segment but not for SRS. "Other" earned $316m of operating income in fiscal 2025. Size: exposure, a balance equal to 74% of pre-tax income.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. A quantitative test in the year after an acquisition is the more rigorous step and quite normal. Leaving out "substantially" possibly means the headroom is narrow. What would settle it: the headroom, discount rate and growth assumptions for each SRS unit.

## 2. Other changes

- **Legal proceedings:** the EPA consent-decree discussion went from "engaged in discussions with the EPA" (FY2024 10-K, p. 29) to "formally terminated", with about $1.7m paid and recovered from installers (FY2025 10-K, p. 29). The Washington refrigerant matter, a $1.6m penalty, was dropped as resolved. Both are ordinary.
- **Risk factors:** a new sentence says "Sustained periods without such events [storms] can lead, and in the past have led, to lower sales" (FY2025 10-K, p. 22; not in the FY2024 10-K, p. 21). It matches the results release's "lack of storm activity in the third quarter" (release, p. 2).
- **Unrecognized tax benefits:**
  - Dropped: the statement that it is "reasonably possible" these benefits fall "by an immaterial amount" over the next twelve months (FY2024 10-K, p. 67).
  - Reworded: "none of which are expected to be paid in the next 12 months" (p. 37) became "nearly all of which are recorded as non-current liabilities" (FY2025 10-K, p. 37).
  - The balance is $559m, an exposure equal to 3.0% of pre-tax income.
- **Stock compensation assumptions:** the option expected term went from 6 to 7 years, and the performance-award term from 5 to 4 years (FY2024 10-K, pp. 70, 72; FY2025 10-K, pp. 70, 72). Neither is discussed. Not quantified.
- **Adjusted measures:** the definition is word-for-word the same in both releases, which exclude only "amortization expense from acquired intangible assets" (release p. 8, both years). Nothing new is left out this year. The amount excluded grew from $425m (2.2% of fiscal 2024 pre-tax income) to $607m (3.3% of fiscal 2025's), and $683m is scheduled for fiscal 2026 (FY2025 10-K, p. 61).
