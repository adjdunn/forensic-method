**The Home Depot, Inc.: fiscal 2025 (52 weeks to 1 February 2026), compared with fiscal 2024 (53 weeks to 2 February 2025)**
Documents used: 10-K key sections for both years (Items 1, 1A, 3, 7, 8 and 9A), the Q4 results releases for both years, statements.xlsx, and the four earlier replies (two text track, two numbers track).

No item is at evidence level 1: neither track found a sentence contradicted by the company's own figures or by another sentence in the same report. All five items below are at level 2, where two or more signals point at the same account.

### Items to investigate

**1. Operating margin and the Primary segment's own margin**
- **Shown:** Operating margin fell from 13.49% to 12.68%, the third straight annual fall. Of the 0.81-point drop, 0.39 points come from the shift toward SRS and GMS, where "Other" earns 2.5%. The other 0.42 points come from the Primary segment's own margin, which went 14.21% → 13.92% → 13.54% (segment table, FY2025 10-K p. 55). The company explains this partly with "a non-recurring legal-related benefit recognized during fiscal 2024" (FY2025 10-K p. 34), but never gives its size. A year earlier it cited "lower legal-related benefits" (FY2024 10-K p. 34), so legal benefits appear in at least two years. Size: $1,334m, 7.2% of pre-tax income (income effect). Level 2.
- **Reading:** Possibly some weakening in the core business (numbers track). Primary sales fell from $153.1bn to $152.0bn and comparable sales grew only 0.3%. If the fiscal 2024 legal benefit turned out to be about 0.4% of Primary sales, the most likely reading would be ordinary.
- **Next:** The size of the fiscal 2023 and 2024 legal benefits, and Primary segment SG&A detail.

**2. Inventory build and a lower shrink estimate**
- **Shown:** Days inventory rose from 81.9 to 85.6 for the year and from 86.2 to 91.3 in Q4. The company's explanation, "higher average inventory levels" (p. 32), restates the measure rather than giving a cause. GMS inventory ($568m, p. 75) covers about half of the excess over fiscal 2024's days.
  - The shrink-estimating method is word-for-word the same in both years: "primarily based on recent shrink results" (p. 39 both). But the sensitivity to a 10% rise in the shrink rate fell from $95m to $79m, which implies the estimated shrink fell by about $160m.
  - The auditor dropped "Estimation of store shrink" as a critical audit matter (FY2024 10-K p. 42). The new one covers only the IT side of inventory (FY2025 10-K p. 41).
  - Size: about 0.9% of pre-tax income from the lower shrink estimate (income effect, rough). The excess inventory is $1,102m, which the numbers track showed as 5.9%, but that is a balance, not an income effect. Level 2.
- **Reading:** The two tracks differ. The numbers track says it can't tell whether this is deliberate stocking, possibly ahead of tariffs, or slower sell-through; comparable transactions fell 1.0%. The text track reads the shrink change as most likely ordinary, since lower losses fit the "lower shrink" credited for gross margin. Physical counts later showing losses above the estimate, or markdowns, would point to weakening.
- **Next:** Fiscal 2026 10-Q inventory by segment, true-ups from physical counts, and markdown or lower-of-cost reserve disclosures.

**3. Goodwill in SRS and GMS ("Other")**
- **Shown:** The SRS units moved from a qualitative test (FY2024 10-K p. 60) to their first quantitative test. Fair value "exceeded" carrying amount, with no headroom given, while the Primary segment's wording keeps "substantially exceeded" (FY2025 10-K p. 60). "Other" earned $316m of operating income (p. 55), roughly 3% before amortization on the about $23.5bn paid for SRS and GMS. Size: exposure of $13.8bn, a balance equal to 74% of pre-tax income. Level 2.
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents. A quantitative test in the year after an acquisition is the normal, more rigorous step. Leaving out "substantially" possibly means narrow headroom.
- **Next:** Headroom, discount rate and growth assumptions for each SRS unit, and the final GMS allocation, which is still preliminary (p. 75).

**4. Payables**
- **Shown:** Payables are 6.98% of sales against an industry level of 9.4%, and days payable fell from 41.7 to 38.1. The company cites "the timing of vendor payments" (FY2025 10-K p. 37), the same words it used a year earlier for the opposite effect (FY2024 10-K p. 38), with no amount either time. Supplier-finance obligations fell from $598m to $414m (p. 51). The explanation only partly holds. Size: a balance gap to the industry of $3,989m (21.4%), and a fiscal 2025 days-payable fall worth $1,090m (5.9%). Both are balances, not income effects. Level 2.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary (numbers track): the fiscal 2024 timing benefit unwound, and that year's 14-week fourth quarter left a high payables base. If days payable keep falling in fiscal 2026, that would point to suppliers being paid sooner, a weakening.
- **Next:** Fiscal 2026 10-Q payables, the supplier-finance roll-forward, and SRS and GMS payment terms.

**5. Customer receivables and trade credit**
- **Shown:** Days sales outstanding went from 7.7 to 12.4 between fiscal 2022 and fiscal 2025. Customer receivables rose 36.5% to $2,588m. The risk factor changed from customers who "primarily use trade credit" to customers who "increasingly use trade credit" (FY2024 10-K p. 23; FY2025 10-K p. 24). The sentence explaining the increase was dropped, and the allowance is still only "not material", with no amount (p. 48 both). Size: $438m above fiscal 2024's days, 2.4% of pre-tax income as a balance (the industry gap is $986m); not an income effect. Level 2.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary in both tracks. GMS brought $899m of receivables (p. 75), which covers the rise. The allowance itself can't be judged from what is disclosed.
- **Next:** The allowance amount and roll-forward, and write-offs under the Pro Trade Credit programme.

### Also noted
- **Adjusted measures** exclude amortization from acquired intangibles every year: $607m, 3.3% of pre-tax income (leans aggressive, read as ordinary; level 3). As an income effect this is larger than item 2's 0.9%.
- **Customer relationships** are amortized straight-line over 19–20 years while valued on attrition (leans aggressive; not quantified).
- **Smaller wording and assumption changes:**
  - Wording on unrecognized tax benefits changed; the balance is $559m (an exposure equal to 3.0% of pre-tax income).
  - The option expected term went from 6 to 7 years and the award term from 5 to 4 years, with no discussion.
  - A depreciation tie-out gap of $62m (pp. 47 and 58).
  - Gross reporting of arranged installation.
  - The self-insurance deferred-tax ratio fell (can't tell).
  - The GMS "approximately 3%" of assets can't be tested as stated.

### Checked and explained
These held when tested:
- Free cash flow below net income: a reversal of tax and payment timing.
- The fall in gross margin: SRS and GMS mix.
- The $6.3bn of acquired sales.
- Inventory turnover, ROIC and adjusted EPS, which all recompute.
- GMS at 1.2% of sales.
- The SRS purchase-price allocation, finalized with immaterial changes.
- Later revisions, which only reclassified figures and did not move income.

### Not examined
The documents had no related-party note, no Schedule II, no returns or doubtful-account reserve amounts, no capitalized interest or software amounts, and no amounts for the legal benefits.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
