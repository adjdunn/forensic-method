## 2. Write-ups

No claim I tested was contradicted, either in the audited statements or in the MD&A. Seven tests held:
- acquisitions added $6.3B of sales;
- GMS was about 1% of sales;
- inventory turnover was 4.4x;
- ROIC was 25.7%;
- lease payments total $15.9B;
- adjusted EPS of $14.69 reconciles;
- SRS/GMS amortization of about $395M matches the reported $398M.

That leaves one area that leans aggressive.

**Adjusted measures: a recurring amortization cost is left out**
- **The report says:** "excludes the impact of amortization expense from acquired intangible assets" and "We do not adjust for the revenue that is generated in part from the use of our acquired intangible assets" (earnings release, Non-GAAP Financial Measures, p.8).
- **Test:** The arithmetic holds: $607M ÷ 995M shares = $0.61 a share; tax at 23.9% = $0.15; $14.23 + $0.61 − $0.15 = $14.69. The cost recurs, though:
  - $425M in FY2024, $607M in FY2025, and $683M scheduled for FY2026 (p.61);
  - $9.7B still to amortize in total.

  Size: $607M, or 3.3% of pre-tax income; FY2026's scheduled amount is 3.7%.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary presentation rather than stretched reporting. The exclusion is disclosed and reconciled, it is common practice, and it is not called one-time. It still makes adjusted EPS about 3% higher every year while SRS and GMS are being amortized. A different reading would follow if the adjustments began to include other items, such as integration costs or impairments, or if management pay were tied to the adjusted figure.
- **Read next:** The proxy statement's incentive-pay metrics, and whether ROIC or other targets use adjusted operating income.

## 3. Also noted

- **Tie-out gaps:**
  - Depreciation (excluding intangibles) is $3,514M in the cash-flow statement (p.47) but $3,452M in Note 3 (p.58), a $62M gap.
  - The MD&A gives GMS cash consideration as "approximately $5.5 billion" (p.32); Note 13 adds to $4,257M + $824M + $354M = $5,435M (pp.73–74).
  - Stock-based compensation is $522M in the cash-flow statement and $524M in Note 9 (p.69).
  - The swap liability is $558M in Note 5 (p.64) and $559M in Note 8 (p.69).
- **Inventory:**
  - Inventory grew 10.1% against whole-company net sales growth of 3.2%; excluding GMS's $568M of acquired inventory, growth was 7.7%.
  - The 4.4x turnover holds only on a five-quarter average of inventory; a simple start-and-end average gives 4.5x.
  - A 10% rise in the shrink rate would cost $79M, or 0.4% of pre-tax (p.39). Inventory is the auditor's critical audit matter.
- **Customer-relationship lives:**
  - The lives are 19 years for GMS (p.74) and 20 years for SRS (p.76), at the top of the "up to approximately 20 years" policy (p.51).
  - The attrition rates behind them are not disclosed, so I can't judge them.
  - As an illustration only, a 15-year life would add about $99M of amortization, or 0.5% of pre-tax.
- **ROIC:** The denominator counts long-term debt and equity but leaves out $4.5B of commercial paper (p.35). Including the average commercial paper balance lowers ROIC from 25.7% to 24.7%.
