**Days inventory rose: 81.9 → 85.6 days (FY24–FY25), and 86.2 → 91.3 in Q4**
- **The company says:** inventory turnover fell, "primarily driven by higher average inventory levels" (FY25 10-K, p. 32), and operating cash fell partly from "increased inventories" (p. 37). Both sentences describe the rise without giving a reason. Last year was different: FY24 turnover rose on "lower average inventory" and "more normalized inventory levels" (FY24 10-K, pp. 32, 38).
- **Test:** The claim is about whole-company inventory in FY25, measured by turnover, and it restates the movement rather than explaining it. The year-end balance rose $2,366m. GMS brought $568m at acquisition (p. 74), which is 24% of the rise and 2.2% of the $25,817m balance. The cash-flow inventory build (whole company, excluding acquired balances) was $1,498m, 63% of the rise. Over the same year, Primary-segment cost of sales fell 1.5%. The company doesn't explain about three-quarters of the rise, so **the explanation does not hold**.
- **Reading:** Most likely you can't tell from these documents. The acquired GMS inventory sits against only about five months of GMS costs, but most of the build came outside the acquisition while Primary-segment volumes shrank. If the later quarters showed the build reversing, or the inventory turned out to be tariff-related buying ahead, that would point to ordinary timing.
- **Read next:** inventory by segment or by Primary/SRS (not disclosed), and the inventory policy and shrink-reserve sections (p. 49, pp. 38–39) for any aged-stock or markdown reserve.

**Operating margin fell: 15.3% → 12.7% (FY22–FY25), and 11.3% → 10.1% in Q4**
- **The company says:** gross margin fell from "the inclusion of SRS and GMS" (p. 34). SG&A rose because of "higher payroll and related costs" plus the "impact of a non-recurring legal-related benefit recognized during fiscal 2024" (p. 34). D&A rose on "increased intangible asset amortization… SRS and GMS" (p. 34). Last year's reasons were similar: FY24 named "higher payroll costs… lower legal-related benefits" and SRS (FY24 10-K, p. 34).
- **Test:** The consolidated FY25 fall was 0.81 points. Splitting it by segment (Primary vs. the SRS-based Other), 48% came from the move toward the lower-margin Other segment (2.5% margin vs. Primary's 13.5%). Higher acquired-intangible amortization ($607m vs. $425m) accounts for 0.10 points, or 13% of the fall. Primary's own margin also fell, 14.2% → 13.9% → 13.5%, worth $580m at FY25 Primary sales. Its gross margin actually rose (33.4% → 33.9% → 34.4%), while its SG&A rose from 17.4% to 19.0% of sales. Neither the payroll claim nor the legal benefit comes with an amount, so neither can be tested as stated. **The explanation partly holds.**
- **Reading:** Possibly the business weakening. Half the fall is acquisition mix, but the Primary segment's SG&A has outgrown its flat sales for two years in a row. If the FY24 legal benefit turned out to be large, much of the FY25 Primary fall would be a one-year comparison effect instead.
- **Read next:** the FY24 and FY25 SG&A components and the amount of the legal-related benefits (not disclosed in these excerpts).

**Days payable fell: 41.7 → 38.1 days (FY24–FY25), and 43.9 → 40.6 in Q4**
- **The company says:** operating cash fell partly from "the timing of vendor payments" (p. 37). Last year it used the same words in the opposite direction: "timing of vendor payments" helped FY24 cash flow (FY24 10-K, p. 38).
- **Test:** The claim is about vendor payment timing in FY25, but the company gives no figure for it. The nearest line, the change in accounts payable *and accrued expenses*, swung by $1,576m, but it also covers accrued expenses, so the claim can't be tested as stated. Supplier-finance obligations fell from $598m to $414m (p. 51). That $184m is 17% of the $1,090m shortfall and 1.6% of the $11,491m payables balance. Payables fell $447m even though GMS added $380m. FY25 days (38.1) are still above FY23 (35.9). **The explanation partly holds.**
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary: it looks like the FY24 timing gain reversing. If supplier terms or supplier-finance use were shrinking, a lasting fall would point to the business weakening.
- **Read next:** the supplier finance program note (p. 51) and payables by segment.

**Other flags**
- **Free cash flow below net income ($1,510m):** the company points to "timing of vendor payments and increased inventories" and "the deferral of our fourth quarter fiscal 2024 estimated federal tax payment" (p. 37). The three named cash-flow lines swung by $3,838m, which is 110% of the $3,485m fall in operating cash. Taken over FY24 and FY25 together, free cash flow of $28,971m equals net income of $28,962m (ratio 1.00). **Holds.** Most likely ordinary timing, although the inventory part feeds the inventory flag above.
- **Days sales outstanding up (7.9 → 12.4):** FY24 customer receivables rose "primarily as a result of the SRS acquisition" (FY24 10-K, p. 48). The FY25 10-K gives no reason (p. 48). Acquired receivables were SRS $1,831m (116% of the FY24 rise of $1,575m, 37% of the FY24 balance) and GMS $899m (p. 74; 130% of the FY25 rise of $694m, 16% of the FY25 balance). **Holds.** Most likely ordinary: these distributors sell on credit. The allowance is "not material" and no amount is given.

**Adjusted earnings:** The only adjustment is acquired-intangible amortization. Adjusted diluted EPS exceeded reported by $0.14 in FY23, $0.33 in FY24 and $0.46 in FY25 (0.9%, 2.2% and 3.2% of reported). Adjusted operating income exceeded reported by $425m in FY24 and $607m in FY25 (2.0% and 2.9%; releases p. 8).

Nothing in the releases is called non-recurring. But a "legal-related benefit" sits inside reported SG&A in both FY23 and FY24 (FY24 10-K p. 34: "lower legal-related benefits"), and the FY25 10-K calls the FY24 one "non-recurring" (p. 34). No amount is given in either year.

**Changes in estimate that lifted income:** None disclosed. Useful lives are the same in both years (5–45 years for buildings, 2–20 years for equipment; p. 49), and the depreciation rate barely moved (7.44% → 7.38%). "Lower shrink" is given as a reason for higher gross margin in both FY24 and FY25 (p. 34), but it isn't presented as a change in estimate and has no amount.

None of the figures above changes on the revised basis. The only revisions split intangible amortization out of cash-flow D&A and out of other assets, and no measure here uses either line.
