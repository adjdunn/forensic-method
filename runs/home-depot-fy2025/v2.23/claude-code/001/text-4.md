## 1. Write-ups

Both files are extracts of key sections (Items 1, 1A, 3, 7, 8 and 9A), and both contain the same sections, so every change below compares like with like. Pre-tax income for FY2025 was $18,602M.

**Critical estimates and auditor's report: smaller shrink estimate, and the auditor dropped shrink as a critical audit matter**
- **Before:** A 10% rise in the shrink rate "would have increased cost of sales by approximately $95 million for fiscal 2024" (FY2024 10-K, p.39). The auditor's critical audit matter (CAM) was "Estimation of store shrink," including "a sensitivity analysis over the shrink reserve estimate" (p.42).
- **After:** "approximately $79 million for fiscal 2025" (FY2025 10-K, p.39). The shrink CAM is gone. The only CAM now is "Sufficiency of audit evidence over certain merchandise inventories," an IT-systems matter (pp.41–42). The method sentence is unchanged: shrink between counts is "primarily based on recent shrink results" (p.39 both years).
- **Why it matters:** The estimated shrink behind the sensitivity fell about 17% (roughly $950M to $790M, taking the sensitivity × 10). Over the same year:
  - whole-company inventory grew 10.1%;
  - the MD&A credits "lower shrink" for supporting gross margin (p.34).

  Size: about $160M, or 0.9% of pre-tax income. This is rough: it assumes the sensitivity scales linearly.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. The estimate follows recent physical counts, and the shrink CAM may have been dropped simply because the estimate became less judgmental. A different reading would follow if later counts showed losses above the estimate, or if the shrink reduction came mostly from the estimated portion rather than the counted portion.

**MD&A, SG&A: a legal-related benefit is now called "non-recurring"**
- **Before:** SG&A rose as a share of sales because of "higher payroll costs, deleverage… and lower legal-related benefits" (FY2024 10-K, p.34). That wording means there were legal benefits in both FY2023 and FY2024.
- **After:** The rise reflects payroll "along with the impact of a non-recurring legal-related benefit recognized during fiscal 2024" (FY2025 10-K, p.34).
- **Why it matters:** The same FY2024 benefit, which last year's wording showed was smaller than FY2023's, is now labelled non-recurring, and no amount is given in either year. This year it partly explains why whole-company SG&A rose 6.8% and went from 18.0% to 18.6% of sales. Size: not quantified.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The benefit may be a genuine one-off settlement, but calling a benefit that appeared two years running "non-recurring" makes the FY2025 SG&A deleverage look more temporary. Amounts for each year, showing the FY2023 and FY2024 items were unrelated, would point to an ordinary reading.

**Debt note: the average commercial-paper table was dropped as borrowing rose**
- **Before:** A table showed the "Maximum amount outstanding during the period | $15,317" and "Average daily short-term borrowings | 1,343" (FY2024 10-K, p.61).
- **After:** The table is dropped. Only the "maximum amount outstanding during that period was $5.8 billion" remains (FY2025 10-K, p.61). Commercial paper is now also used "to support general liquidity, including the repayment of senior note maturities" (p.36).
- **Why it matters:** Year-end commercial paper rose 14.1-fold, from $316M to $4,464M. The average daily balance, which shows how much the company relies on commercial paper through the year, disappeared in the year that reliance grew. This also connects to my earlier point that ROIC excludes commercial paper. Size: a $4.5B balance. At the stated 3.7% rate that is about $165M a year, or 0.9% of pre-tax; this is a rough exposure, not an income effect.
- **Reading:** Possibly ordinary streamlining, since the maximum is still disclosed. But the drop coincides with heavier and more lasting use of commercial paper. Quarterly balances well below year-end would point to the ordinary reading.

## 2. Other changes

- **Trade credit:** The risk factor changed from "primarily use trade credit" (FY2024 p.23) to "increasingly use trade credit" (FY2025 p.24). Over the year, customer receivables rose 36.5%, from $1,896M to $2,588M, part of it from the GMS acquisition (GMS brought $899M of receivables of all kinds; the customer share isn't given). The allowance sentence is unchanged: "not material" (p.48 in both years).
- **Sales per square foot:** "Sales per retail square foot" was dropped from the MD&A table (FY2024 p.33, where it had fallen from $627.17 to $604.55 to $599.92). It does not appear in FY2025 (p.33).
- **Inventory cost method:** "primarily the first-in, first-out method" became "primarily the moving average cost and first-in, first-out methods" (p.49 in both years; Critical Estimates p.39 → p.38). The report does not call this a policy change.
- **Unremitted earnings and tax benefits:**
  - The figure "$5.1 billion of non-cash unremitted earnings" was dropped (p.67 in both years).
  - Unrecognized tax benefits changed from "none of which are expected to be paid in the next 12 months" to "nearly all of which are recorded as non-current" (p.37 in both years).
- **Segment table and non-GAAP:**
  - The Primary segment table no longer shows "Gross profit" or "Total operating expenses" (p.55 in both years).
  - FY2024 department sales were re-presented under the disclosed product realignment. For example, Storage & Organization went from $5,636M to $4,898M (p.57).
  - The non-GAAP definition in the results releases is word-for-word the same; nothing new is excluded.
  - Last year's guidance for the amortization add-back was "approximately $0.40" (FY2024 release, p.8). The actual was $0.46, because GMS was added.
