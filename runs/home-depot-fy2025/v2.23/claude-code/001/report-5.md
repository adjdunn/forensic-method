**The Home Depot, Inc.: fiscal 2025 (52 weeks to Feb 1, 2026), compared with fiscal 2024 (53 weeks to Feb 2, 2025)**
**Documents used:** key sections of the FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks, the Q4 results releases for both years, and the statements workbook. Pre-tax income for FY2025 was $18,602M.

### Items to investigate

**1. Operating margin and SG&A: the Primary segment's margin fell, and a legal-related benefit is now called "non-recurring"**
- **Shown:**
  - Operating margin fell from 15.3% in FY22 to 12.7% in FY25, and from 11.3% to 10.1% in Q4.
  - About half of the FY25 fall comes from mix: the SRS distribution business (the "Other" segment) earns a 2.5% margin.
  - The Primary segment's own margin fell from 14.2% to 13.5%, and its SG&A rose from 17.4% to 19.0% of sales (p.55).
  - Last year's report said FY24 had "lower legal-related benefits" than FY23 (FY24 p.34). This year's report calls the FY24 item "a non-recurring legal-related benefit" (FY25 p.34). Neither year gives an amount.

  Size, as an income effect: the margin shortfall is 7.2% of pre-tax ($1,334M); the Primary segment's own part is 3.1% ($580M). Evidence: level 2 (several signals on the same account).
- **Reading:** The text track can't tell from these documents what the relabelling means; the numbers track says the business is possibly weakening. If the FY24 benefit turns out to be large, the FY25 Primary fall would be mostly a one-year comparison effect.
- **Next:** Find the amounts of the FY23 and FY24 legal-related benefits and a breakdown of Primary-segment SG&A.

**2. Inventory and the shrink estimate: inventory grew faster than sales while estimated shrink fell**
- **Shown:**
  - Days inventory rose from 75.1 to 85.6 (FY23–FY25), and from 86.2 to 91.3 in Q4.
  - Inventory grew 10.1% against whole-company sales growth of 3.2%.
  - The company explains this only as "higher average inventory levels" (p.32). The numbers track found that about three-quarters of the $2,366M rise has no stated reason.
  - The shrink sensitivity fell from $95M to $79M (p.39), yet the shrink-estimate method wording is unchanged.
  - The auditor dropped its "Estimation of store shrink" critical audit matter (FY24 p.42).
  - The MD&A credits "lower shrink" for gross margin (p.34).
  - The 4.4x inventory turnover holds only on a five-quarter average.

  Size: the shrink estimate's income effect is about 0.9% of pre-tax ($160M, rough). The inventory above FY24 days is a balance of $1,102M; the numbers track put it at 5.9%, but it is not an income effect. Evidence: level 2.
- **Reading:** The two tracks differ. The text track reads the shrink change as most likely ordinary, with the estimate following lower counted losses. The numbers track can't tell from these documents why inventory built up. Signs that the build is reversing, or that it was tariff-related buying ahead, would point to ordinary timing.
- **Next:** Find inventory by segment, any aged-stock or markdown reserve, and the next physical-count true-ups.

**3. Commercial paper: borrowing rose sharply and the average-balance disclosure was dropped**
- **Shown:**
  - Year-end commercial paper rose from $316M to $4,464M. It is now also used for "general liquidity, including the repayment of senior note maturities" (p.36).
  - The table of average daily short-term borrowings was dropped (FY24 p.61).
  - ROIC leaves commercial paper out of capital; including it lowers ROIC from 25.7% to 24.7% (p.35).

  Size: exposure of $4.5B. At the stated 3.7% rate that is about $165M a year, roughly 0.9% of pre-tax (rough). Evidence: level 2.
- **Reading:** Possibly ordinary streamlining, since the maximum balance is still disclosed. But the table was dropped in the year reliance on commercial paper grew.
- **Next:** Find quarterly commercial-paper balances and whether ROIC targets in incentive pay use this definition.

**4. Customer receivables: up 36.5%, while the allowance is still "not material"**
- **Shown:**
  - Days sales outstanding rose from 7.7 to 12.4 (FY22–FY25), against an industry level of 10.2.
  - Customer receivables rose from $1,896M to $2,588M.
  - The risk factor changed from "primarily use trade credit" to "increasingly use trade credit" (FY24 p.23 → FY25 p.24).
  - The allowance is still "not material," with no figure given (p.48).

  Size: a balance of $438M above FY24 days (the numbers track's 2.4% is not an income effect). Evidence: level 2.
- **Reading:** The numbers track reads this as most likely ordinary, because the acquired receivables (SRS $1,831M, GMS $899M) explain the rises. The text track can't tell, because the allowance and write-offs are not disclosed.
- **Next:** Find the allowance amount, write-offs, and aging of the SRS/GMS credit book.

**5. Adjusted EPS leaves out a recurring cost**
- **Shown:** Adjusted operating income and EPS exclude acquired-intangible amortization while keeping the revenue those intangibles help produce. The excluded amount was $607M in FY25 and is scheduled at $683M for FY26 (release p.8; 10-K p.61). The definition is unchanged from last year. The FY25 add-back was $0.46 a share, against guidance of about $0.40. Size: income effect of 3.3% of pre-tax ($607M). Evidence: level 3 (a single measure). This is a larger income effect than items 2–4, but it rests on weaker evidence.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary presentation: the exclusion is disclosed, reconciled and common practice. It still adds about 3% to adjusted EPS every year.
- **Next:** Check whether incentive-pay metrics in the proxy use adjusted figures.

### Also noted
- SRS goodwill ($13.8B balance) passed the impairment test with no headroom disclosed, and the Other segment earns a 2.5% margin (p.60).
- Customer-relationship lives of 19–20 years sit at the top of the policy range, and the attrition rates behind them are not disclosed.
- The finance-lease weighted average remaining term stayed at 12 years while the latest lease end date moved from 2055 to 2075; this does not fit, roughly tested (p.59, p.62).
- Several disclosures were dropped:
  - sales per square foot, after two years of decline;
  - the $5.1B unremitted-earnings figure.
- Some assumptions or wording changed without being presented as changes:
  - the option expected term moved from 6 to 7 years;
  - "moving average cost" was added to the inventory cost method.
- Tie-out gaps:
  - depreciation differs by $62M between the cash-flow statement and Note 3;
  - GMS consideration is "approximately $5.5 billion" in the MD&A against $5,435M in the note;
  - stock compensation is $522M against $524M;
  - swap liabilities are $558M against $559M.
- Days payable fell from 41.7 to 38.1, and supplier-finance obligations fell from $598M to $414M; the numbers track reads this as most likely the FY24 timing gain reversing.

### Checked and explained
These looked unusual but held when tested:
- Free cash flow fell below net income, but over FY24 and FY25 together the ratio is 1.00, explained by tax-payment and working-capital timing.
- Acquisitions added $6.3B of sales, as stated.
- GMS is about 1% of sales (1.2%), as stated in the controls section.
- ROIC and adjusted EPS arithmetic reconcile.
- Lease totals reconcile ($15.9B).
- SRS/GMS amortization matches the stated lives.
- Property depreciation rate is stable (7.44% → 7.38%), with useful lives unchanged.
- Self-insurance is recorded undiscounted, which is the conservative choice.

### Not examined
The documents had no related-party note, no Schedule II, no allowance or returns-reserve amounts, no software or capitalized-interest amounts, and no total store square footage.

Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.
