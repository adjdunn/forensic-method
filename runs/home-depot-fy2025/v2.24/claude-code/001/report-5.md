**The Home Depot, Inc.: fiscal 2025 (year to 1 Feb 2026), compared with fiscal 2024 (year to 2 Feb 2025)**
*Documents used: 10-K key sections for both years, the Q4 results releases for both years, the statements workbook, and the earlier replies in this conversation (two text-track, two numbers-track). Pre-tax income is $18,602m. Page numbers are the `[p. N]` markers in the text files.*

No item reaches level 1: no sentence is contradicted by the company's own numbers within the same report.

### Items to investigate

**1. Operating margin and Primary segment costs, including the legal benefit now called "non-recurring"**
- **Shown:** Whole-company operating margin fell from 15.3% to 14.2%, 13.5% and then 12.7%, and the Q4 margin from 11.9% to 11.3% and then 10.1%. The company's explanations only partly hold. The shift toward lower-margin SRS/GMS explains about 48% of the latest fall. The Primary segment's own margin fell from 14.2% to 13.5% while its sales fell 0.7%. The FY2026 10-K blames part of the SG&A rise on "a non-recurring legal-related benefit recognized during fiscal 2024" (p. 34). The FY2025 10-K had cited "lower legal-related benefits" (p. 34), which puts such benefits in both FY2023 and FY2024. No amount is given in either year. Size: $1,334m (7.2%) for the whole-company fall, of which the Primary segment's own decline is $580m (3.1%). Ranks on 7.2%, an income effect. Level 2.
- **Reading:** The two tracks differ. The numbers track reads this as possibly the business weakening: three years of SG&A deleverage in the core segment. The text track can't tell whether "non-recurring" fits. A large, genuinely one-off FY2024 benefit would move both readings toward ordinary.
- **Next:** The legal-related benefit amounts for FY2023–FY2025, and Primary SG&A by component.

**2. Acquired-intangible amortization: excluded from adjusted results and spread over 19–20 years**
- **Shown:** Adjusted operating income and adjusted EPS leave out acquired-intangible amortization (release, p. 8). That is $607m this year, up from $425m. It recurs: $209m of it comes from acquisitions before SRS (p. 56), and fiscal 2026 guidance excludes about $0.50 a share again. Fiscal 2025 guidance had flagged $0.40 a share; the actual was $0.46. Customer relationships are amortized over 20 years (SRS, p. 75) and 19 years (GMS, p. 74), at the top of common practice, with no attrition rate disclosed. Size: 3.3% (amount excluded from adjusted results). A 15-year life would cost about 0.5% more a year (hypothetical, based on general practice). Ranks on 3.3%. Level 2. The definition itself did not change.
- **Reading:** The adjustment is most likely ordinary: it is clearly labelled and fully reconciled. The lives are can't tell. A disclosed attrition rate that supports 20-year relationships would settle the lives.
- **Next:** The final GMS purchase price allocation; the attrition assumptions; whether the proxy statement's pay metrics use adjusted figures.

**3. Inventory: stock built up while the auditor's shrink focus was dropped**
- **Shown:** Days inventory rose from 81.9 to 85.6, and in Q4 from 86.2 to 91.3. The stated reason, "higher average inventory levels" (p. 32), describes the build rather than explaining it. GMS accounts for only 24% of the $2,366m rise. The auditor dropped its "Estimation of store shrink" critical audit matter (FY2025, p. 42) in favour of one on IT and the sufficiency of audit evidence (FY2026, pp. 41–42). The shrink sensitivity fell from $95m to $79m (p. 39). The cost-method wording added "moving average cost" (p. 49) with no effect stated. Size: the income effect is the shrink sensitivity, 0.4%. The $1,102m excess over prior-year days (5.9%, or 7.8% on Q4) is a **balance**. Ranks on 0.4%. Level 2.
- **Reading:** The two tracks differ. The numbers track can't tell, with possibly the business weakening (inventory up while Primary sales fell). The text track reads the auditor change as most likely ordinary. Write-downs called "not material" (p. 49) and the lower shrink point toward stock-building rather than a valuation problem.
- **Next:** Year-end inventory by segment, GMS's year-end inventory, and the history of shrink true-ups.

**4. Customer receivables and trade credit: growth with softer wording**
- **Shown:** Customer receivables rose from $1,896m to $2,588m (+36.5%) against 3.2% growth in sales. Days sales outstanding rose from 11.4 to 12.4, above the industry level. Last year's sentence putting the rise down to an acquisition was dropped (p. 48). The risk factor changed from "an increase in our losses" to "losses", and from "primarily" to "increasingly use trade credit" (FY2025 pp. 21, 23 → FY2026 pp. 21, 24). The allowance is "not material" in both years, with no amount. Size: not quantified as income. $2,588m is an **exposure**; the excess over prior-year days is $438m (2.4%), a **balance**. Level 2.
- **Reading:** Both tracks read this as most likely ordinary: GMS's $899m of acquired receivables more than covers the rise. If GMS's year-end receivables were well below $899m, the existing businesses would be building receivables.
- **Next:** Receivables by business at year end; the allowance amount and write-offs.

**5. SRS goodwill: test passed, but no headroom given**
- **Shown:** SRS reporting units "exceeded" carrying amount, while the Primary units "substantially exceeded" theirs (p. 60). The Other segment's operating margin is 2.5%. GMS goodwill wasn't tested because of how close the acquisition was to the test date. Size: $13.8bn **balance**. Level 3.
- **Reading:** Can't tell: not enough disclosed. A disclosed headroom percentage and the discount-rate assumptions would settle it.
- **Next:** Fiscal 2026 impairment disclosures, and SRS segment results.
