## 1. Write-ups

Neither report discloses a change in an estimate or policy that raised income, so the first group is empty. The three write-ups below are two pieces of softened or reframed wording, then one change in the auditor's report.

**Risk factors: trade-credit wording softened while customer credit grew**
- **Before:** "If our Pros are unable to make their payments, we may experience **an increase in our** losses" (FY2025 10-K, p. 21). Specialty trade and MRO customers "**primarily** use trade credit" (p. 23).
- **After:** "we may experience losses" (FY2026 10-K, p. 21). These customers "**increasingly** use trade credit" (p. 24).
- **Why it matters:** The sentence now describes rising use of credit, yet it drops the warning that losses could rise. Meanwhile customer receivables rose 36.5%, from $1,896m to $2,588m, against 3.2% growth in whole-company net sales. The receivables allowance is still only "not material" in both years (p. 48). Size: not quantified. The $2,588m is an **exposure**.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. The receivables rise matches the GMS acquisition, which brought $899m of receivables, and "increasingly" itself signals more credit use. If a later report adds an allowance amount or shows write-offs growing faster than these receivables, the softening would look premature.

**MD&A expense explanation: a legal benefit is now called "non-recurring"**
- **Before:** fiscal 2024 SG&A rose partly because of "**lower legal-related benefits**" (FY2025 10-K, p. 34). That wording means legal benefits were recognized in fiscal 2023 and again, smaller, in fiscal 2024.
- **After:** SG&A rose because of "the impact of a **non-recurring** legal-related benefit recognized during fiscal 2024" (FY2026 10-K, p. 34).
- **Why it matters:** Read together, the two reports put legal-related benefits in SG&A in two consecutive years. This year the fiscal 2024 one is labelled non-recurring, and neither year gives an amount. Primary segment SG&A rose $1,063m (3.8%), and part of that is attributed to this item. Size: not quantified.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The benefits may be distinct, one-off settlements, which would make the wording ordinary. Amounts for fiscal 2023, 2024 and 2025 showing a similar benefit most years would mean the label puts recurring income in a one-time frame.

**Auditor's report: the shrink critical audit matter was dropped and replaced**
- **Before:** two critical audit matters. One was SRS customer-relationship values; the other was "**Estimation of store shrink**", which included "Performing a sensitivity analysis over the shrink reserve estimate" (FY2025 10-K, p. 42).
- **After:** one critical audit matter, "**Sufficiency of audit evidence over certain merchandise inventories**". Its focus is "the highly automated nature of the process", not the shrink estimate (FY2026 10-K, pp. 41–42).
- **Why it matters:** The shrink estimate is unchanged in method ("primarily based on recent shrink results", p. 39 in both years). But its stated sensitivity fell from $95m to $79m (−17%). That implies a smaller estimated shrink amount, consistent with the MD&A's "lower shrink", and the auditor no longer singles it out. Size: not quantified. Inventory is a $25.8bn **balance**.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. The estimate got smaller, and the new focus fits the "business transformation initiative… migrating certain accounting and finance systems" in the controls section (p. 77). A different reading would follow if the 10-Qs show shrink true-ups, meaning differences between estimated and counted shrink, moving against the company.

## 2. Other changes
- **Adjusted measures (release, p. 8 in both years):** The definition is unchanged; the only new wording is "should not be considered in isolation". The amount excluded rose from $425m to $607m (+43%) and now includes GMS amortization. Fiscal 2025 guidance had excluded about $0.40 a share after tax; the actual was $0.46. Fiscal 2026 guidance excludes about 40 basis points of margin and about $0.50 a share. Size: 3.3% of pre-tax income.
- **Unremitted foreign earnings:** "substantially all **of the $5.1 billion** of non-cash unremitted earnings" became "substantially all non-cash unremitted earnings". The amount is no longer given (p. 67 in both years). The deferred tax liability can't be estimated either way ("impracticable").
- **Inventory cost method:** "primarily the first-in, first-out method" became "primarily the **moving average cost and** first-in, first-out methods" (Note 1 p. 49 in both years; critical estimates p. 39 → p. 38). No effect is stated, and the report doesn't say whether this is a new method or a fuller description.
- **Prior-year sales restated by department:** fiscal 2024 Storage & Organization is now $4,898m, previously $5,636m; Kitchen & Blinds is $7,889m, previously $7,238m; Hardlines is $47,721m, previously $48,562m (pp. 56–57 in both years). The report calls this a realignment of product categories with "no impact on consolidated net sales".
- **Risk factors:**
  - Dropped: suppliers "involved in litigation or governmental enforcement actions related to… PFAS" (FY2025 p. 22).
  - Added: liability from delivery operations (FY2026 pp. 22–23).
  - Added: "Sustained periods without such events [storms] can lead, and in the past have led, to lower sales" (FY2026 p. 22).
  - Dropped from the tax note: the statement that it is "reasonably possible" unrecognized tax benefits fall (FY2025 p. 67), even though they then fell 10.8%.
