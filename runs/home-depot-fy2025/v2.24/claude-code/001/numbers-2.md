**Inventory grew faster than cost of sales** (whole company; days inventory 81.9 → 85.6 in the year to 1 Feb 2026, and 86.2 → 91.3 in Q4)
- **The company says:** "primarily driven by higher average inventory levels during fiscal 2025" (FY2026 10-K, p. 32), and in the cash flow discussion, "increased inventories" (p. 37). Both describe the movement but give no reason for it. Last year the explanation was different: turnover rose on "lower average inventory levels within our Primary segment" (FY2025 10-K, p. 32).
- **Test:** The only cause the documents show is GMS, which brought in $568m of inventory on 4 Sep 2025 (p. 74). That is 24% of the $2,366m rise, 52% of the $1,102m excess over the prior year's days, and 2.2% of the year-end balance. GMS's balance at year end isn't disclosed. Without the acquired amount, inventory still rose 7.7% against 3.4% growth in cost of sales, and days inventory would be 83.7. **The explanation partly holds.**
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents. Possibly the business weakening: inventory built while Primary segment sales fell 0.7%. Write-downs on cost-method inventory were "not material" (p. 49), and the shrink sensitivity fell from $95m to $79m. Both point toward ordinary stock-building rather than a valuation problem.
- **Read next:** Inventory by segment (Primary vs SRS/GMS) at year end, and the Q4 10-Q-level commentary. Neither is in these documents.

**Operating margin fell** (whole company: 14.2% → 13.5% → 12.7%, FY2023 to FY2025)
- **The company says:** It explains each line. Gross margin fell on "the inclusion of SRS and GMS" (p. 34). SG&A rose on "higher payroll and related costs" and a "non-recurring legal-related benefit recognized during fiscal 2024" (p. 34). D&A rose on "increased intangible asset amortization… SRS and GMS" (p. 34). Last year's reasons were similar: SRS inclusion, payroll, deleverage and "lower legal-related benefits" (FY2025 10-K, p. 34).
- **Test:** By segment (p. 55), the shift toward the lower-margin Other businesses (2.5% operating margin) explains about 0.39 points of the 0.81-point fall (48%). Intangible amortization explains 0.10 points (13%), and most of that sits inside the mix effect. The Primary segment's own margin fell too, from 14.2% to 13.9% to 13.5%; that fall is worth about $580m, 44% of the $1,334m size. Primary SG&A rose from 18.2% to 19.0% of sales, and the size of the legal benefit isn't disclosed. **The explanation partly holds.**
- **Reading:** Possibly the business weakening. The core segment's margin has fallen three years running on SG&A deleverage, with Primary sales down 0.7% (the earlier year included a 53rd week). If the undisclosed FY2024 legal benefit was large, the reading would move toward ordinary.
- **Read next:** The amount of the FY2024 legal-related benefit, and Primary SG&A by component.

**Receivables grew faster than sales** (whole company; days sales outstanding 11.4 → 12.4; customer receivables $1,896m → $2,588m)
- **The company says:** Nothing about why receivables rose. The note says only "credit extended directly to certain customers in the ordinary course of business" (FY2026 10-K, p. 48), so silence is the finding. Last year it was different: they "increased… primarily as a result of the SRS acquisition" (FY2025 10-K, p. 48).
- **Test:** GMS brought $899m of receivables at acquisition (p. 74). That is 130% of the $694m whole-company rise and 16% of the year-end balance. Without it, days sales outstanding would be 10.4, below last year's 11.4. GMS's year-end balance isn't disclosed, so the claim can't be tested exactly. **The acquisition explanation holds, though the company doesn't make it.**
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. Only about five months of GMS sales ($2.0bn, p. 75) sit against its full receivables balance. If GMS's year-end receivables were well below $899m, that would point to receivables building in the existing businesses.
- **Read next:** Receivables by business at year end, and any change in the allowance (only "not material" is given).

**The other flags**
- **Free cash flow below net income** (gap $1,510m): the company points to "timing of vendor payments and increased inventories" and the "deferral of our fourth quarter fiscal 2024 estimated federal tax payment" (p. 37; the deferral is also confirmed in the FY2025 10-K, p. 67). The swings in payables, inventory and income taxes payable add up to $3,838m, 110% of the $3,485m fall in operating cash flow. **This holds.** Most likely ordinary timing: tax and payables that helped FY2024 reversed.
- **Payables fell behind cost of sales** (days payable 41.7 → 38.1): the company cites "timing of vendor payments" (p. 37), the same reason that helped last year. Excluding the $380m GMS brought in, payables fell $827m, and the supplier finance program fell from $598m to $414m (3.6% of payables, p. 51). **This partly holds.** Most likely ordinary, with FY2024 ending on a 53rd week.

**Revised figures:** none of my flagged figures change on the revised basis. The revisions were reclassifications only, and I took depreciation from the property note.

**Adjusted earnings:** The only adjustment is acquired-intangible amortization. Adjusted EPS was $0.33 above reported EPS in FY2024 ($15.24 vs $14.91, 2.2%) and $0.46 above in FY2025 ($14.69 vs $14.23, 3.2%), on $425m and $607m of pre-tax amortization. Neither release labels any item "non-recurring", "one-time" or "unusual". The 10-Ks do call the FY2024 legal-related benefit "non-recurring" (p. 34), yet the FY2024 report shows legal-related benefits in FY2023 too ("lower legal-related benefits"). Its amount isn't disclosed, and it isn't excluded from adjusted figures.

**Changes in estimate that lifted income:** none disclosed. The useful-life tables are the same in both years (p. 49). The shrink sensitivity fell from $95m to $79m, which fits the "lower shrink" cited for gross margin, but it isn't presented as a change in estimate.
