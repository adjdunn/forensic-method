**Claims I tested, all of which held:**
- Acquisitions added "$6.3 billion" of sales: Other net sales rose $6,311m.
- Inventory turnover of 4.4x versus 4.7x: 4.36x and 4.67x, using an average of five quarter-end balances.
- The gross-margin dip comes from SRS and GMS: the Primary segment's margin rose from 33.9% to 34.4%, while Other's is 20.6%.
- The rise in D&A is "primarily" intangible amortization: $182m of the $239m increase (76%).
- Operating cash flow fell "$3.5bn": the drop was $3,485m.
- GMS is "approximately 1%" of net sales: $2.0bn is 1.2%.

None was contradicted.

## 2. Write-ups

No claim is contradicted in the audited statements, the MD&A or the adjusted measures. The two write-ups below are the areas that lean aggressive.

**Adjusted measures: a recurring amortization charge is left out**
- **The report says:** adjusted results exclude "amortization expense from acquired intangible assets", and fiscal 2026 guidance "excludes an expected after-tax impact of approximately $0.50" (release, p. 8).
- **Test:** The claim is that adjusted EPS of $14.69 for fiscal 2025 equals GAAP EPS of $14.23 plus $0.61 of amortization, less $0.15 of tax. Recomputed: $607m ÷ 995m diluted shares = $0.61, and the tax effect at the 23.9% rate is $0.15, so the arithmetic holds. On recurrence: $209m of the $607m (34%) is amortization charged in the Primary segment from acquisitions other than SRS (Note 2, p. 56), and the charge is excluded again in 2026 guidance. **Size:** $607m = 3.3% of pre-tax income, which lifts adjusted EPS 3.2% above GAAP EPS.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. There is one clearly labelled adjustment, it is reconciled to GAAP, and GAAP figures come first. The profile marks it aggressive because the cost recurs every year, not because anything is hidden. A different reading would be warranted if adjusted figures drive pay, or if more items join the reconciliation later.
- **Read next:** The proxy statement's incentive metrics, and whether later releases add items to the reconciliation.

**Intangible lives: customer relationships amortized over 19–20 years**
- **The report says:** "Customer relationships | 20" for SRS (Note 13, p. 75) and "| 19" for GMS (p. 74). Straight-line amortization "approximates the pattern of expected economic benefit" (p. 51).
- **Test:** Nothing to test. The report names "customer attrition rates" as a key assumption but does not disclose them. For context: remaining amortization of $9,680m ÷ the fiscal 2026 charge of $683m ≈ 14 years, with 67% of it falling after fiscal 2030 (p. 61). **Size:** if the lives were 15 years, the charge would be about $99m higher, 0.5% of pre-tax income (hypothetical, based on general practice).
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The lives are at the top of the usual range for distributor customer lists. Also, this charge is excluded from adjusted results, so its length only affects GAAP earnings. A disclosed attrition rate that supports relationships of 20 years or more would make the choice ordinary.
- **Read next:** The final GMS purchase price allocation in fiscal 2026 filings, and any disclosure of attrition or retention assumptions.

## 3. Also noted

- **Tie-out gaps:**
  - Depreciation excluding intangibles is $3,514m in the cash flow statement (p. 47) but $3,452m in Note 3 (p. 58), a $62m gap.
  - Stock-based compensation is $522m in the equity statement and cash flow but $524m in Note 9 (p. 69).
  - Derivative liabilities are $558m in Note 5 (p. 64) but $559m in Note 8 (p. 69).
- **ROIC definition:** it uses "long-term debt (including current installments) and equity" (p. 35) and leaves out $4.5bn of commercial paper. Adding the average commercial paper balance lowers ROIC from 25.7% to 24.7%.
- **GMS in the controls exclusion:** the 10-K says GMS is "approximately 3%" of assets excluding goodwill and intangibles (p. 77). Its acquisition-date tangible assets were $2,776m, which is 3.8% of the year-end base. This can't be tested as stated, because GMS's year-end balances aren't given.
- **GMS notes:** $354m of GMS notes repaid at closing are shown as an assumed liability, with the repayment in financing cash flows (p. 74). Yet the MD&A counts that repayment within "aggregate cash consideration totaling approximately $5.5 billion" (p. 32).
