## 2. Write-ups

No claim was contradicted, either in the audited statements and notes or in the management discussion. Two areas lean aggressive.

**Adjusted measures: a recurring amortization charge is excluded**
- **The report says:** "excludes the impact of amortization expense from acquired intangible assets … We do not adjust for the revenue" (earnings release, p.8).
- **Test:** The reconciliation itself holds. $607m ÷ 995m diluted shares = $0.61, and $0.61 × the 23.9% effective tax rate = $0.15, giving adjusted EPS of $14.69. The charge clearly recurs: the schedule shows $683m for fiscal 2026 and "$6,476" million thereafter (10-K p.61). Size: $607m, 3.3% of pre-tax income ($18,602m).
- **Reading:** Most likely an ordinary presentation choice, common among acquirers and fully reconciled. It is slightly stretched, though, because revenue from the acquired customers stays in while their cost comes out. A different reading would follow if the adjusted figure were used to judge whether SRS/GMS earn back what was paid for them. On a GAAP basis, Other earned only $316m on $12.7bn of sales (p.55).
- **Read next:** Pay targets tied to adjusted metrics, and SRS/GMS profit before amortization against the $23.1bn paid.

**Acquired intangibles: lives at the top of the stated range**
- **The report says:** straight-line amortization is used "as this approximates the pattern of expected economic benefit" (Note 1, p.51). Yet the customer-relationship valuations rest on "expected customer attrition rates" (p.74, 76).
- **Test:** The claim about the benefit pattern can't be tested, because the attrition rates are not disclosed. The charge does match the stated lives. SRS at $346m a year plus about five months of GMS gives roughly $395m, against $398m reported (release p.8). Size, illustrative and not a finding: lives of 15 years would have added about $99m (0.5% of pre-tax income), and 10 years about $300m (1.6%).
- **Reading:** Possibly mildly stretched. Under attrition, benefits usually fall over time, so a 20-year straight line most likely pushes expense later. The table labels this choice "leans aggressive", but the effect may be small. If the undisclosed attrition rates are low (sticky trade customers), 19–20 years would be ordinary.
- **Read next:** The attrition and discount rates used for SRS and GMS, and how the GMS lives finish once the measurement period closes.

## 3. Also noted
- **Tie-out gaps:** depreciation is $3,514m in the cash flow statement (p.47) but $3,452m in Note 3 (p.58), a $62m gap. Stock compensation is $522m (p.47) against $524m in Note 9 (p.69). The swap liability is $558m in Note 5 (p.64) against $559m in Note 8 (p.69).
- **GMS controls exclusion:** the "approximately 1%" of net sales holds ($2.0bn ÷ $164.7bn = 1.2%). The "approximately 3%" of assets can't be tested as stated: the only GMS asset figures are at the acquisition date, and those give 3.8% of year-end assets excluding goodwill and intangibles (p.74, 77).
- **SRS goodwill headroom:** Other earns a 2.5% operating margin, and 1.4% pre-tax on the $23.1bn paid for SRS and GMS. It carries $13.8bn of goodwill, tested with the word "exceeded" rather than the "substantially exceeded" used for Primary (p.55, 60).
- **Return on invested capital:** invested capital leaves out the $4.5bn of commercial paper outstanding at year-end. Including it gives 24.7% instead of the 25.7% reported (p.35, 61).
