No disclosed change in an estimate or policy lifted income. The two write-ups below are a change in the auditor's report and a reworded explanation in the management discussion. "Last year" is the 10-K for fiscal 2024 (year ended 2 February 2025); "this year" is the 10-K for fiscal 2025. Pages are the documents' `[p. N]` markers. All six required sections are in both files, so every comparison below could be made.

## 1. Write-ups

**Auditor's report: the store-shrink critical audit matter was dropped and replaced with one on IT evidence**
- **Before:** two critical audit matters. One was the SRS customer-relationship valuation. The other was "Estimation of store shrink", covering shrink "occurring in the period between physical inventory counts and fiscal year-end" and including "a sensitivity analysis over the shrink reserve estimate" (last year's report, p.41–42).
- **After:** dropped. The only matter now is "Sufficiency of audit evidence over certain merchandise inventories". It concerns the "highly automated nature of the process" for retail-method inventory, which runs across multiple IT systems (this year's report, p.41–42).
- **Why it matters:** the shrink estimate method reads word for word the same in both years: "store-specific basis and is primarily based on recent shrink results" (p.39 both years). The disclosed sensitivity to a 10% rise in the shrink rate fell from "$95 million" (last year, p.39) to "$79 million" (this year, p.39). That implies the estimated shrink between counts fell about 17%, from roughly $950m to $790m (rough). The management discussion credits "lower shrink" for gross margin (p.34). Size: exposure of $79m, 0.4% of pre-tax income ($18,602m); last year $95m, 0.5% of $19,406m. Not an income effect.
- **Reading:** Most likely ordinary. Critical audit matters change, and retail-method inventory is still covered under a new angle. The SRS matter fell away because that valuation is finished. A different reading would be possible if the lower shrink estimate came from changing what counts as "recent shrink results" rather than from fewer actual losses. Neither report gives the shrink reserve balance or how far estimates differed from actual counts.

**Management discussion, SG&A: the fiscal 2024 legal benefit is now called "non-recurring"**
- **Before:** fiscal 2024 SG&A rose as a share of sales because of "higher payroll costs, deleverage from a negative comparable sales environment and lower legal-related benefits" (last year's report, p.34).
- **After:** fiscal 2025 SG&A rose because of "higher payroll and related costs in fiscal 2025 along with the impact of a non-recurring legal-related benefit recognized during fiscal 2024 within our Primary segment" (this year's report, p.34).
- **Why it matters:** Last year's wording implies legal-related benefits in both fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024. This year the fiscal 2024 benefit is called non-recurring, but neither report gives an amount. It is used to explain an SG&A ratio that rose 0.62 points company-wide (about $1.0bn at fiscal 2025 sales). In the Primary segment the ratio rose 0.84 points, from 18.2% to 19.0% (about $1.3bn). Size: not quantified.
- **Reading:** Can't tell. The fiscal 2024 benefit may well have been one-off, and the two sentences don't strictly conflict, since a smaller benefit can still be non-recurring. If legal benefits turn out to arrive most years, calling this one "non-recurring" makes the fiscal 2025 cost increase look more temporary than it is. Pointing the other way would be an amount for the benefit, or a statement that no similar benefits came in fiscal 2023 or fiscal 2025.

**Building on the last reply:**
- **Adjusted measures:** the definition is word for word the same in both releases (p.8 each). The only wording change in that section adds "considered in isolation or as" to the standard caution. The measures leave out nothing new this year. The excluded amortization grew from $425m (2.2% of fiscal 2024 pre-tax income) to $607m (3.3% of fiscal 2025). After tax it came to $0.46 a share, against the $0.40 guided last year (last year's release, p.8), most likely because GMS was added.
- **Intangible lives:** the SRS lives are unchanged at 20 and 5 years. GMS adds 19 and 7.

## 2. Other changes
- **Inventory cost method:** "primarily the first-in, first-out method" (last year p.39, p.49) became "primarily the moving average cost and first-in, first-out methods" (this year p.38, p.49). No change is disclosed and no effect is stated; possibly it now describes the SRS and GMS stock. Not quantified.
- **Uncertain tax positions:**
  - The sentence that it is "reasonably possible" exam settlements and expiring time limits "could reduce our unrecognized tax benefits by an immaterial amount" was dropped (last year p.67). In fiscal 2025, settlements and expiries actually cut them by $75m: 12% of the opening $627m, and 0.4% of pre-tax income.
  - In the management discussion, "none of which are expected to be paid in the next 12 months" (last year p.37) became "nearly all of which are recorded as non-current liabilities" (this year p.37).
- **Trade-credit risk factor:** Pros "primarily use trade credit" (last year p.23) became "increasingly use trade credit" (this year p.24). Customer receivables rose 36% to $2,588m (balance), with $899m acquired with GMS. The allowance is still described only as "not material" (p.48 both years).
- **Legal proceedings and risk factors:**
  - The EPA matter went from "cannot predict the amount" (last year p.29) to a final payment of about $1.7m, recovered from installers, with the decree terminated (this year p.29).
  - The Washington Department of Ecology penalty of about $1.6m was dropped.
  - The PFAS supplier-litigation sentence was dropped (last year p.22) and appears nowhere in this year's report.
  - A new sentence on storms was added: "Sustained periods without such events can lead, and in the past have led, to lower sales" (this year p.22).
- **Indefinite-lived intangibles:** the annual test was described as "using a quantitative analysis" (last year p.61) and is now "based on quantitative factors" (this year p.61). Not quantified.
