**First gather the candidates.** Go through all four replies, including rows in tables and one-line items, and collect:
- every claim whose test did not hold, only partly held or could not be tested as stated;
- every area judged to lean aggressive;
- every flag the company did not explain;
- every row of the statements table whose "still fits" answer is anything other than yes (no, can't tell, rough), and every change in wording or estimate written up;
- every item with a reading other than ordinary, including "can't tell".

A tie-out gap between two figures for the same item, and a prior-year figure that the report itself explains as a revision or reclassification, are not signals: they stay under "Also noted".

Merge items about the same account or the same estimate into one, and keep all of its evidence: a statement that stayed the same while its number moved belongs with the estimate it describes, and a flag from the numbers track belongs with the note that explains it in the text track. Each merged item names, in its Shown line, the replies and lines it absorbed (for example "from text-4 write-up 1 and table rows 1 and 4"), so nothing is dropped silently, and it carries the company's own stated reason for an estimate change whenever an earlier reply quoted one.

**Carry over the size and the reading.** Each earlier reply gives a size on the same base: the effect on the latest year's reported pre-tax income, as a share of that income, with balances and exposures labelled as such. Take each item's size from the reply that gave it; calculate the share in code only where a reply gives the amount without the share. Where two replies size the same account differently, give both and say which is the income effect. Rank on the larger income effect; a balance or exposure never ranks the item. A gap between a reported figure and an adjusted one is not an income effect: label it "excluded from adjusted income", rank it after income effects at its level as a balance is, and size the item on the amount the contradicted sentence covers. Where no amount was put on an item, write "not quantified". Each earlier reply also chose a reading. Use it; don't choose again. Where the two tracks read the same account differently, give both readings and say so: that disagreement is itself worth the analyst's time.

**Rank the items by strength of evidence first, then by size within each level.**

Strength of evidence, strongest first:
1. A sentence contradicted by the company's own numbers, or by another sentence in the same report. The sentence and the number must share subject, period and measure, and the number must point the opposite way. A sentence the numbers only fail to account for in full, or a sentence about a different measure, is level 2 or 3. Within level 1, list every item contradicted within the audited statements and notes first, in size order, then every item contradicted in the management discussion or an adjusted measure, in size order. A notes item is never placed below a management-discussion or adjusted-measure item, however small.
2. Two or more signals pointing at the same account in the same period, from either track.
3. A single measure, a single change in wording, or a single policy that leans aggressive, out of line with the company's history or with general practice.

Within a level: first the items whose size is an effect on reported income, largest first; then the items whose size is a balance or an exposure, largest first; then the unquantified items. A balance never outranks an income effect at the same level, however large. Never move an item across levels on size alone. Where an item at a lower level is much larger than one above it, say so in its Shown line; the reader does the trading off.

**Structure:**
- **Header:** two lines: the company and periods, and the documents used.
- **Items to investigate, up to five.** Fewer is fine; if nothing qualifies, say so in one line. Three lines each:
  - **Shown:** what the filings show, in one or two sentences, with page references; the size, labelled, and where an item carries more than one size, the one it ranks on ("ranks on 23%"); and the evidence level.
  - **Reading:** the reading from the earlier reply (ordinary, the business weakening, the reporting stretched, or can't tell from these documents) and what would change it.
  - **Next:** what to read or ask to settle it.
- **Also noted:** one or two lines listing the candidates that did not make the list and were not explained.
- **Checked and explained:** one or two lines listing items that looked unusual but have an ordinary explanation that held when tested.
- **Not examined:** one line on what the documents did not allow.
- **Closing line:** "Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's."

**Rules:**

- A reading is a judgment, not a finding. Word it as "most likely" or "possibly", never as a fact.
- State nothing more strongly than the evidence behind it.
- An item can matter without any misreporting: a weakening business is a finding too.
- Don't use words like fraud, manipulation or red flag in your own sentences.
- Keep it to about one page. Merge items only when they concern the same account or the same estimate; anything else that misses the list goes under "Also noted".

**Inventory: days inventory up from 81.9 to 85.6, with inventory rising 6.7 pp faster than cost of sales (FY Feb-26)**
- **The company says:** "inventory turnover ratio was 4.4 times… compared to 4.7… primarily driven by higher average inventory levels" (Annual report FY Feb-26, p. 32). That restates the movement; it gives no cause. The cash-flow discussion also only names "increased inventories" (p. 37). Last year the explanation went the other way: turnover rose on "lower average inventory levels within our Primary segment" (Annual report FY Feb-25, p. 32).
- **Test:** The note on the GMS acquisition gives $568m of inventory acquired. That is 24% of the $2,366m rise and 2.2% of the $25,817m year-end balance. The cash-flow line, which leaves acquisitions out, shows $1,498m of increase from existing businesses: 63% of the rise, or 6.4% growth on the opening balance. The Primary segment's cost of sales per week rose only 0.4% (52 against 53 weeks). Excluding the GMS inventory, days inventory is still 83.7 against 81.9. The company names no cause, so there is no explanation to test (does not hold). Q4 shows the same pattern: inventory rose 10.1% while cost of sales per day rose 3.9%.

The report also mentions tariff costs and "some price increases" (p. 32). A deliberate pre-tariff build would point to the ordinary reading, but the documents don't say whether there was one.
- **Read next:** Inventory by segment and the markdown and shrink reserve movements. Neither is in these documents: the notes only say cost-method adjustments were "not material" (p. 48).

**Operating margin: down from 13.50% to 12.69% (FY Feb-26); Q4 down from 11.3% to 10.1%**

The explanation partly holds.
- **Reading:** Possibly the business weakening in the Primary segment, with SG&A outgrowing sales for a second year. If the undisclosed legal benefit turned out to be most of the 0.84 pp rise in Primary SG&A, the reading would be ordinary.
