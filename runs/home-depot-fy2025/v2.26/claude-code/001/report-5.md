**The Home Depot: fiscal 2025 (52 weeks to 1 February 2026), compared with fiscal 2024 (53 weeks to 2 February 2025)**
Sources: the key sections of both 10-Ks, both Q4 results releases and statements.xlsx. Below, "text-1" and "text-2" are my two earlier replies; "numbers-1" and "numbers-2" are the pasted replies. Sizes are shares of fiscal 2025 pre-tax income of $18,602m.

No company sentence was contradicted by its own numbers or by another sentence, so nothing reaches level 1. Items 1–4 are level 2; item 5 is level 3.

## Items to investigate

**1. Operating margin and SG&A: Primary SG&A is outgrowing sales, partly explained by an unquantified "non-recurring" legal benefit**
- **Shown:** Operating margin fell from 13.50% to 12.69%. About 48% of the fall is mix shift toward SRS. Another 43% comes from Primary SG&A, which went from 18.2% to 19.0% of segment sales while segment gross margin rose (p.33–34, 55). Last year the higher fiscal 2024 SG&A was blamed partly on "lower legal-related benefits". This year the fiscal 2024 benefit is "non-recurring", with no amount in either year (p.34 both years). Size: $1,334m, 7.2% of pre-tax income, an income effect; ranks on 7.2%. From numbers-1 flag 2, numbers-2 write-up 2 and text-2 write-up 2.
- **Reading:** The tracks differ. Numbers-2: possibly the business weakening, with Primary SG&A outgrowing sales for a second year. Text-2: can't tell from these documents; calling the benefit "non-recurring" could make the cost rise look temporary. If the benefit turned out to be most of the 0.84-point rise, both readings would move to ordinary.
- **Next:** Ask for the amount of the fiscal 2024 legal benefit, any similar benefits in fiscal 2023 and 2025, and Primary payroll costs.

**2. Acquired intangibles: long lives, and amortization excluded from adjusted results**
- **Shown:** SRS customer relationships have a 20-year life and GMS's 19, at the top of the "up to approximately 20 years" policy range. Amortization is straight-line even though the valuations assume customer attrition (p.51, 74, 76). The charge does match the stated lives. Adjusted operating income and EPS exclude the $607m of amortization while keeping the related revenue (release p.8). The schedule shows $683m in fiscal 2026 and $6,476m thereafter (p.61). The adjusted EPS gap widened from $0.33 to $0.46.
- **Sizes:** income effect, illustrative, of 0.5–1.6% if lives were 15 or 10 years; ranks on 1.6%. Excluded from adjusted income: $607m, 3.3%.
- **Sources:** text-1 write-ups 1–2 and profile rows, text-2 statements row and note on adjusted measures, numbers-2 "Adjusted earnings".
- **Reading:** For the adjusted measure, most likely an ordinary, fully reconciled choice, slightly stretched because the revenue stays in. For the lives, possibly mildly stretched. Low customer attrition would make 19–20 years ordinary.
- **Next:** The attrition and discount rates used for SRS and GMS; the final GMS lives; whether pay targets use adjusted figures.

**3. Merchandise inventory: a build the company does not explain, while shrink review moved out of the critical audit matters**
- **Shown:**
  - Days inventory rose from 81.9 to 85.6, and Q4 from 86.2 to 91.3. Excluding GMS it is still 83.7 against 81.9. The company only restates the movement ("higher average inventory levels", p.32, 37).
  - The store-shrink critical audit matter was dropped (last year p.42); the new matter covers IT evidence for retail-method inventory (p.41–42).
  - The shrink sensitivity fell from $95m to $79m, an implied estimate down about 17%, with the method wording unchanged (p.39 both years).
  - The cost method now reads "moving average cost and first-in, first-out", with no change disclosed (p.38, 49).
- **Sizes:** balance of $1,102m in excess inventory (numbers-1 expressed it as 5.9% of pre-tax income); shrink exposure $79m, 0.4%. No income effect. The balance is far larger than item 2's income effect but cannot outrank it.
- **Sources:** numbers-1 flag 3, numbers-2 write-up 1, text-2 write-up 1, its shrink table row and its "Other changes" line 1.
- **Reading:** The tracks differ. Numbers-2: possibly the business weakening, with stock built ahead of flat demand (comparable transactions −1.0%). Text-2: the change of audit matter is most likely ordinary, and the shrink estimate can't be judged. A deliberate build ahead of tariffs would point to ordinary.
- **Next:** Inventory by segment; markdown and shrink reserve movements; how estimated shrink compared with actual counts.

**4. Receivables: days sales outstanding rising, an allowance with no figure, and a risk factor now saying Pros "increasingly" use trade credit**
- **Shown:** Days sales outstanding rose from 7.9 to 11.4 to 12.4; Q4 went from 12.1 to 13.3. Receivables are 3.4% of sales against an industry 2.8%. GMS brought $899m of receivables, which covers the rise. The company explains nothing for fiscal 2025 and calls the allowance only "not material" (p.48). The risk factor changed from "primarily use" to "increasingly use trade credit" (last year p.23, this year p.24).
- **Sizes:** balance of $438m above prior-year days (numbers-1 gave 2.4%); customer receivables $2.6bn, also a balance.
- **Sources:** numbers-1 flag 5, numbers-2 write-up 3, text-1 profile row 2, text-2 "Other changes" line 3.
- **Reading:** Numbers-2: most likely ordinary, a mix shift toward distribution businesses that extend credit. Text-1: can't tell, not enough disclosed. Rising days in the Primary business would change the reading.
- **Next:** Receivables by segment and an allowance roll-forward with write-offs.

**5. SRS goodwill: test passed with no stated headroom against thin segment earnings**
- **Shown:** The SRS reporting units' fair value only "exceeded" carrying amount, where Primary's "substantially exceeded". GMS goodwill was not tested, on the ground that the deal was too recent (p.60). Other earned $316m of operating income (2.5% margin) after $398m of amortization (p.55). Size: balance, $13.8bn of goodwill in Other. Level 3. From text-1 profile row and "Also noted" line 3.
- **Reading:** Can't tell from these documents. Fair value close to carrying amount is normal for recent purchases.
- **Next:** Headroom, discount rate and growth assumptions for each SRS reporting unit, and SRS/GMS profit before amortization.

## Checked and explained
- Free cash flow ran $1,510m (8.1%) below net income. Vendor timing, the deferred tax payment and inventory explain 102% of the working-capital swing, and two-year free cash flow equals two-year net income.
- The fall in payables fits vendor timing plus $184m less under the supplier finance program.
- These claims held when tested:
  - GMS was about 1% of sales.
  - Acquisitions added "$6.3 billion" of sales.
  - The rise in depreciation and amortization came "primarily" from intangibles.
  - Return on invested capital and the lease and deferred-revenue tie-outs reconcile.
  - Property lives fit the depreciation rate (7.44% to 7.38%).
  - The amortization charge matches the stated intangible lives.
