## Forensic review: Hertz Global Holdings, Inc.

**Periods:** FY2009 to FY2013; quarters Q1 2011 to Q4 2013.
**Documents:**
- statements workbook;
- FY2012 and FY2013 10-K sections: business, risk factors, legal, auditor's report, critical estimates, MD&A, Notes 2 and 8, restructuring, related parties, Schedule II;
- Q4 FY2012 and Q4 FY2013 releases.

2012 and 2011 figures were revised in the FY2013 10-K.

### Top five items to investigate

**1. Fleet depreciation cut while cars sold at a loss** (a sentence contradicted by the company's numbers)
- *Filings:* U.S. rate cuts lowered depreciation by $139.4m in 2012 (32% of revised pre-tax income) and $44.2m in 2013, described as "indicative of the residual values experienced". The same note reports U.S. disposals swinging from a $100.6m gain to a $48.2m loss on "declining residual values" (Note 8, p. 126; MD&A, p. 54). The MD&A says per-vehicle depreciation "decreased" (p. 42), but it rose 2.9% including losses.
- *Innocent:* the cuts are prospective, for newer cars sold at retail.
- *Concerning:* book values exceeded market, and the shortfall was deferred.
- *Next:* 2013 10-Q Note 8 by quarter; units sold and proceeds per car.

**2. Holding periods** (several signals on fleet depreciation, 2013)
- *Filings:* car lives changed from "Useful lives … 4 to 28 months" to "Holding periods … 4 to 36 months" (Note 2, pp. 81/91). The average stayed at 18 months (Business, p. 11), while the MD&A cites "longer holding periods" (p. 53). The depreciation rate fell from 15.2% to 13.6%. New wording says rates are "adjusted prospectively" (p. 45). PSUs vest on Corporate EBITDA, which is net of fleet depreciation (p. 47).
- *Innocent:* fleet mix.
- *Concerning:* periods were extended inside the estimate.
- *Next:* assumptions by model year; fleet age profile; PSU targets.

**3. Prior-period figures that disagree** (the company's own numbers)
- *Filings:* $46.3m of errors (Note 2, p. 83), against $47.9m in the release. Vendor incentives were booked against marketing "instead of reducing the cost of revenue earning equipment". 2012 restructuring payments were reprinted as $36.8m vs $26.8m, unexplained (pp. 126/141).
- *Innocent:* small, disclosed corrections under a clean audit opinion.
- *Concerning:* the errors cluster in fleet cost and reserves.
- *Next:* Item 9A; the "other immaterial errors".

**4. Recurring "one-time" items** (a label contradicted by recurrence)
- *Filings:* adjusted pre-tax income runs 1.7-2.1x GAAP. Restructuring has recurred since 2007 ($645.4m; Note 14, p. 140). The definition dropped "non-cash" for debt charges (MD&A, p. 50).
- *Innocent:* distinct events.
- *Concerning:* adjusted profit overstates the recurring level.
- *Next:* quarterly reconciliations; the credit-agreement definition of Corporate EBITDA.

**5. Receivables and allowance** (several signals, receivables)
- *Filings:* customer receivables outgrew revenue in 2012, including Q3 before the acquisition. In 2013, receivables fell 19.8% on revenue up 19.4%, while other assets rose from $470.1m to $717.2m. The allowance rose to 2.0%, and the 2012 allowance was revised from $25.1m to $29.3m (Schedule II, p. 158).
- *Innocent:* Donlen and Dollar Thrifty timing; the Brazil correction.
- *Concerning:* revenue booked ahead of collection, then balances moved out of receivables.
- *Next:* receivables and other-assets composition; the Simply Wheelz receivable.

*Context:* a shareholder class action covers February to November 2013 statements (Legal, p. 138).

### Checked and explained
- The year-end 2012 DSO and days-payable spikes follow Dollar Thrifty (21% of assets, 2% of revenue).
- Advertising, Note 8 and Schedule II reprints tie to the corrections.
- Segment re-presentation.
- Auditor's-report and related-party changes follow the acquisition and the sponsors' exit.
- The M-score is below -1.78 in all years, but it is structurally blind for a fleet business.

### Not examined
Goodwill inputs by reporting unit; the public-liability rollforward; receivables composition; Item 9A; 10-Qs; proxy; covenants; tax notes.

*Items to investigate, not conclusions. The verdicts are the analyst's.*
