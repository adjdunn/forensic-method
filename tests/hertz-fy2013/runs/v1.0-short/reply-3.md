## Disclosure changes: FY2012 10-K vs FY2013 10-K

I ignored changes from the Dollar Thrifty acquisition, the 2013 segment change, the sponsors' exit and date updates. The following are unchanged in substance:
- revenue recognition;
- the receivables policy;
- the auditor's report (it only drops the acquisition exclusion and does not mention the prior-period revision).

**1. Changes that matter**

| Section | Before (FY2012) | After (FY2013) | Pages | Why it might matter | Innocent reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lives (Note 2) | "Useful lives are as follows: … Cars \| 4 to 28 months" | "Holding periods are as follows: … Cars \| 4 to 36 months" | 81 / 91 | A longer maximum period means lower depreciation per month. | Reflects the non-program and Dollar Thrifty fleet mix. |
| Rate-cut rationale (Note 8) | "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2012 and 2011." | "The favorable adjustments reflect changes from the impact of car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion and the optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty." | 113 / 126 | "Strong" is dropped. The reason moves from market prices to internal choices. | Retail channels raise proceeds per car. |
| Residual values (MD&A to Note 8) | "We believe the residual values have remained fairly stronger primarily due to continued short supply of recent model year used vehicles…" | "The loss on vehicle sales in our car rental operations was primarily due to a combination of declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles…" | 42 / 126 | Residuals reversed, yet U.S. rates were still cut. | Losses on older cars can coexist with sound rates on new ones. |
| Critical estimates | "…we use historical experience and monitor market conditions to set depreciation rates." | "…we use historical experience, as well as industry residual value guidebooks, and the monitoring of market conditions…" and "Depreciation rates are adjusted prospectively through the remaining expected life." | 44 / 45 | A residual shortfall is spread forward rather than taken at once. | Prospective adjustment is standard; guidebooks add an outside check. |
| Critical estimates (pay) | (absent) | "…the PSUs had an additional vesting condition which called for the number of units that will be awarded based on achievement of a certain level of Corporate EBITDA…" | – / 47 | Corporate EBITDA deducts fleet depreciation (ER-Q4-FY2013 Table 7), so the estimate touches vesting. | A common metric, newly described. |
| Errors (Note 2) | "Certain prior period amounts have been reclassified to conform with current year presentation." | Adds: "…we identified certain out of period errors totaling $46.3 million…" | 80 / 83 | The errors sit in fleet cost and receivable reserves. The release says $47.9m. | Judged immaterial and disclosed. |
| Non-GAAP definition | "…plus non-cash purchase accounting charges, non-cash debt charges…" | "…plus certain non-cash purchase accounting charges, debt-related charges…" | 50 / 50 | "Non-cash" is dropped; cash debt premiums are added back in 2013. | Refinancing-year wording. |
| Legal and risk factors | (absent) | "On November 20, 2013, a purported shareholder class action…" and "We have recognized losses as a result of our relationship with … FSNA … and Simply Wheelz…" | 138; risk factors 24 | A disclosure claim covering 2013, and a $44.0m loss excluded from adjusted results. | Class actions often follow price falls. |

What stayed still while the numbers moved: the 18-month U.S. average is unchanged. Meanwhile, the car range widened by eight months, disposals swung from gain to loss, and U.S. rates were cut again.
