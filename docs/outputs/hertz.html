Items to investigate

1. Car depreciation: rates cut while the same note shows losses on car sales

  • Shown:
    • Note 8 says the U.S. rate cuts ($44.2m) were "indicative of the residual values experienced". The same note shows a $48.2m U.S. loss on sales "due to … declining residual values" (p.126), and MD&A cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54).
    • The word "strong" was dropped from that Note 8 sentence (FY2012 p.113).
    • The car holding-period range was extended from 4–28 to 4–36 months and relabelled from "useful lives" to "holding periods" (FY2012 p.81 → p.91). It is not presented as a change in estimate.
    • The U.S. average holding period stays at "eighteen months" (p.11, both years). Yet MD&A newly cites "longer holding periods" (p.53) and keeps "longer holding period does not necessarily equate to higher costs" (p.42).
    • The company's stated reason for the cuts: "car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion and the optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" (p.126).
    • The fleet depreciation rate fell from 15.17% to 14.27% (revised basis).
    • Sizes (both are income effects):
      • Depreciation not charged at the 2012 rate: $152.1m = 22.9% (first reported $174.5m = 26.3%).
      • Company-stated rate cut: $44.2m = 6.7% ($39.6m = 6.0% net).
      • Loss on sales: $48.2m = 7.3%.
    • Ranks on 22.9%. Evidence level 1, in the audited notes.
    • Absorbs: text-1 profile row 3 and write-up 2; text-2 write-ups 1–2, table rows 1–5 and disclosed-changes rows 1–2; num-1 flag 5; num-2 item 2 and its estimate-change section.
  • Reading: The replies disagree, and that is worth the analyst's time. Text-1 and text-2 write-up 2 read it as most likely the business weakening. Text-2 write-up 1 and num-2 read it as possibly the reporting stretched. Gains on later sales of 2013 cars, or better prices through the retail channel, would favour the ordinary reading.
  • Next: quarter-by-quarter rate changes and sale results in the 2013 quarterly reports; how the 18-month average is computed; sale results by channel.

2. Adjusted pre-tax income: "one-time" charges that recur every year

  • Shown:
    • Note 11 (p.131) defines the measure as excluding "certain one-time charges".
    • Note 14 shows restructuring in every year presented: $645.4m since 2007, with "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed" (p.140).
    • Restructuring plus related charges: $98.8m in 2013, $49.1m in 2012, $66.2m in 2011.
    • New exclusions in 2013: integration $40.0m, relocation $7.8m, impairment and other $44.0m, together 13.8%. "Non-cash debt charges" was renamed "debt-related charges".
    • Size: excluded from adjusted income, $98.8m = 14.9% (the amount the contradicted sentence covers). The total gap between adjusted and reported pre-tax income is $490.1m = 73.9%. Evidence level 1, in the audited notes; ranks after item 1 because an exclusion is not an income effect.
    • Absorbs: text-1 profile row 9 and write-up 1; text-2 other change 1; num-2 adjusted-earnings section.
  • Reading: Most likely the reporting stretched (text-1). Charges that fall away once the Dollar Thrifty integration and the headquarters move end would point to ordinary.
  • Next: 2014 restructuring charges; the credit-agreement definition of "non-recurring" behind Corporate EBITDA.

3. Intangible asset lives: the policy range does not cover lives used elsewhere in the notes

  • Shown: The Note 2 lives table now says "Other intangible assets | 3 to 10 years" (p.91; it was "3 to 20 years", FY2012 p.81). Note 2 also says "two to fifteen years" (p.93), and Donlen's intangibles carry 16- and 20-year lives (p.99). Not quantified. Evidence level 1, in the audited notes (text-2 table row 4).
  • Reading: Can't tell from these documents; the earlier reply gave no reading.
  • Next: the lives actually applied to each intangible class, and amortization by class.

4. Goodwill: the level at which impairment is tested is described two ways

  • Shown:
    • Note 2 says testing is "one level below our operating segments" (p.93; identical in FY2012 p.83).
    • MD&A lists five reporting units (p.46; the same five in FY2012 p.46), and these match the operating segments in Note 11 (p.130–131).

    • Absorbs: text-1 write-up 3 and profile row 4.

  • Next: fair-value headroom by reporting unit and for the trade name.

  • Equipment and property lives fit the depreciation charged, roughly.

Prompt 2, follow the flags

1. Days payable fell from 52.9 to 42.7 in FY2013. On the revised basis, payables fell 3.5% while cost of sales rose 19.4%, both for the whole company. As first reported, FY2012 was 52.7 days and the size was $226.3m; revised, the size is $232.8m, 35.1% of pre-tax income.

  • The company says: nothing about the payables balance. The nearest sentence is about the rise in operating cash flow: "due to the timing of our payments" (FY2013 annual report, p.64). It doesn't explain the payables balance. Last year: the same words (FY2012 annual report, p.60).
  • Test: That sentence claims a cause of the $879.9m whole-company rise in operating cash flow, 2013 against 2012.
    • Timing of payments would show in the liability lines of operating cash flow. Those lines added $40.3m more than in 2012, which is 4.6% of the $879.9m rise. The cash-flow payables line itself added less ($23.2m against $34.0m), while the balance-sheet payables fell $35.3m.
    • My own acquisition test needs Dollar Thrifty's costs, which the documents don't give. The nearest measure is payables against sales, using pro forma 2012 revenue of $10,197.4m (p.99). On that measure the part-year effect covers 59% of the fall (40.7 to 32.8 days).
    • So the timing sentence does not hold for payables, and the acquisition partly holds, on a sales-based stand-in only.
  • Reading: Possibly ordinary. Year-end fleet buying fell: Q4 fleet spending was $978.6m against $1,928.3m a year earlier (FY2013 results release, Table 7). That would lower payables if fleet purchases sit there. Against that, fleet purchases held in payables and accrued liabilities together rose from $247.0m to $289.1m (p.81), which leaves this one undecided.
  • Read next: a breakdown of accounts payable between fleet and non-fleet. It is not in these documents.

2. Fleet depreciation rate fell from 15.17% to 14.27% in 2013 (revised). As first reported it was 15.28% to 14.25%. The revised size is $152.1m, 22.9% of pre-tax income (first reported, $174.5m). The fall is all in cars: the car rate went from 16.74% to 15.43%, while the equipment rate went from 9.41% to 9.63%.

  • The company says: U.S. rate changes gave "net decreases of $44.2 million" in depreciation, and "the cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2013 annual report, p.42; also p.126).
    • Last year the wording was different: "indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012 annual report, p.113). The word "strong" has gone.
  • Test: Fleet-wide rate changes, net $39.6m (p.40), cover 26% of the revised size, or 22% of the car-only size of $177.4m.
    • Other sentences in the same report point the other way. U.S. disposals swung from gains of $100.6m to losses of $48.2m, a $148.8m worsening, "due to … declining residual values" (p.126). U.S. depreciation rose partly from "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54).
    • The holding-period reason can't be seen in the numbers: the U.S. average holding period was "eighteen months" in both years (FY2013 annual report p.11, FY2012 annual report p.11).
    • U.S. depreciation per car per month, leaving out disposal results, fell 14.1% ($241.6 to $207.7). That uses the segment line, which also holds lease charges, so it is an approximation.
    • So the explanation partly holds.
  • Reading: Possibly the reporting stretched. Rates were cut while realised sale prices fell short of book values. If the cuts relate to Dollar Thrifty cars sold through retail at better prices, that would point to an ordinary reading, but the reports give no figures by channel.
  • Read next: the quarterly disposal results and the rate changes in the 2013 quarterly reports. They are not attached.

3. Payables are 9.0% of sales against the industry's 4.5%. The size is $483.2m, 72.9% of pre-tax income.

  • The company says: nothing about the level of payables. Last year: nothing.
  • Test:
    • Property and equipment purchases in payables are $55.6m.
    • Fleet purchases in "accounts payable and accrued liabilities" are $289.1m (p.81). They can't be split out, so this is an upper limit.
    • Together they cover at most 71% of the gap and leave payables at 5.8% of sales. This partly holds, and only on my own test.
  • Reading: Possibly ordinary, since a rental company buys its fleet on credit. If payables less fleet purchases stayed well above 4.5%, that would point to longer payment terms.
  • Read next: the payables composition. It is not disclosed.

2. Write-ups

Adjusted pre-tax income: recurring restructuring presented as one-time

  • The report says: adjusted pre-tax income excludes "certain one-time charges and non-operational items" (Note 11, p.131). It is the audited segment measure.
  • Test: The claim is that the excluded restructuring charges are one-time. Note 14 shows restructuring charges in every year presented: $56.4m in 2011, $38.0m in 2012 and $77.0m in 2013. It also says "we incurred $645.4 million" between 2007 and 2013 (about $92m a year) and "Additional efficiency and cost saving initiatives are being developed" (p.140). The claim does not hold. Size: restructuring plus restructuring-related charges excluded in 2013 come to $98.8m = 14.9% of PTI.
  • Reading: Most likely the reporting is stretched. A cost that has appeared every year for seven years is part of running the business, so adjusted income overstates recurring earnings. A different reading would be supported if 2013's charges turn out to be tied to discrete events that end: the Dollar Thrifty integration and the $21.9m headquarters move.
  • Read next: the 2014 restructuring charges, and the credit-agreement definition of "non-recurring" used for Corporate EBITDA (release, Table 7).

Vehicle depreciation: rates cut while vehicles sold at a loss

  • The report says: "The cumulative effect of the reduction in rates was also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S." (Note 8, p.126).
  • Test: The claim is that U.S. residual values realized in 2013 supported the $44.2m cut in depreciation. The matching figure is the 2013 result on U.S. vehicle sales. The same note gives "net losses of $48.2 million", attributed to "declining residual values from falling demand for used vehicles". MD&A also cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54). The claim does not hold for 2013. Size: the rate cut is $44.2m = 6.7% of PTI. The loss is $48.2m = 7.3% of PTI, but only 1.1% of U.S. disposal proceeds.
  • Reading: Most likely the business weakening: used-car prices fell faster than the rate estimates, which the note says carry over "from previous quarters". The policy itself still leans aggressive, because the report cut rates in a year its own figures show losses on sale. A different reading would be supported if rates were raised within 2013 and the net cut came from changes made in 2012.
  • Read next: the quarter-by-quarter rate changes and disposal results in the 2013 and 2014 quarterly reports.

Goodwill: reporting units described two different ways

  • The report says: goodwill is tested "at the reporting unit level which is one level below our operating segments" (Note 2, p.93).
  • Test: The claim is about the level of the reporting units. MD&A lists five reporting units: U.S. Car Rental, Europe, Other International, Donlen and Worldwide Equipment Rental (p.46). Note 11 names exactly these as the operating segments (p.130–131). So the units are the operating segments, not one level below them. The claim does not hold. Size: not quantified; goodwill is a $1,347.5m balance.
  • Reading: Most likely ordinary wording left over from the Q3 2013 segment change. A different reading would be supported if units were combined, which would make an impairment less likely to show; no headroom is disclosed to check this.
  • Read next: the reporting units and fair-value headroom by unit before the segment change.

    • Impairment is $40.0m in the cash flow statement and Note 15, but $44.0m as "impairment charges and other" in the adjusted reconciliation (p.80, p.146, p.134).
    • The doubtful-accounts provision is $45.9m in the cash flow statement but $41.9m in Schedule II (p.80, p.158).
  • Error corrections (p.83–85): the sums hold ($6.8m + $18.7m + $9.2m = $34.7m for prior years; $11.6m for 2013). The 2011 correction is 5.8% of that year's as-reported PTI but is called not material. The main error was vendor incentives booked against marketing expense instead of vehicle cost.

1. Write-ups

All page numbers are in the 10-Ks unless a results release is named. Pre-tax income (PTI) for 2013 is $663.1m.

Revenue earning equipment (Notes 2 and 8): longer car holding-period range and new reasons for cutting depreciation rates

  • Before: "Useful lives are as follows: Cars | 4 to 28 months" (FY2012, p.81). The 2012 rate cuts were explained only as "indicative of the strong residual values experienced" (FY2012, p.113).
  • After: "Holding periods are as follows: Cars | 4 to 36 months" (FY2013, p.91). The rate cuts are now explained by "car sales channel diversification, acceleration of our retail sales expansion and the optimization of fleet holding periods related to the integration of Dollar Thrifty" (p.126). Two sentences were also added to the critical estimates: "industry residual value guidebooks" and "Depreciation rates are adjusted prospectively through the remaining expected life" (p.45).
  • Why it matters: The longest car holding period went up 29% (28 to 36 months) in the same year depreciation rates were cut by $44.2m. U.S. depreciation per car-month before disposal results fell 14.1%, from $241.6 to $207.7. The report does not present the longer range as a change in estimate, and gives no effect for it. Size: stated effect of the rate cut $44.2m = 6.7% of PTI ($39.6m = 6.0% net company-wide); the longer range itself is not quantified.
  • Reading: Possibly the reporting stretched: holding assumptions were lengthened while the report's own U.S. sales results turned to losses. A different reading would be supported if the 28-month top of the range only reflected Dollar Thrifty's longer-held fleet, or if 2014 sales of these cars produce gains.

Note 8: "strong" dropped from residual values while U.S. sale results turned to losses

  • Before: "indicative of the strong residual values experienced in the U.S." (FY2012, p.113). MD&A: "We believe the residual values have remained fairly stronger" (FY2012, p.42).
  • After: "also indicative of the residual values experienced in the U.S. for the years ended December 31, 2013…" (p.126). The MD&A sentence is dropped, and MD&A now cites "a deterioration in the used vehicle residual values" (p.54).
  • Why it matters: U.S. vehicle sales went from a $100.6m gain to a $48.2m loss, a $148.8m swing equal to 22.4% of PTI. The report removed "strong" but kept the claim that residual values supported the rate cuts and extended it to 2013. Last reply's test showed the claim does not hold for 2013. Size: the loss is $48.2m = 7.3% of PTI.
  • Reading: Most likely the business weakening, with the wording trimmed instead of withdrawn. A different reading would be supported if the 2013 losses came only from the timing of sales, which Note 8 also cites, and residual values recovered.

Legal (Note 13): first dollar figure on the Sobel case, and a new "not material" statement

  • Before: The settlement was rejected and "the parties will again be engaging in mediation". No amount and no conclusion on materiality (FY2012, p.122).
  • After: "A judgment - which could potentially exceed $40.0 million" and "We continue to believe the outcome of this case will not be material" (p.137). This follows a partial summary judgment for the plaintiffs on restitution, plus class certification. A securities class action was also added (p.138).
  • Why it matters: The first amount given equals 6.0% of PTI. The FY2012 report contains no earlier view on this case for the company to "continue" holding. Size: exposure ≥$40.0m = 6.0% of PTI.
  • Reading: Can't tell. No accrual is disclosed. A different reading would be supported if an accrual existed or the March 2014 mediation settled well below $40m.
